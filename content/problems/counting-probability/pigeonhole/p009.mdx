<Problem>
Call a set of positive integers **triple-free** if it contains no two elements $a$ and $b$ with $b = 3a$. What is the size of the largest triple-free subset of $\{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 27\}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Step 1: Build chains under tripling.**

Partition $\{1, \ldots, 27\}$ into chains where each chain connects an integer to its multiples of $3$. Every integer belongs to exactly one chain (start from the unique element in the chain not divisible by $3$, or that equals $27$).

The chains are:

| Chain | Elements | Length |
|-------|----------|--------|
| $1 \to 3 \to 9 \to 27$ | $\{1, 3, 9, 27\}$ | 4 |
| $2 \to 6 \to 18$ | $\{2, 6, 18\}$ | 3 |
| $4 \to 12$ | $\{4, 12\}$ | 2 |
| $5 \to 15$ | $\{5, 15\}$ | 2 |
| $7 \to 21$ | $\{7, 21\}$ | 2 |
| $8 \to 24$ | $\{8, 24\}$ | 2 |
| Singletons | $\{10,11,13,14,16,17,19,20,22,23,25,26\}$ | 1 each |

**Verify the count:** $4 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 12 = 27$. ✓

The $12$ singletons are integers in $(9, 27]$ that are not divisible by $3$ and whose one-third is not an integer in $\{1,\ldots,27\}$ — specifically, the integers from $10$ to $26$ that are not multiples of $3$: that is $\{10,11,13,14,16,17,19,20,22,23,25,26\}$.

**Step 2: Maximize the triple-free subset within each chain.**

A triple-free subset cannot contain two consecutive elements $a, 3a$ of a chain. This is equivalent to choosing an **independent set** in a path graph.

- **Length-4 chain** $\{1,3,9,27\}$: The independent sets of a 4-vertex path have maximum size $2$. Choose $\{1, 9\}$ or $\{3, 27\}$ (skip every other). Contribution: $2$.
- **Length-3 chain** $\{2,6,18\}$: Maximum independent set of a 3-vertex path has size $2$. Choose $\{2, 18\}$. Contribution: $2$.
- **Length-2 chains** ($\{4,12\}$, $\{5,15\}$, $\{7,21\}$, $\{8,24\}$): Each allows at most $1$. Contribution: $1 \times 4 = 4$.
- **Singletons** (12 elements): Include all. Contribution: $12$.

**Step 3: Total.**

$$2 + 2 + 4 + 12 = 20.$$

**Explicit example:** $S = \{1, 9\} \cup \{2, 18\} \cup \{4\} \cup \{5\} \cup \{7\} \cup \{8\} \cup \{10,11,13,14,16,17,19,20,22,23,25,26\}$.

Check: $|S| = 2+2+1+1+1+1+12 = 20$. No element of $S$ is three times another element of $S$. ✓

**Why $21$ is impossible:** Any $21$-element subset must take at least $21 - 18 = 3$ elements from the non-singleton chains (total $4+3+2+2+2+2=15$ chain elements; singletons only provide $12$). The maximum from chains is $2+2+1+1+1+1=8$; $12+8=20$, so $21$ is impossible.

The largest triple-free subset of $\{1,\ldots,27\}$ has $\boxed{20}$ elements.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
