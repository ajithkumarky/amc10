<Problem>
A school club has members in grades $9$, $10$, and $11$. Members are randomly assigned to one of three advisory groups labeled $X$, $Y$, and $Z$. What is the minimum number of club members needed to **guarantee** that at least $8$ members are assigned to the same advisory group?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We use the generalized Pigeonhole Principle: if $n$ objects are placed into $3$ containers, at least one container holds at least $\lceil n/3 \rceil$ objects.

We need $\lceil n/3 \rceil \geq 8$, which means $n/3 > 7$, so $n > 21$, giving $n \geq 22$.

**Verify the boundary:**
- With $21$ members, the groups could be split $7$-$7$-$7$ — no group has $8$. So $21$ is not enough.
- With $22$ members, even if the adversary tries to spread them as evenly as possible, the best they can do is $8$-$7$-$7$ — one group must have at least $8$.

The minimum number of members is $22$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
