<Problem>
Ten points are placed inside or on an equilateral triangle with side length $3$. What is the smallest value $d$ such that some two of the ten points are guaranteed to be within distance $d$ of each other (i.e., the distance between them is at most $d$)?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Divide and conquer with pigeonhole.**

Subdivide the equilateral triangle of side $3$ into $9$ smaller equilateral triangles, each with side length $1$. (This is a standard triangular grid: draw lines parallel to each side at distances $1, 2$ from each vertex, creating $3^2 = 9$ unit triangles.)

These $9$ small triangles are the containers.

**Applying pigeonhole:** We have $10$ points placed into $9$ containers. At least one small triangle contains $2$ (or more) of the ten points.

**Maximum distance inside a unit equilateral triangle:** Any two points inside or on an equilateral triangle of side $1$ are at most distance $1$ apart (the longest distance between two points of an equilateral triangle equals its side length).

Therefore, some two of the ten points lie in the same unit triangle and are at distance at most $1$.

**Tightness:** With exactly $9$ points (one per small triangle), we can place them at vertices of the triangular grid, where adjacent points are exactly distance $1$ apart and non-adjacent points are farther. So $9$ points do not guarantee distance $\leq d$ for any $d < 1$, confirming $d = 1$ is tight.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
