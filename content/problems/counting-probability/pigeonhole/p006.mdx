<Problem>
Integers are selected one at a time from $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$ without replacement. What is the minimum number of integers that must be selected to **guarantee** that two of the selected integers are **consecutive** (differ by exactly $1$)?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Partition $\{1, 2, \ldots, 8\}$ into four pairs of consecutive integers:
$$\{1,2\},\quad\{3,4\},\quad\{5,6\},\quad\{7,8\}.$$
These four pairs are the containers. Two integers from the same pair differ by exactly $1$.

**Adversarial scenario:** The adversary picks integers to avoid giving you a complete pair. At worst, they give you one integer from each pair: for instance $\{1, 3, 5, 7\}$. After $4$ selections, no two are consecutive.

The $5$th selection must come from one of the four pairs that already has a representative. That completes one pair, giving two consecutive integers.

**Check $4$ is not enough:** Choosing $\{1, 3, 5, 7\}$ gives $4$ integers with no two consecutive.

The minimum number of integers is $\boxed{5}$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
