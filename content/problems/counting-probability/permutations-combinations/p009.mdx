<Problem>
Four boys and four girls are seated around a circular table (rotations of a seating arrangement are considered identical). In how many ways can they be arranged so that no two boys sit in adjacent seats?
</Problem>

<Solution>
For no two boys to be adjacent among 8 people at a circular table, the boys and girls must **alternate** — the only way to seat 4 boys among 4 girls in a circle with no two boys adjacent is in the pattern B-G-B-G-B-G-B-G.

**Step 1 — Fix the girls' circular arrangement.**

To handle the rotational symmetry, fix one girl in a seat. This removes all 8 rotations. The remaining 3 girls fill the other 3 "girl" seats in:
$$3! = 6 \text{ ways.}$$

**Step 2 — Place the boys in the alternating seats.**

Once the girls are seated, the 4 gaps between consecutive girls are determined. The 4 boys can be arranged in these 4 seats in:
$$4! = 24 \text{ ways.}$$

**Step 3 — Multiply.**
$$3! \times 4! = 6 \times 24 = 144.$$

*Why must boys and girls alternate?* In a cycle of 8 seats, the only way to choose 4 seats so that no two are adjacent (cyclically) is to pick every other seat — the two alternating sets $\{1,3,5,7\}$ and $\{2,4,6,8\}$. Once the girls fix one of these patterns (via the fixed-girl convention), the boys automatically occupy the other alternating set.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
