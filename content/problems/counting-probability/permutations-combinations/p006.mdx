<Problem>
From a group of $8$ people, one person is chosen as president and $2$ different people (from the remaining $7$) are chosen as vice-presidents. The two vice-president positions are identical (neither is ranked above the other). In how many ways can this selection be made?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since the two vice-president positions are **identical** (unordered), we handle the selection in two independent steps.

**Step 1 — Choose the president:** Any of the 8 people may be chosen. That gives $8$ options.

**Step 2 — Choose 2 vice-presidents from the remaining 7:** Because the two VP roles are the same, this is a combination:
$$\binom{7}{2} = \frac{7 \cdot 6}{2} = 21.$$

**Total:**
$$8 \times 21 = 168.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Overcounting correction">
If we temporarily treat both VP positions as distinct, the count would be $8 \times 7 \times 6 = 336$ (ordered choices for president, VP1, VP2). But swapping the two VPs gives the same outcome, so divide by $2! = 2$: $336 / 2 = 168$.
</AlternateMethod>
