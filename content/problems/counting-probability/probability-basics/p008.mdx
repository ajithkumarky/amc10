<Problem>
Three fair six-sided dice are rolled simultaneously. What is the probability that at least two of the three dice show the same number?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Use the complement: it is easier to count the outcomes where **all three dice show different numbers**.

The total number of outcomes is $6^3 = 216$.

For all three dice to show different numbers: choose distinct values for dice 1, 2, 3 in order. There are $6$ choices for die 1, $5$ remaining choices for die 2, and $4$ remaining choices for die 3:

$$6 \times 5 \times 4 = 120 \text{ outcomes where all three differ.}$$

$$P(\text{all different}) = \frac{120}{216} = \frac{5}{9}.$$

Therefore,

$$P(\text{at least two the same}) = 1 - \frac{5}{9} = \frac{4}{9}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Direct count">
Count outcomes by cases:

- **Exactly two dice match (a pair):** Choose the matching value: $6$ ways. Choose which two dice form the pair: $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ ways. Choose a different value for the third die: $5$ ways. Total: $6 \times 3 \times 5 = 90$.

- **All three dice match (a triple):** Choose the common value: $6$ ways. Total: $6$.

Favorable outcomes: $90 + 6 = 96$.

$$P = \frac{96}{216} = \frac{4}{9}. \checkmark$$
</AlternateMethod>
