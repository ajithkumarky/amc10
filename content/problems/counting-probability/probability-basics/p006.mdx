<Problem>
Spinner $A$ has three equally-sized sections labeled $1$, $2$, $3$. Spinner $B$ has four equally-sized sections labeled $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$. Both spinners are spun once, independently. What is the probability that the two spinners land on the same number?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The sample space consists of all pairs $(a, b)$ where $a \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ and $b \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, giving $3 \times 4 = 12$ equally likely outcomes.

The favorable outcomes (same number on both) are: $(1,1)$, $(2,2)$, $(3,3)$ — exactly $3$ outcomes. (There is no $(4, 4)$ because Spinner A has no $4$.)

$$P(\text{same number}) = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}.$$

**Alternative:** For each value $v \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, $P(\text{Spinner A shows } v) = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $P(\text{Spinner B shows } v) = \dfrac{1}{4}$. Since the spinners are independent:

$$P(\text{both show } v) = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12}.$$

Summing over the three matching values: $3 \times \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. ✓

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
