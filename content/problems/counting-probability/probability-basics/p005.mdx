<Problem>
A family has $3$ children. Assuming each child is equally likely to be a boy or a girl (independent of the others), what is the probability that the family has **exactly $2$ girls**?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Each child is independently a boy (B) or girl (G) with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$ each. The sample space has $2^3 = 8$ equally likely outcomes:

$$\{BBB,\; BBG,\; BGB,\; BGG,\; GBB,\; GBG,\; GGB,\; GGG\}.$$

The outcomes with exactly $2$ girls are: $BGG$, $GBG$, $GGB$ — that is $3$ outcomes.

$$P(\text{exactly 2 girls}) = \frac{3}{8}.$$

**Using the binomial formula:** $\dbinom{3}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^1 = 3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{8} = \dfrac{3}{8}$. ✓

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
