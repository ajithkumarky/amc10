<Problem>
A point is chosen uniformly at random inside the unit square $[0,1]\times[0,1]$. What is the probability that the point is closer to at least one corner of the square than $\dfrac{1}{2}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A point is within distance $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a given corner if and only if it lies inside the quarter-disk of radius $\dfrac{1}{2}$ centered at that corner. There are four such quarter-disks, one at each corner.

**Do any two quarter-disks overlap?** Adjacent corners are distance $1$ apart; the sum of the two radii equals $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2} = 1$, so the quarter-disks are tangent (they touch only at the midpoint of the shared edge) and have no interior overlap. Opposite corners are distance $\sqrt{2} > 1$ apart, so those quarter-disks don't even touch.

Because there is no overlap, the areas add directly.

$$\text{Total favorable area} = 4 \times \frac{1}{4}\pi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = 4 \times \frac{\pi}{16} = \frac{\pi}{4}.$$

The unit square has area $1$, so

$$P = \frac{\pi/4}{1} = \frac{\pi}{4}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
