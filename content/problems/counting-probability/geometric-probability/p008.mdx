<Problem>
Two numbers $x$ and $y$ are chosen independently and uniformly at random from the interval $[0, 1]$. What is the probability that $|x - y| < \dfrac{1}{3}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place $x$ on the horizontal axis and $y$ on the vertical axis. The sample space is the unit square with area $1$.

The condition $|x - y| < \dfrac{1}{3}$ is satisfied in the diagonal band between the lines $y = x + \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $y = x - \dfrac{1}{3}$.

It is easier to compute the **complement**: $|x - y| \ge \dfrac{1}{3}$.

The unfavorable region consists of two right triangles:
- Upper-left: $y > x + \dfrac{1}{3}$, a triangle with legs $1 - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
- Lower-right: $y < x - \dfrac{1}{3}$, a triangle with legs $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

$$\text{Unfavorable area} = 2 \times \frac{1}{2} \times \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{9}.$$

$$P\!\left(|x-y| < \frac{1}{3}\right) = 1 - \frac{4}{9} = \frac{5}{9}.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
