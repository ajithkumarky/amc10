<Problem>
In a school club, $15$ students play chess and $12$ students play checkers. Exactly $5$ students play both games. How many students play chess or checkers (or both)?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We want $|\text{Chess} \cup \text{Checkers}|$. Applying the inclusion-exclusion formula for two sets:

$$|\text{Chess} \cup \text{Checkers}| = |\text{Chess}| + |\text{Checkers}| - |\text{Chess} \cap \text{Checkers}|.$$

The $5$ students who play both are counted once in the $15$ and once in the $12$, so we subtract them once:

$$15 + 12 - 5 = 22.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Partitioning into disjoint groups">
Split the players into three non-overlapping groups:

- Chess only: $15 - 5 = 10$
- Checkers only: $12 - 5 = 7$
- Both: $5$

By the Addition Principle: $10 + 7 + 5 = 22$.
</AlternateMethod>
