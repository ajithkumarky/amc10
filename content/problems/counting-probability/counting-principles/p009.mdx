<Problem>
A committee of $5$ people is to be chosen from a group of $6$ men and $5$ women. In how many ways can the committee be formed if it must contain at least $2$ men and at least $2$ women?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The committee has $5$ people and must include at least $2$ men and at least $2$ women. Since $2 + 2 = 4$ and the committee has $5$ members, the only valid gender splits are:

| Men | Women |
|-----|-------|
| 2   | 3     |
| 3   | 2     |

(A split of $4$ men and $1$ woman fails the "at least $2$ women" condition, and $1$ man and $4$ women fails the "at least $2$ men" condition.)

**Case 1: 2 men, 3 women.**
$$\binom{6}{2} \times \binom{5}{3} = 15 \times 10 = 150.$$

**Case 2: 3 men, 2 women.**
$$\binom{6}{3} \times \binom{5}{2} = 20 \times 10 = 200.$$

Since the two cases are mutually exclusive, the total is:
$$150 + 200 = 350.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
