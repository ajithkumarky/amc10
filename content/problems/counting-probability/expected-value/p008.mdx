<Problem>
Two fair six-sided dice are rolled independently. What is the expected value of the **product** of the two numbers rolled?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $X$ be the result of the first die and $Y$ the result of the second die.

Since the two dice are rolled **independently**, we can use the product rule for expectation:

$$E[XY] = E[X] \cdot E[Y].$$

For a single fair die:

$$E[X] = E[Y] = \frac{1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6}{6} = \frac{21}{6} = \frac{7}{2}.$$

Therefore:

$$E[XY] = \frac{7}{2} \cdot \frac{7}{2} = \frac{49}{4}.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Direct computation">
There are $36$ equally likely pairs $(i, j)$ with $i, j \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$. The expected product is

$$E[XY] = \frac{1}{36} \sum_{i=1}^{6} \sum_{j=1}^{6} ij = \frac{1}{36} \left(\sum_{i=1}^{6} i\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{6} j\right) = \frac{1}{36} \cdot 21 \cdot 21 = \frac{441}{36} = \frac{49}{4}.$$
</AlternateMethod>
