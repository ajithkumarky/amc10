<Problem>
Five students — Alex, Blake, Casey, Drew, and Emery — each write their name on a slip of paper. The five slips are shuffled and randomly handed back, one to each student. What is the expected number of students who receive their own slip?
</Problem>

<Solution>
For each student $k$, define the indicator random variable

$$I_k = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if student } k \text{ receives their own slip,} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

The total number of students who receive their own slip is $X = I_1 + I_2 + I_3 + I_4 + I_5$.

By symmetry, the probability that any particular student gets their own slip is $\dfrac{1}{5}$ (each student is equally likely to receive any of the $5$ slips). Thus

$$E[I_k] = P(I_k = 1) = \frac{1}{5}.$$

By **linearity of expectation** (which holds regardless of whether the $I_k$ are independent):

$$E[X] = E[I_1] + E[I_2] + E[I_3] + E[I_4] + E[I_5] = 5 \cdot \frac{1}{5} = 1.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
