<Problem>
The base-3 representation of a positive integer $N$ consists of exactly twelve 1s and no other digits: $N = \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{12 \text{ ones}}_3$. What is the sum of the digits of $N$ when written in base 9?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Key idea:** Since $9 = 3^2$, every pair of consecutive base-3 digits corresponds to exactly one base-9 digit.

The base-3 representation $\underbrace{11\cdots1}_{12}_3$ has 12 digits. Group them in pairs from right to left:
$$\underbrace{11}_{}\,\underbrace{11}_{}\,\underbrace{11}_{}\,\underbrace{11}_{}\,\underbrace{11}_{}\,\underbrace{11}_{}_3$$

Each pair $11_3$ equals $1 \cdot 3 + 1 = 4$ in base 10, which is the single base-9 digit $4$.

Therefore $N = \underbrace{44\cdots4}_{6 \text{ fours}}_9$.

The sum of digits of $N$ in base 9 is $6 \times 4 = \mathbf{24}$.

**Verification:** $N = \frac{3^{12} - 1}{3 - 1} = \frac{531441 - 1}{2} = 265720$.

In base 9: $265720 = 4 \cdot 9^5 + 4 \cdot 9^4 + 4 \cdot 9^3 + 4 \cdot 9^2 + 4 \cdot 9 + 4 = 4(9^5 + 9^4 + 9^3 + 9^2 + 9 + 1)$.
Check: $4 \cdot \frac{9^6-1}{8} = 4 \cdot \frac{531441-1}{8} = 4 \cdot 66430 = 265720$. ✓

Digit sum $= 6 \times 4 = 24$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
