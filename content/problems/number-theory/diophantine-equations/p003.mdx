<Problem>
How many ordered pairs of positive integers $(x, y)$ satisfy
$$xy + x - 6y = 10?$$
</Problem>

<Solution>
Apply Simon's Favorite Factoring Trick. Factor the left side by grouping:
$$x(y + 1) - 6y = 10.$$
Add $-6$ to both sides (equivalently, add $(-6)(1)$ to complete the factoring):
$$x(y + 1) - 6(y + 1) = 10 - 6 = 4.$$
$$(x - 6)(y + 1) = 4.$$

Since $y$ is a positive integer, $y + 1 \geq 2$. Since $x$ is a positive integer, $x - 6 \geq 1 - 6 = -5$.

List all integer factor pairs $(a, b)$ with $a \cdot b = 4$ and $b \geq 2$:

| $a = x-6$ | $b = y+1$ | $x$ | $y$ | Valid? |
|-----------|-----------|-----|-----|--------|
| $1$ | $4$ | $7$ | $3$ | ✓ |
| $2$ | $2$ | $8$ | $1$ | ✓ |
| $4$ | $1$ | $10$ | $0$ | $y=0$ ✗ |
| $-1$ | $-4$ | $5$ | $-5$ | $y<0$ ✗ |
| $-2$ | $-2$ | $4$ | $-3$ | $y<0$ ✗ |
| $-4$ | $-1$ | $2$ | $-2$ | $y<0$ ✗ |

Only $(7, 3)$ and $(8, 1)$ are valid positive integer pairs.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
