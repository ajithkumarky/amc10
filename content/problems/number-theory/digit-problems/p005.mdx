<Problem>
How many three-digit positive integers have digits that sum to exactly $7$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We need the count of triples $(d_1, d_2, d_3)$ with $d_1 \in \{1, \ldots, 9\}$, $d_2, d_3 \in \{0, \ldots, 9\}$, and $d_1 + d_2 + d_3 = 7$.

**Shift the leading digit:** let $d_1' = d_1 - 1 \in \{0, \ldots, 8\}$. Then we need
$$d_1' + d_2 + d_3 = 6, \quad d_1', d_2, d_3 \geq 0.$$

**Unrestricted count (stars-and-bars):**
$$\binom{6 + 2}{2} = \binom{8}{2} = 28.$$

**Check upper-bound violations:** Any variable exceeding $9$ would force the other two to sum to at most $-4$, which is impossible. So no solutions violate the upper bounds.

There are $\mathbf{28}$ such three-digit integers.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
