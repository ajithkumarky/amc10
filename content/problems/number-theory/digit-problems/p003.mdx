<Problem>
A two-digit number equals $4$ times the sum of its digits. When the digits are reversed, the new number exceeds the original by $27$. What is the original two-digit number?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the original number be $\overline{ab} = 10a + b$ with $a \geq 1$.

**Condition 1:** The number equals 4 times its digit sum.
$$10a + b = 4(a + b) \implies 6a = 3b \implies b = 2a.$$

**Condition 2:** The reversed number exceeds the original by 27.
$$\overline{ba} - \overline{ab} = (10b + a) - (10a + b) = 9(b - a) = 27 \implies b - a = 3.$$

From $b = 2a$ and $b - a = 3$: substituting gives $2a - a = 3$, so $a = 3$ and $b = 6$.

The original number is $\mathbf{36}$.

**Check:** $36 = 4(3 + 6) = 4 \cdot 9 = 36$ ✓. Reversed: $63 - 36 = 27$ ✓.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
