<Problem>
How many integers from $1$ to $50$, inclusive, are relatively prime to $50$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Two integers are **relatively prime** when their GCD equals $1$. We need to count integers in $\{1, 2, \ldots, 50\}$ with $\gcd(n, 50) = 1$.

This count is Euler's totient $\phi(50)$.

Factor $50 = 2^1 \cdot 5^2$. The totient formula gives:
$$\phi(50) = 50 \cdot \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right)\left(1 - \frac{1}{5}\right) = 50 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{5} = 50 \cdot \frac{4}{10} = 20.$$

In plain terms: among $1$ to $50$, we exclude multiples of $2$ (there are $25$) and multiples of $5$ (there are $10$), but add back multiples of $10$ (there are $5$, counted twice). By inclusion-exclusion:
$$50 - 25 - 10 + 5 = 20.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
