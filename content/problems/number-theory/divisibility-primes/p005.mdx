<Problem>
How many integers from $1$ to $100$ inclusive have exactly $3$ positive divisors?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A positive integer $n$ has exactly $3$ divisors if and only if $n = p^2$ for some prime $p$.

**Why?** If $n = p_1^{a_1} p_2^{a_2} \cdots$, then $\tau(n) = (a_1+1)(a_2+1)\cdots = 3$. Since $3$ is prime, the only factorization is $3 = 3$, meaning there is exactly one prime factor $p$ and $a_1 = 2$. So $n = p^2$.

The divisors of $p^2$ are exactly $1, p, p^2$ — confirming there are $3$.

Now find all primes $p$ with $p^2 \le 100$, i.e., $p \le 10$:
$$p \in \{2, 3, 5, 7\}, \quad p^2 \in \{4, 9, 25, 49\}.$$

(The next prime is $11$, and $11^2 = 121 > 100$.)

There are $\mathbf{4}$ such integers.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
