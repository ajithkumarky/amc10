<Problem>
What is the sum of all positive divisors of $28$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Factor: $28 = 2^2 \times 7$.

The sum-of-divisors formula for $n = p_1^{a_1} p_2^{a_2} \cdots$ is:
$$\sigma(n) = \left(\sum_{i=0}^{a_1} p_1^i\right)\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^{a_2} p_2^j\right)\cdots$$

Apply it to $28$:
$$\sigma(28) = (1 + 2 + 4)(1 + 7) = 7 \times 8 = 56.$$

Verify by listing all divisors of $28$: $1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28$.
$$1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14 + 28 = 56. \checkmark$$

(As a bonus: since $\sigma(28) = 2 \times 28$, the number $28$ is a **perfect number** — a fun number-theory fact!)

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
