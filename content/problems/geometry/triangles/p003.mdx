<Problem>
In right triangle $ABC$ with the right angle at $C$, the altitude from $C$ to the hypotenuse $\overline{AB}$ has length $6$, and the hypotenuse $AB = 13$. Find $AC + BC$.
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Find the area two ways.** Using the hypotenuse and the altitude $h = 6$:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot h = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 13 \cdot 6 = 39$$

Using the two legs:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot AC \cdot BC = 39 \implies AC \cdot BC = 78$$

**Use the Pythagorean theorem.** Since the right angle is at $C$:

$$AC^2 + BC^2 = AB^2 = 169$$

**Combine with the identity** $(AC + BC)^2 = AC^2 + BC^2 + 2 \cdot AC \cdot BC$:

$$(AC + BC)^2 = 169 + 2(78) = 169 + 156 = 325$$

$$AC + BC = \sqrt{325} = \sqrt{25 \cdot 13} = 5\sqrt{13}$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Check feasibility">
We can verify a valid triangle exists: from $AC + BC = 5\sqrt{13}$ and $AC \cdot BC = 78$, the legs satisfy $t^2 - 5\sqrt{13}\,t + 78 = 0$. The discriminant is $325 - 312 = 13 > 0$, confirming two distinct positive legs.
</AlternateMethod>
