<Problem>
For what value of $k$ are the lines $3x - ky = 4$ and $2x + 6y = 7$ perpendicular?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Step 1: Find the slopes.**

Rewrite each line in slope-intercept form.

Line 1: $3x - ky = 4 \implies y = \dfrac{3}{k}x - \dfrac{4}{k}$, so $m_1 = \dfrac{3}{k}$ (assuming $k \neq 0$).

Line 2: $2x + 6y = 7 \implies y = -\dfrac{1}{3}x + \dfrac{7}{6}$, so $m_2 = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.

**Step 2: Apply the perpendicularity condition.**

Two lines are perpendicular when $m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$:

$$\frac{3}{k} \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = -1$$

$$-\frac{1}{k} = -1$$

$$k = 1.$$

**Check:** With $k = 1$, Line 1 is $3x - y = 4$, slope $3$. Line 2 has slope $-\tfrac{1}{3}$. Indeed $3 \cdot (-\tfrac{1}{3}) = -1$. $\checkmark$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
