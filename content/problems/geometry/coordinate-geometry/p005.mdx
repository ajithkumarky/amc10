<Problem>
Point $T = (3, -1)$ is reflected over the line $y = x$ to obtain point $T'$. What is the distance from $T'$ to the origin?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Step 1: Reflect $T$ over $y = x$.**

Reflecting a point $(a, b)$ over the line $y = x$ swaps its coordinates: $(a, b) \mapsto (b, a)$.

So $T = (3, -1)$ maps to $T' = (-1, 3)$.

**Step 2: Compute the distance from $T'$ to the origin.**

$$d = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Distance preserved check">
Note that $T = (3,-1)$ is at distance $\sqrt{9+1} = \sqrt{10}$ from the origin as well. This is expected: reflecting over any line through the origin preserves all distances from the origin. Both $T$ and $T'$ lie on a circle of radius $\sqrt{10}$ centered at the origin.
</AlternateMethod>
