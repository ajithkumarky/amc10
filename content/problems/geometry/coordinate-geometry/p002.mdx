<Problem>
Let $M$ be the midpoint of the segment joining $A = (-3, 7)$ and $B = (5, -1)$. Find the equation of the line that passes through $M$ and is perpendicular to $\overline{AB}$.
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Step 1: Find the midpoint.**

$$M = \left(\frac{-3 + 5}{2},\; \frac{7 + (-1)}{2}\right) = \left(1,\; 3\right).$$

**Step 2: Find the slope of $\overline{AB}$.**

$$m_{AB} = \frac{-1 - 7}{5 - (-3)} = \frac{-8}{8} = -1.$$

**Step 3: Find the perpendicular slope.**

The perpendicular slope is the negative reciprocal of $-1$:

$$m_\perp = -\frac{1}{-1} = 1.$$

**Step 4: Write the line equation.**

Using point-slope form through $M = (1, 3)$ with slope $1$:

$$y - 3 = 1 \cdot (x - 1) \implies y = x + 2.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Symmetry check">
The perpendicular bisector of a segment is the set of all points equidistant from both endpoints. To verify, check that $M = (1,3)$ satisfies $y = x + 2$: indeed $3 = 1 + 2$. Also confirm the slope $1 \neq -1 = m_{AB}$ and that $1 \cdot (-1) = -1$, confirming perpendicularity.
</AlternateMethod>
