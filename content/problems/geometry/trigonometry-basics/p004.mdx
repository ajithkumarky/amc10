<Problem>
In triangle $ABC$, $AB = 7$, $BC = 8$, and $\angle B = 120°$. What is the length of side $AC$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We apply the Law of Cosines. Side $AC$ is opposite $\angle B$, so:
$$AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2 - 2 \cdot AB \cdot BC \cdot \cos(\angle B).$$

Substituting $AB = 7$, $BC = 8$, and $\cos 120° = -\dfrac{1}{2}$:
$$AC^2 = 49 + 64 - 2(7)(8)\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = 113 + 56 = 169.$$

Therefore $AC = \sqrt{169} = 13$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Why the obtuse angle increases the side">
With $\angle B = 120°$ the two sides $AB$ and $BC$ "open up" beyond a right angle, pushing $A$ and $C$ farther apart. Contrast with $\angle B = 90°$: then $AC = \sqrt{49+64} = \sqrt{113} \approx 10.6$. The obtuse angle adds $56$ under the radical, raising $AC$ to $13$ — a clean answer signaling a Pythagorean-type structure.
</AlternateMethod>
