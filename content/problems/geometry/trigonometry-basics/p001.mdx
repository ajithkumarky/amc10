<Problem>
In right triangle $ABC$ with the right angle at $C$, leg $AC = 3$ and leg $BC = 4$. What is $\sin(\angle BAC)$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
First find the hypotenuse using the Pythagorean theorem:
$$AB = \sqrt{AC^2 + BC^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5.$$

For $\angle BAC$ (the angle at vertex $A$), by SOH-CAH-TOA:
- **Opposite** side: $BC = 4$
- **Hypotenuse**: $AB = 5$

$$\sin(\angle BAC) = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{BC}{AB} = \frac{4}{5}.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Pythagorean triple recognition">
The sides $3$, $4$, $5$ form the classic Pythagorean triple. The sine of the angle opposite the leg of length $4$ is $\dfrac{4}{5}$ directly.
</AlternateMethod>
