<Problem>
In triangle $ABC$, $AB = 10$, $BC = 6$, and the area of the triangle is $24$. What is $\sin(\angle ABC)$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The area formula using two sides and the included angle gives:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot BC \cdot \sin(\angle ABC).$$

Substituting $AB = 10$, $BC = 6$, and Area $= 24$:
$$24 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot 6 \cdot \sin(\angle ABC) = 30\sin(\angle ABC).$$

Solving:
$$\sin(\angle ABC) = \frac{24}{30} = \frac{4}{5}.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Checking plausibility">
The maximum possible area for two sides of length $10$ and $6$ occurs when $\sin(\angle ABC) = 1$, giving area $= 30$. Our area is $24 = 30 \cdot \tfrac{4}{5}$, so $\sin(\angle ABC) = \tfrac{4}{5}$, which corresponds to a $\approx 53.1°$ angle — entirely reasonable.
</AlternateMethod>
