<Problem>
Two right circular cones are similar. The smaller cone has base radius $4$ and height $6$. The larger cone has base radius $6$. What is the volume of the larger cone?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Step 1 — find the scale factor.**

Since the cones are similar, all corresponding linear dimensions are proportional. The base radii are $4$ (small) and $6$ (large), so the linear scale factor is:
$$k = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}$$

**Step 2 — find the volume of the smaller cone.**

$$V_{\text{small}} = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi (4)^2 (6) = \frac{1}{3}\pi \cdot 96 = 32\pi$$

**Step 3 — scale the volume.**

Volumes of similar solids scale as $k^3$:
$$V_{\text{large}} = k^3 \cdot V_{\text{small}} = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^3 \cdot 32\pi = \frac{27}{8} \cdot 32\pi = 108\pi$$

*Check:* The larger cone has height $6 \cdot \tfrac{3}{2} = 9$. Directly: $V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(6)^2(9) = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi \cdot 324 = 108\pi$. ✓

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
