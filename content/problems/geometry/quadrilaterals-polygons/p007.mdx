<Problem>
A regular octagon has side length $2$. What is its area?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place the regular octagon inside the smallest square that contains it. Each corner of the square is cut off as a right isosceles triangle to produce the octagon.

If the octagon has side length $s = 2$, then each cut-off triangle has legs of length $a$, where the hypotenuse equals the octagon's side:
$$a\sqrt{2} = 2 \implies a = \sqrt{2}$$

The outer square has side length:
$$s + 2a = 2 + 2\sqrt{2}$$

Area of the outer square:
$$(2 + 2\sqrt{2})^2 = 4 + 8\sqrt{2} + 8 = 12 + 8\sqrt{2}$$

Each corner triangle has area $\dfrac{1}{2}a^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2})^2 = 1$. There are four such triangles, so their total area is $4$.

Area of the octagon:
$$(12 + 8\sqrt{2}) - 4 = 8 + 8\sqrt{2}$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
