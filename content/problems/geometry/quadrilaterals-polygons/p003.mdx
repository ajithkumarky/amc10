<Problem>
A rhombus has diagonals of length $10$ and $24$. What is the perimeter of the rhombus?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles. Each half-diagonal has lengths $5$ and $12$, forming the legs of a right triangle whose hypotenuse is one side of the rhombus.

By the Pythagorean theorem:
$$\text{side} = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$$

Since all four sides of a rhombus are equal, the perimeter is:
$$4 \times 13 = 52$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Recognizing a 5-12-13 right triangle">
The half-diagonals $5$ and $12$ are the two shorter legs of the well-known Pythagorean triple $(5, 12, 13)$. Recognizing this triple immediately gives the side length $13$ without computing $\sqrt{169}$.

Perimeter $= 4 \times 13 = 52$.

Answer: **C**.
</AlternateMethod>
