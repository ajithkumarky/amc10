<Problem>
A square has side length $6$. A circle is circumscribed about the square (passes through all four vertices), and a second circle is inscribed in the square (tangent to all four sides). What is the area of the region inside the circumscribed circle but outside the inscribed circle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Inscribed circle:** The inscribed circle is tangent to all four sides of the square, so its diameter equals the side length. With side $6$, the inscribed circle has radius $r_{\text{in}} = 3$.

$$A_{\text{in}} = \pi(3)^2 = 9\pi.$$

**Circumscribed circle:** The circumscribed circle passes through all four vertices. Its radius equals half the diagonal of the square.

$$\text{Diagonal} = 6\sqrt{2}, \quad r_{\text{out}} = 3\sqrt{2}.$$

$$A_{\text{out}} = \pi(3\sqrt{2})^2 = \pi \cdot 18 = 18\pi.$$

**Annular region:**

$$A = A_{\text{out}} - A_{\text{in}} = 18\pi - 9\pi = 9\pi.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
