<Problem>
What is the remainder when $x^5 - 1$ is divided by $x^2 + x + 1$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The remainder of dividing a degree-$5$ polynomial by a degree-$2$ divisor has degree at most $1$, so write

$$x^5 - 1 = (x^2+x+1)\,q(x) + (ax + b).$$

Let $\omega$ be a root of $x^2 + x + 1 = 0$. Such a root satisfies $\omega^3 = 1$ (it is a primitive cube root of unity, since $x^3 - 1 = (x-1)(x^2+x+1)$).

**Evaluate at $x = \omega$:** Since $(x^2+x+1)\,q(\omega) = 0$,

$$\omega^5 - 1 = a\omega + b.$$

Reduce the exponent: $\omega^5 = \omega^{3+2} = \omega^2$. Also, from $\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$ we get $\omega^2 = -\omega - 1$. So

$$\omega^5 - 1 = \omega^2 - 1 = (-\omega - 1) - 1 = -\omega - 2.$$

Matching coefficients of $\omega$ and the constant: $a = -1$, $b = -2$.

The remainder is $\boxed{-x - 2}$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
