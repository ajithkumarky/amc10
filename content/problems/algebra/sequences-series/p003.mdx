<Problem>
In a geometric sequence, the $2$nd term is $6$ and the $5$th term is $162$. What is the $7$th term?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the first term be $a$ and the common ratio be $r$. Then:

$$a_2 = ar = 6 \qquad \text{and} \qquad a_5 = ar^4 = 162.$$

Dividing the second equation by the first:

$$\frac{ar^4}{ar} = r^3 = \frac{162}{6} = 27 \implies r = 3.$$

From $ar = 6$: $a = 6/3 = 2$.

The 7th term is:

$$a_7 = ar^6 = 2 \cdot 3^6 = 2 \cdot 729 = 1458.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Stepping from the 5th term">
From the 5th term, multiply by $r = 3$ twice: $a_6 = 162 \cdot 3 = 486$ and $a_7 = 486 \cdot 3 = 1458$.
</AlternateMethod>
