<Problem>
What is $\dfrac{8^{2/3}}{4^{-1/2}}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Evaluate the numerator and denominator separately.

**Numerator:** $8^{2/3} = \left(8^{1/3}\right)^2 = 2^2 = 4$, since $8^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{8} = 2$.

**Denominator:** $4^{-1/2} = \dfrac{1}{4^{1/2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Divide:

$$\frac{8^{2/3}}{4^{-1/2}} = \frac{4}{1/2} = 4 \times 2 = 8.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Single power-of-2 base">
Write everything as a power of $2$: $8 = 2^3$ and $4 = 2^2$.

$$\frac{(2^3)^{2/3}}{(2^2)^{-1/2}} = \frac{2^{3 \cdot 2/3}}{2^{2 \cdot (-1/2)}} = \frac{2^2}{2^{-1}} = 2^{2-(-1)} = 2^3 = 8.$$
</AlternateMethod>
