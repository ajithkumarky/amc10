<Problem>
What is $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{\!\log_2 12}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $k = \log_2 12$, so by definition $2^k = 12$.

Rewrite the base $\frac{1}{2}$ as a power of $2$:

$$\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^k = (2^{-1})^k = 2^{-k}.$$

Now express $2^{-k}$ in terms of $2^k = 12$:

$$2^{-k} = \frac{1}{2^k} = \frac{1}{12}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Substitution with exponent identity">
Write $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\log_2 12} = \frac{1}{2^{\log_2 12}}$. The denominator equals $2^{\log_2 12} = 12$ (by the fundamental identity $b^{\log_b x} = x$). So the expression equals $\frac{1}{12}$.
</AlternateMethod>
