<Problem>
A car drives from town $A$ to town $B$ at a speed of 60 mph, then immediately returns from $B$ to $A$ at a speed of 40 mph. What is the car's average speed, in miles per hour, for the entire round trip?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $d$ miles be the one-way distance from $A$ to $B$.

Time from $A$ to $B$: $\dfrac{d}{60}$ hours.

Time from $B$ to $A$: $\dfrac{d}{40}$ hours.

Total distance traveled: $2d$ miles.

Total time: $\dfrac{d}{60} + \dfrac{d}{40} = \dfrac{2d}{120} + \dfrac{3d}{120} = \dfrac{5d}{120} = \dfrac{d}{24}$ hours.

Average speed $= \dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} = \dfrac{2d}{d/24} = 2d \cdot \dfrac{24}{d} = 48$ mph.

Notice that the distance $d$ cancels — the average speed depends only on the two speeds, not the distance. The formula for a round trip is the harmonic mean: $\dfrac{2 \cdot 60 \cdot 40}{60 + 40} = \dfrac{4800}{100} = 48$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
