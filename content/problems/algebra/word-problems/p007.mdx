<Problem>
Workers $A$, $B$, and $C$ can complete a certain job alone in 10, 15, and 20 days respectively. All three start working together. After 3 days, worker $A$ leaves. Two days after $A$ leaves, worker $B$ also leaves. Worker $C$ then finishes the job alone. How many days does $C$ work alone to complete the job?
</Problem>

<Solution>
It helps to track the fraction of the job completed in each phase.

**Phase 1** (days 1–3, all three work):

$$3\!\left(\frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{20}\right) = 3 \cdot \frac{6 + 4 + 3}{60} = 3 \cdot \frac{13}{60} = \frac{13}{20}.$$

**Phase 2** (next 2 days, $B$ and $C$ work):

$$2\!\left(\frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{20}\right) = 2 \cdot \frac{4 + 3}{60} = 2 \cdot \frac{7}{60} = \frac{7}{30}.$$

**Total completed** after both phases:

$$\frac{13}{20} + \frac{7}{30} = \frac{39}{60} + \frac{14}{60} = \frac{53}{60}.$$

**Remaining fraction:** $1 - \dfrac{53}{60} = \dfrac{7}{60}$.

Worker $C$ alone does $\tfrac{1}{20}$ of the job per day, so the time for $C$ to finish is:

$$\frac{7/60}{1/20} = \frac{7}{60} \times 20 = \frac{140}{60} = \frac{7}{3} \text{ days.}$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
