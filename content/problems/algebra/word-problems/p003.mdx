<Problem>
A 40-liter solution is 30% acid. Pure water (0% acid) is added to dilute the solution to 20% acid. How many liters of pure water must be added?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The amount of acid in the original solution is $0.30 \times 40 = 12$ liters, and this amount does not change when water is added.

Let $w$ be the number of liters of water added. The new total volume is $40 + w$ liters, and we need the acid concentration to be 20%:

$$\frac{12}{40 + w} = 0.20.$$

Solving: $12 = 0.20(40 + w) = 8 + 0.20w$, so $0.20w = 4$, giving $w = 20$.

**Check:** After adding 20 liters, the total volume is 60 liters and the acid is still 12 liters. Concentration: $12/60 = 0.20 = 20\%$. ✓

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
