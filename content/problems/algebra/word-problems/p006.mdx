<Problem>
A chemist has 8 liters of a solution that is 25% salt. She wants to mix in some of a 60% salt solution to produce a mixture that is exactly 40% salt. How many liters of the 60% salt solution must she add?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $x$ = liters of 60% solution added.

The amount of salt before mixing: $0.25 \times 8 = 2$ liters of salt from the first solution, plus $0.60x$ liters of salt from the second solution.

The final mixture has volume $8 + x$ liters and must be 40% salt:

$$0.25(8) + 0.60x = 0.40(8 + x).$$

$$2 + 0.60x = 3.2 + 0.40x.$$

$$0.20x = 1.2 \implies x = 6.$$

**Check:** Final volume = 14 liters. Salt = $2 + 0.60(6) = 2 + 3.6 = 5.6$ liters. Concentration = $5.6/14 = 0.40 = 40\%$. ✓

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
