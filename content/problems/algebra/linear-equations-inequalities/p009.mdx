<Problem>
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are real numbers satisfying
$$2a + b = 17 \quad \text{and} \quad a + 2b = 16.$$
What is the value of $a + b$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A direct approach (solve for $a$ and $b$ separately) works but takes time. The key insight is to add the two equations:
$$(2a + b) + (a + 2b) = 17 + 16$$
$$3a + 3b = 33$$
$$3(a + b) = 33$$
$$a + b = 11.$$

No individual values of $a$ or $b$ are needed.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Solving individually to verify">
From $2a + b = 17$: $b = 17 - 2a$. Substitute into $a + 2b = 16$:
$$a + 2(17 - 2a) = 16 \implies a + 34 - 4a = 16 \implies -3a = -18 \implies a = 6.$$

Then $b = 17 - 12 = 5$. So $a + b = 11$. ✓

The "add the equations" shortcut is crucial at AMC speed — looking for a clever combination that yields the target expression directly is a hallmark of difficulty-5 thinking.
</AlternateMethod>
