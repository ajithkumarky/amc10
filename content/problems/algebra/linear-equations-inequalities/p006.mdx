<Problem>
For what value of $k$ is the equation $5(2x + 1) - 3(x - 4) = 7x + k$ an identity (true for every real number $x$)?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Expand the left side:
$$10x + 5 - 3x + 12 = 7x + k$$
$$7x + 17 = 7x + k.$$

For this to hold for **every** value of $x$, the constant terms must be equal (the $7x$ terms already match):
$$k = 17.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Plugging in two values">
If the equation is an identity, it must work for $x = 0$ and $x = 1$.

$x = 0$: left side $= 5(1) - 3(-4) = 5 + 12 = 17$, right side $= k$. So $k = 17$.

Verify with $x = 1$: left $= 5(3) - 3(-3) = 15 + 9 = 24$, right $= 7 + 17 = 24$. ✓
</AlternateMethod>
