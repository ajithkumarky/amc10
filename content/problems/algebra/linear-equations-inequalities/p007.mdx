<Problem>
For what positive value of $m$ does the system of equations
$$3x + my = 12$$
$$9x + 6y = m$$
have no solution?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A 2×2 linear system has no solution when the two lines are parallel — same slope (so same ratio of coefficients) but different right-hand sides.

Write both equations in the form $ax + by = c$:
- Equation 1: $3x + my = 12$
- Equation 2: $9x + 6y = m$

The coefficient ratio condition (parallel lines) requires:
$$\frac{3}{9} = \frac{m}{6} \implies \frac{1}{3} = \frac{m}{6} \implies m = 2.$$

Now check that the system is actually **inconsistent** (not just dependent) when $m = 2$: multiply Equation 1 by 3 to get $9x + 6y = 36$, but Equation 2 says $9x + 6y = 2$. Since $36 \neq 2$, the system is inconsistent and has **no solution**.

The positive value is $m = 2$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Determinant approach">
The system $ax + by = e$, $cx + dy = f$ has no unique solution when the determinant $\Delta = ad - bc = 0$.

Here $\Delta = 3 \cdot 6 - m \cdot 9 = 18 - 9m = 0 \implies m = 2$.

Since the augmented-matrix check shows $36 \neq 2$ (as above), the system is inconsistent, not dependent. So $m = 2$.
</AlternateMethod>
