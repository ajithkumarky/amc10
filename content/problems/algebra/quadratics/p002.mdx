<Problem>
The roots of $x^2 - 6x + p = 0$ differ by $2$. What is $p$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
By Vieta's formulas, the sum of the roots is $6$. Let the roots be $r$ and $r + 2$. Then $r + (r+2) = 6$, so $r = 2$ and the roots are $2$ and $4$.

The product of the roots equals $p$ (from Vieta), so $p = 2 \cdot 4 = 8$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Discriminant approach">
If the roots differ by $2$, then $|r_1 - r_2| = 2$. Recall that $(r_1 - r_2)^2 = (r_1 + r_2)^2 - 4 r_1 r_2 = 36 - 4p$. Setting this equal to $4$ gives $4 = 36 - 4p$, so $p = 8$.
</AlternateMethod>
