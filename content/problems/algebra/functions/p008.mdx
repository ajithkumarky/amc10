<Problem>
The graph of $y = f(x)$ is shifted left 2 units and then reflected over the $x$-axis to produce the graph of $y = g(x)$. If $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 3$, what is the maximum value of $g(x)$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Step 1 — Shift left by 2.** Replacing $x$ with $x + 2$ shifts the graph left by $2$:

$$h(x) = f(x + 2) = (x+2)^2 - 4(x+2) + 3.$$

Expand:

$$(x+2)^2 = x^2 + 4x + 4, \quad -4(x+2) = -4x - 8.$$

$$h(x) = x^2 + 4x + 4 - 4x - 8 + 3 = x^2 - 1.$$

**Step 2 — Reflect over the $x$-axis.** Negate the function:

$$g(x) = -h(x) = -(x^2 - 1) = 1 - x^2.$$

**Step 3 — Find the maximum.** Since $g(x) = 1 - x^2$ is a downward-opening parabola, its maximum is at $x = 0$:

$$g(0) = 1 - 0 = 1.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
