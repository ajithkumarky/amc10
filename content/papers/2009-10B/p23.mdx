<Problem>
Rachel and Robert run on a circular track. Rachel runs counterclockwise and completes a lap every $90$ seconds, and Robert runs clockwise and completes a lap every $80$ seconds. Both start from the same line at the same time. At some random time between $10$ minutes and $11$ minutes after they begin to run, a photographer standing inside the track takes a picture that shows one-fourth of the track, centered on the starting line. What is the probability that both Rachel and Robert are in the picture?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The picture covers $\tfrac{1}{4}$ of the track centered on the start line, so each runner must be within $\tfrac{1}{8}$ of a lap from the start. Work in seconds with $t \in [600, 660]$.

**Rachel** (period 90 s) is in frame when $t$ is within $\tfrac{90}{8} = 11.25$ s of a multiple of $90$. The only multiple of $90$ in or near $[600,660]$ is $t=630$, so Rachel is in frame for $t \in [618.75,\, 641.25]$.

**Robert** (period 80 s) is in frame when $t$ is within $\tfrac{80}{8} = 10$ s of a multiple of $80$. The only multiple of $80$ relevant to $[600,660]$ is $t=640$, so Robert is in frame for $t \in [630,\, 650]$.

**Both in frame:** the intersection $[630, 641.25]$ has length $11.25$ seconds.

$$P = \frac{11.25}{60} = \frac{45/4}{60} = \frac{45}{240} = \frac{3}{16}.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
