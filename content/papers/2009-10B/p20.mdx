<Problem>
Triangle $ABC$ has a right angle at $B$, $AB=1$, and $BC=2$. The angle bisector of $\angle A$ intersects side $\overline{BC}$ at $D$. What is $BD$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
By the Angle Bisector Theorem, $D$ divides $\overline{BC}$ in the ratio $BD:DC = AB:AC$.

We have $AB = 1$ and $BC = 2$, so $AC = \sqrt{AB^2 + BC^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4} = \sqrt{5}$.

Thus $BD:DC = AB:AC = 1:\sqrt{5}$.

Since $BD + DC = BC = 2$:
$$BD = \frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{5}} \cdot 2 = \frac{2}{1+\sqrt{5}} = \frac{2({\sqrt{5}-1})}{(\sqrt{5}+1)(\sqrt{5}-1)} = \frac{2(\sqrt{5}-1)}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
