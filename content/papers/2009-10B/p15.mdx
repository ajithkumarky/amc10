<Problem>
When a bucket is two-thirds full of water, the bucket and water weigh $a$ kilograms. When the bucket is one-half full of water the total weight is $b$ kilograms. In terms of $a$ and $b$, what is the total weight in kilograms when the bucket is full of water?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $w$ be the weight of the empty bucket and $f$ be the weight of a full bucket of water (water only). Then:

$$w + \frac{2}{3}f = a, \qquad w + \frac{1}{2}f = b.$$

Subtract the second from the first:
$$\frac{2}{3}f - \frac{1}{2}f = a - b \implies \frac{1}{6}f = a - b \implies f = 6(a-b).$$

From the second equation: $w = b - \dfrac{1}{2}f = b - 3(a-b) = b - 3a + 3b = 4b - 3a$.

Total weight when full: $w + f = (4b - 3a) + 6(a-b) = 4b - 3a + 6a - 6b = 3a - 2b$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
