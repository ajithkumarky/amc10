<Problem>
Each face of a cube is given a single narrow stripe painted from the center of one edge to the center of the opposite edge. The choice of the edge pairing is made at random and independently for each face. What is the probability that there is a continuous stripe encircling the cube?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Each face is a square with two possible stripe orientations (horizontal or vertical, connecting opposite edge midpoints). So each face has a $\tfrac{1}{2}$ probability for each orientation, and all $6$ faces are independent.

A continuous stripe encircling the cube is a closed loop of stripes that wraps around. Such a loop must go through $4$ faces in a band (either the 4 faces around the vertical axis, the 4 around one horizontal axis, etc.). There are $3$ such bands corresponding to the $3$ pairs of opposite faces.

For a band of $4$ faces (say the front, right, back, left faces going around), all $4$ faces must have their stripes aligned perpendicular to the axis of the band (i.e., horizontal stripes if the band goes around the vertical axis). This requires each of the $4$ faces in the band to have its stripe in the correct orientation.

**Probability a specific band forms a loop:** Each of the $4$ faces in the band must choose the correct orientation: probability $\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^4 = \tfrac{1}{16}$. The other $2$ faces (top and bottom) can have any orientation.

**Three bands:** Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be the events that bands 1, 2, 3 respectively form a loop. By inclusion-exclusion:

$P(A \cup B \cup C) = 3P(A) - 3P(A \cap B) + P(A \cap B \cap C).$

$P(A) = \tfrac{1}{16}$ (the 4 faces in band $A$ all correct; the 2 others are free).

$P(A \cap B)$: Bands $A$ and $B$ share 2 faces. The 4 faces in $A$ need one orientation; the 4 faces in $B$ need another orientation. But the 2 shared faces need BOTH orientations simultaneously — impossible! So $P(A \cap B) = 0$ when the two bands share faces and require conflicting orientations. Actually, band $A$ (vertical axis) requires those 4 faces to be horizontal; band $B$ (one horizontal axis) requires its 4 faces to be vertical. Two faces belong to both bands — they can't be both horizontal and vertical, so $P(A \cap B) = 0$.

Thus $P(A \cup B \cup C) = 3 \times \tfrac{1}{16} = \tfrac{3}{16}$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
