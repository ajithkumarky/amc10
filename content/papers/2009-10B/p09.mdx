<Problem>
Segment $BD$ and $AE$ intersect at $C$, as shown, $AB=BC=CD=CE$, and $\angle A = \frac{5}{2} \angle B$. What is the degree measure of $\angle D$?

The figure shows two triangles sharing vertex $C$: triangle $ABC$ (with $A$ at lower-left, $B$ at upper-left, $C$ in the middle) and triangle $DCE$ (with $D$ at lower-right, $E$ at upper-right). We have $AB = BC = CD = CE$.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since $AB = BC$, triangle $ABC$ is isosceles with $\angle BAC = \angle BCA$... wait, $AB = BC$ means the angles opposite them are equal: $\angle BCA = \angle BAC$. Let $\angle B = \beta$, so $\angle A = \frac{5}{2}\beta$.

In triangle $ABC$: $\angle A + \angle B + \angle ACB = 180°$.

Since $AB = BC$, we have $\angle BAC = \angle BCA$, but $\angle BAC = \angle A = \frac{5}{2}\beta$.

So $\frac{5}{2}\beta + \beta + \frac{5}{2}\beta = 180°$, giving $6\beta = 180°$, so $\beta = 30°$.

Thus $\angle A = \frac{5}{2}(30°) = 75°$ and $\angle ACB = 75°$.

Since $BD$ and $AE$ are straight lines intersecting at $C$, $\angle DCE$ is the vertical angle to $\angle ACB$:
$$\angle DCE = \angle ACB = 75°.$$

Since $CD = CE$, triangle $DCE$ is isosceles with $\angle CDE = \angle CED$.
$$\angle D = \frac{180° - 75°}{2} = \frac{105°}{2} = 52.5°.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
