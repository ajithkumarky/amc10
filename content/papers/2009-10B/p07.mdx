<Problem>
By inserting parentheses, it is possible to give the expression $2\times3 + 4\times5$ several values. How many different values can be obtained?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The expression has three operations: two multiplications and one addition. By inserting parentheses, we change the order of operations. Let's systematically list all possible parenthesizations and their values:

1. $2 \times 3 + 4 \times 5 = 6 + 20 = 26$ (standard order)
2. $(2 \times 3 + 4) \times 5 = 10 \times 5 = 50$
3. $2 \times (3 + 4 \times 5) = 2 \times 23 = 46$
4. $2 \times (3 + 4) \times 5 = 2 \times 7 \times 5 = 70$
5. $(2 \times 3 + 4 \times 5)$ is just the original $= 26$, same as (1)
6. $2 \times (3 + 4) \times 5 = 70$, already counted

Let me be more careful. The four numbers 2, 3, 4, 5 with operations $\times, +, \times$ are fixed in that order. Possible groupings:

- $((2 \times 3) + 4) \times 5 = 10 \times 5 = 50$
- $(2 \times (3 + 4)) \times 5 = 14 \times 5 = 70$
- $(2 \times 3) + (4 \times 5) = 6 + 20 = 26$
- $2 \times ((3 + 4) \times 5) = 2 \times 35 = 70$ (duplicate)
- $2 \times (3 + (4 \times 5)) = 2 \times 23 = 46$

The distinct values are $26, 46, 50, 70$ — that's $4$ values.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
