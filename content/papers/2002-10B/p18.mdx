<Problem>
Four distinct circles are drawn in a plane. What is the maximum number of points where at least two of the circles intersect?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Two distinct circles can intersect in at most $2$ points. The number of pairs of circles from $4$ circles is $\binom{4}{2} = 6$.

Therefore the maximum number of intersection points is $6 \times 2 = 12$.

This maximum is achievable: arrange the four circles so that every pair intersects in exactly $2$ distinct points, and all $12$ intersection points are distinct (no three circles share a common intersection point). Such a configuration exists.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
