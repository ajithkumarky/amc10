<Problem>
When $15$ is appended to a list of integers, the mean is increased by $2$. When $1$ is appended to the enlarged list, the mean of the enlarged list is decreased by $1$. How many integers were in the original list?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the original list have $n$ integers with mean $\mu$.

**After appending 15:** The new list has $n+1$ integers with mean $\mu + 2$, so

$$\frac{n\mu + 15}{n+1} = \mu + 2 \implies n\mu + 15 = n\mu + 2n + \mu + 2 \implies \mu = 13 - 2n. \tag{1}$$

**After appending 1 to that list:** The list now has $n+2$ integers. The sum of the $n+1$ element list is $(n+1)(\mu+2)$, and appending $1$ gives mean $(\mu+2)-1 = \mu+1$, so

$$\frac{(n+1)(\mu+2) + 1}{n+2} = \mu + 1 \implies (n+1)(\mu+2) + 1 = (n+2)(\mu+1).$$

Expanding: $n\mu + 2n + \mu + 3 = n\mu + n + 2\mu + 2$, which simplifies to $n + 1 = \mu$. $\tag{2}$

Substituting (2) into (1): $n + 1 = 13 - 2n \Rightarrow 3n = 12 \Rightarrow n = 4$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
