<Problem>
The arithmetic mean of the nine numbers in the set $\{9, 99, 999, 9999, \ldots, 999999999\}$ is a $9$-digit number $M$, all of whose digits are distinct. The number $M$ does not contain the digit
</Problem>

<Solution>
The nine numbers are $\underbrace{9\cdots9}_{k}= 10^k - 1$ for $k = 1, 2, \ldots, 9$.

Their sum:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{9}(10^k - 1) = (10 + 10^2 + \cdots + 10^9) - 9 = \frac{10(10^9 - 1)}{9} - 9 = \frac{10^{10} - 10 - 81}{9} = \frac{10^{10} - 91}{9}.$$

The mean is this divided by $9$:
$$M = \frac{10^{10} - 91}{81}.$$

Now $10^{10} = 10{,}000{,}000{,}000$, so:
$$81 \times M = 9{,}999{,}999{,}909.$$

Divide: $M = 9{,}999{,}999{,}909 \div 81$.

$81 \times 100{,}000{,}000 = 8{,}100{,}000{,}000$. Remainder: $1{,}899{,}999{,}909$.
$81 \times 23{,}000{,}000 = 1{,}863{,}000{,}000$. Remainder: $36{,}999{,}909$.
$81 \times 456{,}000 = 36{,}936{,}000$. Remainder: $63{,}909$.
$81 \times 789 = 63{,}909$.

So $M = 123{,}456{,}789$.

The digits of $M$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9$ — all nine nonzero digits. The missing digit is $0$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
