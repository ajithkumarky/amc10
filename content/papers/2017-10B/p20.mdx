<Problem>
The number $21! = 51{,}090{,}942{,}171{,}709{,}440{,}000$ has over $60{,}000$ positive integer divisors. One of them is chosen at random. What is the probability that it is odd?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A divisor of $21!$ is odd if and only if it has no factor of $2$.

First, find the exact power of $2$ in $21!$ using Legendre's formula:
$$\nu_2(21!) = \left\lfloor\frac{21}{2}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor\frac{21}{4}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor\frac{21}{8}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor\frac{21}{16}\right\rfloor = 10 + 5 + 2 + 1 = 18.$$

So $21! = 2^{18} \cdot m$ where $m$ is odd.

The total number of divisors of $21!$ is the product of $(e_p + 1)$ over all prime powers $p^{e_p}$ in the factorization. The divisors of $21!$ are of the form $2^a \cdot d$ where $0 \leq a \leq 18$ and $d \mid m$.

The odd divisors are those with $a = 0$, i.e., divisors of $m$. So the fraction of odd divisors is:
$$\frac{\text{number of divisors of } m}{\text{number of divisors of } 21!} = \frac{1}{18 + 1} = \frac{1}{19}.$$

This is because the $2$-component contributes exactly $19$ choices ($a = 0, 1, \ldots, 18$), and a divisor is odd exactly when $a = 0$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
