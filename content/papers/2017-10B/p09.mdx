<Problem>
A radio program has a quiz consisting of $3$ multiple-choice questions, each with $3$ choices. A contestant wins if he or she gets $2$ or more of the questions right. The contestant answers randomly to each question. What is the probability of winning?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Each question is answered correctly with probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and incorrectly with probability $\dfrac{2}{3}$, independently.

The contestant wins by getting exactly $2$ or exactly $3$ correct.

**Exactly 3 correct:** $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^3 = \dfrac{1}{27}$.

**Exactly 2 correct:** Choose which $2$ questions are correct: $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ ways. Each such outcome has probability $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2 \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{2}{27}$. Total: $3 \cdot \dfrac{2}{27} = \dfrac{6}{27}$.

**Total probability of winning:**
$$\frac{6}{27} + \frac{1}{27} = \frac{7}{27}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
