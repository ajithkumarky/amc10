<Problem>
How many of the base-ten numerals for the positive integers less than or equal to $2017$ contain the digit $0$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We count integers from $1$ to $2017$ that contain at least one digit $0$. It's easier to count those containing a $0$ directly by cases.

**1-digit numbers ($1$–$9$):** None contain $0$.

**2-digit numbers ($10$–$99$):** The tens digit is $1$–$9$, units digit is $0$–$9$. Those with a $0$ must have units digit $0$: $10, 20, \ldots, 90$ — that's $9$ numbers.

**3-digit numbers ($100$–$999$):** Form $\overline{abc}$ where $a \in \{1,\ldots,9\}$, $b,c \in \{0,\ldots,9\}$. Count those with at least one $0$ among $b, c$:
- Total 3-digit numbers: $900$.
- Those with no $0$ (i.e., $b,c \in \{1,\ldots,9\}$): $9 \times 9 \times 9 = 729$.
- Those with at least one $0$: $900 - 729 = 171$.

**4-digit numbers ($1000$–$2017$):**

Split into $1000$–$1999$ and $2000$–$2017$.

*$1000$–$1999$:* Form $1\overline{bcd}$ where $b,c,d \in \{0,\ldots,9\}$. Always has leading $1$, so we need at least one $0$ among $b, c, d$:
- Total: $1000$.
- Those with no $0$ ($b,c,d \in \{1,\ldots,9\}$): $9^3 = 729$.
- Those with at least one $0$: $1000 - 729 = 271$.

*$2000$–$2017$:* These are $2000, 2001, \ldots, 2017$ — all $18$ numbers. Which contain a $0$?
- $2000$: three $0$s. Yes.
- $2001$–$2009$: form $200d$, all contain $0$. That's $9$ numbers.
- $2010$: $2, 0, 1, 0$ — contains $0$. Yes.
- $2011$–$2017$: form $201d$ with $d \in \{1,\ldots,7\}$. None contain $0$ since digits are $2,0,1,d$ — wait, $0$ appears in position 2! So all of $2011$–$2017$ contain $0$.

Numbers $2000$–$2017$ that contain $0$: all $18$ (since every number $2000$–$2017$ has $0$ as the hundreds digit or lower). Let me verify: $2000$ has $0$s; $2001$–$2009$ have $0$s; $2010$ has $0$s; $2011$–$2017$ all have $0$ in the tens place. Yes, all $18$ contain $0$.

Wait — check $2011$: digits are $2, 0, 1, 1$. Yes, has a $0$.

So all $18$ numbers from $2000$ to $2017$ contain a $0$.

**Total:** $0 + 9 + 171 + 271 + 18 = 469$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
