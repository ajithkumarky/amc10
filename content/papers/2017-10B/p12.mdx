<Problem>
Elmer's new car gives $50\%$ better fuel efficiency. However, the new car uses diesel fuel, which is $20\%$ more expensive per liter than the gasoline the old car used. By what percent will Elmer save money if he uses his new car instead of his old car for a long trip?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the old car use $1$ liter per kilometer (for simplicity), and let gasoline cost $\$1$ per liter.

**Old car cost per km:** $\$1$ per liter $\times$ $1$ liter/km $= \$1$/km.

**New car:** $50\%$ better efficiency means $\dfrac{2}{3}$ liters per km (it travels $1.5\times$ farther per liter, so uses $\dfrac{1}{1.5} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ liters per km).

Diesel costs $20\%$ more: $\$1.20$ per liter.

**New car cost per km:** $1.20 \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \$0.80$/km.

**Savings:** old cost $-$ new cost $= 1.00 - 0.80 = 0.20$.

**Percent saved:** $\dfrac{0.20}{1.00} = 20\%$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
