<Problem>
Call a positive integer $\textbf{monotonous}$ if it is a one-digit number or its digits, when read from left to right, form either a strictly increasing or a strictly decreasing sequence. For example, $3$, $23578$, and $987620$ are monotonous, but $88$, $7434$, and $23557$ are not. How many monotonous positive integers are there?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Every monotonous number corresponds uniquely to a nonempty subset of digits arranged in order — increasing or decreasing.

**Strictly increasing numbers** use digits from $\{1, 2, \ldots, 9\}$ (digit $0$ cannot begin a number, and in increasing order it would be the leading digit). Each nonempty subset of $\{1,\ldots,9\}$ gives exactly one increasing number, yielding $2^9 - 1 = 511$ numbers. This count includes all $9$ single-digit numbers $1$–$9$.

**Strictly decreasing numbers with $\geq 2$ digits** can use any subset of $\{0, 1, \ldots, 9\}$ of size $\geq 2$, since the largest digit always leads (no leading-zero issue). The count is $2^{10} - 1 - 10 = 1013$ numbers.

These two sets are disjoint: the $511$ increasing numbers include all $9$ single-digit numbers (which are not in the decreasing-multi-digit set), and no multi-digit number is both strictly increasing and strictly decreasing.

$$\text{Total} = 511 + 1013 = 1524.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
