<Problem>
Tina randomly selects two distinct numbers from the set $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, and Sergio randomly selects a number from the set $\{1, 2, \ldots, 10\}$. The probability that Sergio's number is larger than the sum of the two numbers chosen by Tina is
</Problem>

<Solution>
Tina's possible pairs and their sums:

| Pair | Sum |
|------|-----|
| $\{1,2\}$ | 3 |
| $\{1,3\}$ | 4 |
| $\{1,4\}$ | 5 |
| $\{1,5\}$ | 6 |
| $\{2,3\}$ | 5 |
| $\{2,4\}$ | 6 |
| $\{2,5\}$ | 7 |
| $\{3,4\}$ | 7 |
| $\{3,5\}$ | 8 |
| $\{4,5\}$ | 9 |

There are $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ equally likely pairs. For each pair, the number of values of Sergio's pick (from $\{1,\ldots,10\}$) that are strictly greater than the sum:

| Sum $s$ | Count of Sergio's values $> s$ |
|---------|-------------------------------|
| 3 | 7 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 5 |
| 5 | 5 |
| 6 | 4 |
| 6 | 4 |
| 7 | 3 |
| 7 | 3 |
| 8 | 2 |
| 9 | 1 |

Total favorable outcomes $= 7+6+5+5+4+4+3+3+2+1 = 40$.

Total outcomes $= 10 \times 10 = 100$.

Probability $= \dfrac{40}{100} = \dfrac{2}{5}$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
