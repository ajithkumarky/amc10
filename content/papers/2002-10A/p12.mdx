<Problem>
Mr. Earl E. Bird leaves his house for work at exactly 8:00 A.M. every morning. When he averages $40$ miles per hour, he arrives at his workplace three minutes late. When he averages $60$ miles per hour, he arrives three minutes early. At what average speed, in miles per hour, should Mr. Bird drive to arrive at his workplace precisely on time?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $d$ = distance (miles) and $t$ = on-time travel time (hours).

At $40$ mph he's $3$ min $= \frac{1}{20}$ hr late: $\dfrac{d}{40} = t + \dfrac{1}{20}$.

At $60$ mph he's $3$ min early: $\dfrac{d}{60} = t - \dfrac{1}{20}$.

Subtracting the second from the first:
$$\frac{d}{40} - \frac{d}{60} = \frac{2}{20} = \frac{1}{10}.$$
$$d\left(\frac{1}{40} - \frac{1}{60}\right) = \frac{1}{10} \implies d \cdot \frac{1}{120} = \frac{1}{10} \implies d = 12 \text{ miles}.$$

From the second equation: $t = \dfrac{12}{60} + \dfrac{1}{20} = \dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{1}{20} = \dfrac{5}{20} = \dfrac{1}{4}$ hr.

Required speed $= \dfrac{d}{t} = \dfrac{12}{1/4} = 48$ mph.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
