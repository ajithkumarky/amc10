<Problem>
The digits $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $5$, $6$, $7$, and $9$ are used to form four two-digit prime numbers, with each digit used exactly once. What is the sum of these four primes?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The digit $8$ is not available (it's excluded from the list). The eight digits $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9$ form four two-digit primes.

No two-digit prime ends in an even digit or $5$, so the units digits must come from $\{1, 3, 7, 9\}$ — and there are exactly four such digits available. The tens digits must be $\{2, 4, 5, 6\}$.

The sum of all eight digits is $1+2+3+4+5+6+7+9 = 37$.

If the tens digits are $\{2, 4, 5, 6\}$ and units digits are $\{1, 3, 7, 9\}$, the total sum of the four numbers is:

$$10(2+4+5+6) + (1+3+7+9) = 10(17) + 20 = 170 + 20 = 190.$$

We must verify that a valid arrangement exists. Try: $41, 23, 67, 59$ — check primality: $41$ (prime), $23$ (prime), $67$ (prime), $59$ (prime). Sum $= 41+23+67+59 = 190$. Valid.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
