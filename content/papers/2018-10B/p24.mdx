<Problem>
Let $ABCDEF$ be a regular hexagon with side length $1$. Denote by $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ the midpoints of sides $\overline{AB}$, $\overline{CD}$, and $\overline{EF}$, respectively. What is the area of the convex hexagon whose interior is the intersection of the interiors of $\triangle ACE$ and $\triangle XYZ$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place the regular hexagon with center at the origin and vertices $A=(1,0)$, $B=(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})$, $C=(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})$, $D=(-1,0)$, $E=(-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})$, $F=(\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})$.

The midpoints are $X=(\tfrac{3}{4},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4})$, $Y=(-\tfrac{3}{4},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4})$, $Z=(0,-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})$.

**Edge equations.** For $\triangle ACE$: edge $CE$ is $x=-\tfrac{1}{2}$; edge $AC$ is $x+\sqrt{3}y=1$; edge $EA$ is $x-\sqrt{3}y=1$. For $\triangle XYZ$: edge $XY$ is $y=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$; edge $XZ$ is $\sqrt{3}x-y=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$; edge $YZ$ is $\sqrt{3}x+y=-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

**Six intersection vertices.** Each edge of one triangle meets two edges of the other. Finding the six pairwise intersections:

- $AC \cap XY$: $x+\sqrt{3}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}=1 \Rightarrow x=\tfrac{1}{4}$. So $Q_1=(\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4})$.
- $CE \cap XY$: $x=-\tfrac{1}{2}$, $y=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$. So $Q_2=(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4})$.
- $CE \cap YZ$: $x=-\tfrac{1}{2}$, then $-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}+y=-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $y=0$. Thus $Q_3=(-\tfrac{1}{2},0)$.
- $EA \cap YZ$: from $x=1+\sqrt{3}y$ into $\sqrt{3}x+y=-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ gives $4y=-\tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $y=-\tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}$, $x=-\tfrac{1}{8}$. Thus $Q_4=(-\tfrac{1}{8},-\tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{8})$.
- $EA \cap XZ$: from $x=1+\sqrt{3}y$ into $\sqrt{3}x-y=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ gives $4\sqrt{3}y=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}-\sqrt{3}$, so $y=-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{8}$... wait, applying $120°$ rotation to $Q_1$ gives $Q_5=(\tfrac{1}{4},-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4})$ and rotating $Q_2$ gives $Q_6=(\tfrac{5}{8},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{8})$.

**Shoelace formula.** Ordering the vertices counterclockwise as $Q_2, Q_1, Q_6, Q_5, Q_4, Q_3$:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\bigl|x_1(y_2-y_6)+x_2(y_3-y_1)+x_3(y_4-y_2)+x_4(y_5-y_3)+x_5(y_6-y_4)+x_6(y_1-y_5)\bigr|$$

Substituting and collecting terms over a common denominator of $32$:

$$= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\!\left(\frac{4}{16}+\frac{1}{16}+\frac{10}{16}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot\frac{15}{16} = \frac{15\sqrt{3}}{32}.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
