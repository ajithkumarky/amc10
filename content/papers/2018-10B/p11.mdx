<Problem>
Which of the following expressions is never a prime number when $p$ is a prime number?
</Problem>

<Solution>
For any prime $p \neq 3$, we have $p \not\equiv 0 \pmod{3}$, so $p^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$. Since $26 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, this gives

$$p^2 + 26 \equiv 1 + 2 \equiv 0 \pmod{3} \quad \text{for every prime } p \neq 3.$$

For $p = 3$: $p^2 + 26 = 9 + 26 = 35 = 5 \times 7$, also not prime.

Since $p^2 + 26 \geq 4 + 26 = 30 > 3$, divisibility by $3$ makes it composite in every case. Thus $p^2 + 26$ is **never** prime.

The other expressions can each be prime: $p^2+16=41$ at $p=5$; $p^2+24=73$ at $p=7$; $p^2+46=71$ at $p=5$; $p^2+96=457$ at $p=19$. So only (C) is never prime.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
