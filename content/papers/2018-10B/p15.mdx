<Problem>
A closed box with a square base is to be wrapped with a square sheet of wrapping paper. The box is centered on the wrapping paper with the vertices of the base lying on the midlines of the square sheet of paper. The four corners of the wrapping paper are to be folded up over the sides and brought together to meet at the center of the top of the box, point $A$. The box has base length $w$ and height $h$. What is the area of the sheet of wrapping paper?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place the square paper axis-aligned with side length $s$, centered at the origin. The box is rotated $45°$ on the paper so that its base corners land on the paper's midlines (the axes). Each base corner is at distance $\frac{w\sqrt{2}}{2}$ from the center of the box.

Each corner of the paper lies along a diagonal at distance $\frac{s\sqrt{2}}{2}$ from the center. When that corner folds up to reach point $A$ (the center of the top of the box), the paper travels: from the paper corner inward along the diagonal to the nearest base corner of the box (distance $\frac{s\sqrt{2}}{2} - \frac{w\sqrt{2}}{2}$), then up the box side (distance $h$), then across the top to point $A$ (distance $\frac{w\sqrt{2}}{2}$). These three segments must unfold flat into the paper's diagonal, so:

$$\frac{s\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{w\sqrt{2}}{2} + h + \frac{w\sqrt{2}}{2} = w\sqrt{2} + h.$$

Thus $s = (w+h)\sqrt{2}$, and the area of the paper is $s^2 = 2(w+h)^2$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
