<Problem>
Andy and Betsy both live in Mathville. Andy leaves Mathville on his bicycle at $1{:}30$, traveling due north at a steady $8$ miles per hour. Betsy leaves on her bicycle from the same point at $2{:}30$, traveling due east at a steady $12$ miles per hour. At what time will they be exactly the same distance from their common starting point?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $t$ be the number of hours after $2{:}30$ (when Betsy leaves). At that moment:

- Andy has already been riding for $1$ hour, so his total riding time is $t + 1$ hours. His distance from the start is $8(t+1)$ miles.
- Betsy's distance from the start is $12t$ miles.

We want these distances to be equal:

$$8(t+1) = 12t$$
$$8t + 8 = 12t$$
$$8 = 4t$$
$$t = 2.$$

So they are equidistant $2$ hours after $2{:}30$, which is $4{:}30$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
