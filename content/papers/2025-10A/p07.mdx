<Problem>
Suppose $a$ and $b$ are real numbers. When the polynomial $x^3 + x^2 + ax + b$ is divided by $x - 1$, the remainder is $4$. When the polynomial is divided by $x - 2$, the remainder is $6$. What is $b - a$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
By the Remainder Theorem, the remainder when $p(x) = x^3 + x^2 + ax + b$ is divided by $x - c$ equals $p(c)$.

**From $x = 1$:**
$$p(1) = 1 + 1 + a + b = 4 \implies a + b = 2. \quad (1)$$

**From $x = 2$:**
$$p(2) = 8 + 4 + 2a + b = 6 \implies 2a + b = -6. \quad (2)$$

Subtract equation (1) from equation (2):
$$(2a + b) - (a + b) = -6 - 2$$
$$a = -8.$$

From equation (1): $b = 2 - a = 2 - (-8) = 10$.

Therefore:
$$b - a = 10 - (-8) = 18.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
