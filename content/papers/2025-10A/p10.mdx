<Problem>
A semicircle has diameter $\overline{AB}$ and chord $\overline{CD}$ of length $16$ parallel to $\overline{AB}$. A smaller semicircle with diameter on $\overline{AB}$ and tangent to $\overline{CD}$ is cut from the larger semicircle, as shown. What is the area of the resulting figure, shown shaded?

<Solution>
Let the large semicircle have radius $R$ and center $O$ at the origin, so $A = (-R, 0)$ and $B = (R, 0)$.

The chord $CD$ is parallel to $AB$ at height $h$. Since $C$ and $D$ lie on the large semicircle:

$$\text{Half-length of } CD = \sqrt{R^2 - h^2} = 8 \quad \Rightarrow \quad R^2 - h^2 = 64. \quad (*)$$

The small semicircle has its diameter on $\overline{AB}$ and is tangent to $\overline{CD}$ from below. If the small semicircle has radius $r$ and center at $(x_0, 0)$, then "tangent to $CD$" means the top of the small semicircle just touches $CD$, so $r = h$.

**Key insight — the area doesn't depend on knowing $R$ and $h$ separately.** The shaded area is:

$$\text{Shaded} = \text{Large semicircle} - \text{Small semicircle}.$$

$$= \frac{\pi R^2}{2} - \frac{\pi r^2}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2}(R^2 - r^2) = \frac{\pi}{2}(R^2 - h^2) = \frac{\pi}{2} \cdot 64 = 32\pi.$$

(We used $r = h$ and the relation $(*)$.)

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
