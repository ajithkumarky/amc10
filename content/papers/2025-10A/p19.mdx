<Problem>
An array of numbers is constructed beginning with the numbers $-1,\ 3,\ 1$ in the top row. Each adjacent pair of numbers is summed to produce a number in the next row. Each row begins and ends with $-1$ and $1$, respectively.

$$\begin{array}{ccccc} -1 & 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 & 4 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 & 6 & 5 & 1 \end{array}$$

If the process continues, one of the rows will sum to $12{,}288$. In that row, what is the third number from the left?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Understanding the structure.** The top row is $(-1, 3, 1)$. Each new row is formed by inserting the sum of each adjacent pair; the endpoints are always fixed at $-1$ (left) and $1$ (right). 

Observe that row $n$ (starting with row $1$ as the top row) has $n+2$ entries. Let's call the entries of row $n$: $a_{n,0}, a_{n,1}, \ldots, a_{n,n+1}$ where $a_{n,0} = -1$ and $a_{n,n+1} = 1$.

The "middle" entries follow Pascal's triangle. Notice:
- Row 1: $-1,\ 3,\ 1$
- Row 2: $-1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 1$
- Row 3: $-1,\ 1,\ 6,\ 5,\ 1$

The interior entry $a_{n,j}$ (for $1 \le j \le n$) satisfies the recurrence $a_{n,j} = a_{n-1,j-1} + a_{n-1,j}$ (Pascal-like). Starting values: $a_{1,1} = 3$.

The general formula (by induction using Pascal's triangle weights from rows starting at $(-1, 3, 1)$):

A row-$n$ entry at position $j$ (interior) is:
$$a_{n,j} = (-1)\binom{n}{j} + 3\binom{n}{j-1} + 1\binom{n}{j-2}$$
where $\binom{n}{-1} = \binom{n}{-2} = 0$.

(This is because the three starting values $-1, 3, 1$ each propagate via Pascal's triangle weights $\binom{n-1}{j-1}$, but shifted positions.)

More carefully: treat the row-1 entries as seeds at positions 0, 1, 2. Entry $a_{n,j}$ in row $n$ (0-indexed positions $0$ through $n+1$) gets contribution $\binom{n-1}{j-k}$ from seed at position $k$ (for applicable $k$):

$$a_{n,j} = (-1)\binom{n-1}{j} + 3\binom{n-1}{j-1} + 1\binom{n-1}{j-2}.$$

**Row sum.** The sum of row $n$ is:
$$S_n = \sum_{j=0}^{n+1} a_{n,j}.$$

From the data: $S_1 = 3$, $S_2 = 6$, $S_3 = 12$. The sums double each row: $S_n = 3 \cdot 2^{n-1}$.

Set $S_n = 12288$: $3 \cdot 2^{n-1} = 12288 \Rightarrow 2^{n-1} = 4096 = 2^{12} \Rightarrow n = 13$.

**Third entry from the left in row 13** is $a_{13, 2}$:
$$a_{13,2} = (-1)\binom{12}{2} + 3\binom{12}{1} + 1\binom{12}{0} = (-1)(66) + 3(12) + 1(1) = -66 + 36 + 1 = -29.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
