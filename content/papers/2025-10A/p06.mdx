<Problem>
In an equilateral triangle each interior angle is trisected by a pair of rays. The intersection of the interiors of the middle $20^\circ$-angle at each vertex is the interior of a convex hexagon. What is the degree measure of the smallest angle of this hexagon?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Each interior angle of the equilateral triangle is $60^\circ$. Trisecting gives three $20^\circ$ sectors at each vertex. The "middle $20^\circ$-angle" at each vertex means the two inner trisecting rays (those that bound the central $20^\circ$ sector) create a hexagon inside the triangle.

The hexagon has vertices where trisecting rays from different vertices of the triangle intersect. Each vertex of the hexagon lies at the intersection of two trisecting rays from two different triangle vertices.

Label the triangle vertices $P$, $Q$, $R$. At each vertex, the three $20^\circ$ sectors are: outer-left, middle, outer-right. The middle sector is bounded by two rays; the hexagon is bounded by one ray from each of the six rays (two per vertex).

At each vertex of the hexagon, two of the six rays meet. Consider a vertex of the hexagon formed by:
- The inner-right ray from $P$ (making $20^\circ$ with side $PQ$, measuring from inside)
- The inner-left ray from $Q$ (making $20^\circ$ with side $PQ$, measuring from inside)

In triangle $PQ$-intersection: These two rays form a triangle with side $PQ$. The angle at $P$ from side $PQ$ is $20^\circ$, and the angle at $Q$ from side $PQ$ is $20^\circ$. So the angle at the intersection point (hexagon vertex) is $180^\circ - 20^\circ - 20^\circ = 140^\circ$.

For a vertex formed by intersecting rays from two vertices that are NOT adjacent along the same side: Consider the ray from $P$ (inner-right) and ray from $R$ (inner-left). The ray from $P$ goes at $20^\circ + 20^\circ = 40^\circ$ from side $PR$ (middle of the $60^\circ$ at $P$), and the ray from $R$ goes at $40^\circ$ from side $PR$ at $R$. The angle at intersection: $180^\circ - 40^\circ - 40^\circ = 100^\circ$.

Wait, let me be more careful. The two inner trisecting rays at $P$ make angles of $20^\circ$ and $40^\circ$ with side $PQ$ (within the triangle). The ray closer to $PQ$ makes angle $20^\circ$; the ray closer to $PR$ makes angle $40^\circ$ from $PQ$ (or equivalently $20^\circ$ from $PR$).

The hexagon vertices alternate between two types:
1. Intersection of the ray at $20^\circ$ from $PQ$ at $P$, and the ray at $20^\circ$ from $PQ$ at $Q$: angle $= 180^\circ - 20^\circ - 20^\circ = 140^\circ$.
2. Intersection of the ray at $40^\circ$ from $PQ$ at $P$ (i.e., $20^\circ$ from $PR$), and the ray at $40^\circ$ from $QR$ at $Q$ (i.e., $20^\circ$ from $QR$): Using triangle $PQX$ where $X$ is on $QR$-direction... 

Actually the hexagon alternates between vertices with angles $140^\circ$ and $100^\circ$. Since a convex hexagon has angle sum $720^\circ$, and the hexagon has 3-fold symmetry with alternating angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$:
$$3\alpha + 3\beta = 720^\circ \Rightarrow \alpha + \beta = 240^\circ.$$

We found $\alpha = 140^\circ$ for one type, giving $\beta = 100^\circ$. The smallest angle is $100^\circ$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
