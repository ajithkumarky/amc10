<Problem>
A pair of fair $6$-sided dice is rolled $n$ times. What is the least value of $n$ such that the probability that the sum of the numbers face up on a roll equals $7$ at least once is greater than $\dfrac{1}{2}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The probability that a single roll of two dice gives a sum of 7 is $\dfrac{6}{36} = \dfrac{1}{6}$ (the six pairs are $(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)$).

It's easier to compute the complement: the probability that no roll gives a sum of 7 in $n$ rolls is $\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^n$.

We want:
$$1 - \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^n > \frac{1}{2} \implies \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^n < \frac{1}{2}.$$

Compute:
- $n=1$: $(5/6)^1 = 0.833 > 1/2$
- $n=2$: $(5/6)^2 = 25/36 \approx 0.694 > 1/2$
- $n=3$: $(5/6)^3 = 125/216 \approx 0.579 > 1/2$
- $n=4$: $(5/6)^4 = 625/1296 \approx 0.482 < 1/2$ ✓

The smallest $n$ for which $(5/6)^n < 1/2$ is $n = 4$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
