<Problem>
Ant Amelia starts on the number line at $0$ and crawls in the following manner. For $n=1,2,3,$ Amelia chooses a time duration $t_n$ and an increment $x_n$ independently and uniformly at random from the interval $(0,1).$ During the $n$th step of the process, Amelia moves $x_n$ units in the positive direction, using up $t_n$ minutes. If the total elapsed time has exceeded $1$ minute during the $n$th step, she stops at the end of that step; otherwise, she continues with the next step, taking at most $3$ steps in all. What is the probability that Amelia's position when she stops will be greater than $1$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Amelia takes steps until the cumulative time first exceeds 1. She takes at most 3 steps. Let $T_n = t_1 + \cdots + t_n$ be the cumulative time after step $n$.

- She stops after step 1 if $T_1 > 1$, i.e., $t_1 > 1$. But $t_1 \in (0,1)$, so $T_1 < 1$ always. She always takes at least 2 steps.
- She stops after step 2 if $T_2 > 1$, i.e., $t_1 + t_2 > 1$.
- She stops after step 3 otherwise (if $T_2 \leq 1$; she always stops by step 3 since $T_3 > 0 + 0 + 0$ but with probability 1 she may have $T_3 \leq 1$ for some configurations — actually she stops at the end of step 3 regardless of whether $T_3 > 1$).

Wait: re-reading: "If the total elapsed time has exceeded 1 minute during the $n$th step, she stops at the end of that step." Since $T_1 = t_1 < 1$ always (as $t_1 < 1$), she never stops after step 1. She may stop after step 2 (if $T_2 > 1$) or she continues to step 3 and stops there regardless.

**Two scenarios:**

**Scenario A: Stops after step 2** ($t_1 + t_2 > 1$). Position = $x_1 + x_2$. Need $x_1 + x_2 > 1$.

**Scenario B: Stops after step 3** ($t_1 + t_2 \leq 1$). Position = $x_1 + x_2 + x_3$. Need $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 > 1$.

All six variables $t_1, t_2, t_3, x_1, x_2, x_3$ are independent uniform on $(0,1)$. Also, $t_i$ and $x_i$ are independent of each other.

Let $T = t_1+t_2$ and $X = x_1+x_2$ (independent of $T$), and $x_3$ independent of all.

$P(\text{stop after step 2}) = P(T > 1) = P(t_1+t_2>1) = 1/2$.

$P(\text{stop after step 3}) = 1/2$.

**Probability position $> 1$ | Scenario A:**
$P(x_1+x_2 > 1) = 1/2$.

Note: $x_1, x_2$ are independent of $t_1, t_2$, so given that we stop after step 2 (i.e., $T>1$), the distribution of $x_1+x_2$ is unchanged (independent). So:

$P(\text{pos} > 1 \mid \text{Scenario A}) = P(x_1+x_2>1) = 1/2$.

**Probability position $> 1$ | Scenario B:**
$P(x_1+x_2+x_3 > 1 \mid t_1+t_2 \leq 1)$.

Again $x_i$ and $t_i$ are independent, so:
$P(x_1+x_2+x_3>1 \mid t_1+t_2\leq 1) = P(x_1+x_2+x_3>1)$.

For three uniform$(0,1)$ random variables: $P(U_1+U_2+U_3>1) = 1 - P(U_1+U_2+U_3\leq 1) = 1 - \frac{1}{6} \cdot 3! \cdot \text{Vol}(U_1+U_2+U_3\leq 1, U_i>0)$.

The volume of $\{(u_1,u_2,u_3) \in (0,1)^3 : u_1+u_2+u_3 \leq 1\}$ is $\frac{1}{6}$ (it's a simplex with volume $1/3!$).

So $P(U_1+U_2+U_3 \leq 1) = 1/6$, and $P(U_1+U_2+U_3>1) = 5/6$.

**Total probability:**

$$P(\text{pos}>1) = P(A) \cdot P(\text{pos}>1|A) + P(B) \cdot P(\text{pos}>1|B)$$
$$= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{6} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{5}{12} = \frac{3}{12} + \frac{5}{12} = \frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
