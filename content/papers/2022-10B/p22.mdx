<Problem>
Let $S$ be the set of circles in the coordinate plane that are tangent to each of the three circles with equations $x^{2}+y^{2}=4$, $x^{2}+y^{2}=64$, and $(x-5)^{2}+y^{2}=3$. What is the sum of the areas of all circles in $S$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Label the three given circles:
- $\omega_1$: center $O_1 = (0,0)$, radius $r_1 = 2$.
- $\omega_2$: center $O_2 = (0,0)$, radius $r_2 = 8$.
- $\omega_3$: center $O_3 = (5,0)$, radius $r_3 = \sqrt{3}$.

Note $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ are concentric, and $\omega_3$ is inside $\omega_2$ (since $5 + \sqrt{3} < 8$) and outside $\omega_1$ (since $5 - \sqrt{3} > 2$, as $5 - 1.73 \approx 3.27 > 2$).

Let the unknown circle in $S$ have center $(h,k)$ and radius $\rho > 0$.

**Tangency with $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ (concentric circles):**

A circle tangent to both $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ has its center on the $x$-axis... actually not necessarily. Let's think about this more carefully.

For a circle to be internally or externally tangent to $\omega_1$ (radius 2, center $O$) and also tangent to $\omega_2$ (radius 8, center $O$):

Distance from $(h,k)$ to origin: let $d = \sqrt{h^2+k^2}$.

The circle is tangent to $\omega_1$: $d = \rho + 2$ (external) or $d = |\rho - 2|$ (internal). 
The circle is tangent to $\omega_2$: $d = \rho + 8$ (external) or $d = |\rho - 8|$ (internal).

**Case analysis on types of tangency with $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$:**

Since $\omega_1 \subset \omega_2$ (they're concentric with $r_1 < r_2$), and a circle tangent to both:

**Sub-case A: Externally tangent to $\omega_1$, internally tangent to $\omega_2$ (inside $\omega_2$):**
$d = \rho + 2$ and $\rho + d = 8$ (the unknown circle is inside $\omega_2$, so $d + \rho = 8$).
$d = 8 - \rho$ and $d = \rho + 2$: $8 - \rho = \rho + 2 \Rightarrow \rho = 3$, $d = 5$.
So all such circles have $\rho = 3$ and center at distance 5 from origin.

**Sub-case B: Internally tangent to $\omega_1$ (surrounds $\omega_1$), internally tangent to $\omega_2$ (inside $\omega_2$):**
$d = \rho - 2$ (unknown circle surrounds $\omega_1$) and $d = 8 - \rho$ (inside $\omega_2$).
$\rho - 2 = 8 - \rho \Rightarrow \rho = 5$, $d = 3$.
So all such circles have $\rho = 5$, center at distance 3 from origin.

**Sub-case C: Internally tangent to $\omega_2$ from outside (unknown circle surrounds $\omega_2$):**
$d = \rho - 8$ and either $d = \rho + 2$ (ext. tangent to $\omega_1$) or $d = \rho - 2$ (surrounds $\omega_1$).
- $\rho - 8 = \rho + 2$: $-8 = 2$, impossible.
- $\rho - 8 = \rho - 2$: $-8 = -2$, impossible.

**Sub-case D: Externally tangent to $\omega_2$ (outside both):**
$d = \rho + 8$ and $d = \rho + 2$ or $d = \rho - 2$.
- $\rho + 8 = \rho + 2$: impossible.
- $\rho + 8 = \rho - 2$: impossible.

So circles tangent to both $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ must be in sub-case A ($\rho=3$, $d=5$) or sub-case B ($\rho=5$, $d=3$).

**Now apply tangency with $\omega_3$:** center $(5,0)$, radius $\sqrt{3}$.

Distance from $(h,k)$ to $(5,0)$: $\delta = \sqrt{(h-5)^2 + k^2}$.

**Sub-case A: $\rho = 3$, $h^2+k^2 = 25$.** Need $\delta = 3 + \sqrt{3}$ (external) or $\delta = |3 - \sqrt{3}| = 3 - \sqrt{3}$ (internal, since $\rho > r_3$).

- $\delta = 3 + \sqrt{3}$: $\sqrt{(h-5)^2+k^2} = 3+\sqrt{3}$.

Using $h^2+k^2 = 25$: $(h-5)^2+k^2 = h^2-10h+25+k^2 = 50-10h$.

$50 - 10h = (3+\sqrt{3})^2 = 9 + 6\sqrt{3} + 3 = 12 + 6\sqrt{3}$.

$10h = 50 - 12 - 6\sqrt{3} = 38 - 6\sqrt{3}$, so $h = \dfrac{38-6\sqrt{3}}{10} = \dfrac{19-3\sqrt{3}}{5}$.

$k^2 = 25 - h^2$. We need $k^2 \geq 0$. $h \approx \dfrac{19 - 5.196}{5} \approx \dfrac{13.804}{5} \approx 2.76$. $h^2 \approx 7.62 < 25$ ✓. Two solutions (with $k = \pm\sqrt{25-h^2}$).

- $\delta = 3 - \sqrt{3}$: $50 - 10h = (3-\sqrt{3})^2 = 9 - 6\sqrt{3} + 3 = 12 - 6\sqrt{3}$.

$10h = 38 + 6\sqrt{3}$, $h = \dfrac{19+3\sqrt{3}}{5} \approx \dfrac{19+5.196}{5} \approx 4.84$. $h^2 \approx 23.4 < 25$ ✓. Two solutions.

So sub-case A yields **4 circles** total (2 from each tangency type), all with $\rho = 3$.

**Sub-case B: $\rho = 5$, $h^2+k^2 = 9$.** Need $\delta = 5 + \sqrt{3}$ or $\delta = 5 - \sqrt{3}$.

$\delta^2 = 50 - 10h$.

- $\delta = 5+\sqrt{3}$: $(5+\sqrt{3})^2 = 25+10\sqrt{3}+3 = 28+10\sqrt{3}$.

$50-10h = 28+10\sqrt{3}$, $h = \dfrac{22-10\sqrt{3}}{10} = \dfrac{11-5\sqrt{3}}{5} \approx \dfrac{11-8.66}{5} \approx 0.47$. $h^2 \approx 0.22 < 9$ ✓. Two solutions.

- $\delta = 5-\sqrt{3}$: $(5-\sqrt{3})^2 = 25-10\sqrt{3}+3 = 28-10\sqrt{3}$.

$50-10h = 28-10\sqrt{3}$, $h = \dfrac{22+10\sqrt{3}}{10} = \dfrac{11+5\sqrt{3}}{5} \approx \dfrac{11+8.66}{5} \approx 3.93$. $h^2 \approx 15.4 > 9$. $k^2 = 9 - h^2 < 0$: impossible.

So sub-case B yields **2 circles** with $\rho = 5$, and **1 possibility** with $\delta = 5+\sqrt{3}$ giving 2 circles. Wait: 2 circles (from the one valid $\delta$).

Sub-case B yields $\boxed{2}$ circles (with $k = \pm\sqrt{9-h^2}$), all with $\rho = 5$.

**Total: 4 + 2 = 6 circles in $S$.**

**Sum of areas:**
- 4 circles with $\rho = 3$: area $= 9\pi$ each, total $= 36\pi$.
- 2 circles with $\rho = 5$: area $= 25\pi$ each, total $= 50\pi$.

Sum $= 36\pi + 50\pi = 86\pi$.
