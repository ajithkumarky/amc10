<Problem>
Let $S$ be the set of circles in the coordinate plane that are tangent to each of the three circles with equations $x^{2}+y^{2}=4$, $x^{2}+y^{2}=64$, and $(x-5)^{2}+y^{2}=3$. What is the sum of the areas of all circles in $S$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Label the three given circles:
- $\omega_1$: center $(0,0)$, radius $2$.
- $\omega_2$: center $(0,0)$, radius $8$.
- $\omega_3$: center $(5,0)$, radius $\sqrt{3}$.

Note $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$ are concentric, $\omega_3$ lies between them (since $5-\sqrt{3}>2$ and $5+\sqrt{3}<8$).

Let an unknown circle have center $(h,k)$, radius $\rho$, and set $d=\sqrt{h^2+k^2}$.

**Tangency conditions with the concentric pair $\omega_1$, $\omega_2$.**

Since the only nonzero scalar multiples of a positive radius that stay positive are scale-factor $\pm 1$, the four tangency-type combinations reduce to exactly two consistent cases:

- **Case A** (externally tangent to $\omega_1$, internally tangent inside $\omega_2$): $d = \rho+2$ and $d+\rho = 8$. Solving: $\rho = 3$, $d = 5$.
- **Case B** (internally tangent surrounding $\omega_1$, internally tangent inside $\omega_2$): $d = \rho-2$ and $d = 8-\rho$. Solving: $\rho = 5$, $d = 3$.

All other sign combinations lead to contradictions ($\rho+8=\rho+2$, etc.).

**Applying tangency with $\omega_3$.** Let $\delta = \sqrt{(h-5)^2+k^2}$. Since $d^2 = h^2+k^2$, we have $\delta^2 = d^2 - 10h + 25$.

The unknown circle is tangent to $\omega_3$ when $\delta = \rho + \sqrt{3}$ (external) or $\delta = \rho - \sqrt{3}$ (unknown contains $\omega_3$, valid since $\rho > \sqrt{3}$ in both cases).

**Case A ($\rho=3$, $d^2=25$):** $\delta^2 = 50-10h$.

- $\delta = 3+\sqrt{3}$: $50-10h = 12+6\sqrt{3}$, so $h = \dfrac{19-3\sqrt{3}}{5} \approx 2.76$. Then $k^2 = 25-h^2 > 0$. **Two circles.**
- $\delta = 3-\sqrt{3}$: $50-10h = 12-6\sqrt{3}$, so $h = \dfrac{19+3\sqrt{3}}{5} \approx 4.84$. Then $k^2 = 25-h^2 > 0$. **Two circles.**

Case A yields **4 circles**, each with $\rho=3$.

**Case B ($\rho=5$, $d^2=9$):** $\delta^2 = 34-10h$.

- $\delta = 5+\sqrt{3}$: $34-10h = 28+10\sqrt{3}$, so $h = \dfrac{3-5\sqrt{3}}{5} \approx -1.13$. Then $k^2 = 9-h^2 > 0$. **Two circles.**
- $\delta = 5-\sqrt{3}$: $34-10h = 28-10\sqrt{3}$, so $h = \dfrac{3+5\sqrt{3}}{5} \approx 2.33$. Then $k^2 = 9-h^2 > 0$. **Two circles.**

Case B yields **4 circles**, each with $\rho=5$.

**Sum of areas:**

$$4\cdot\pi(3^2) + 4\cdot\pi(5^2) = 36\pi + 100\pi = 136\pi.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
