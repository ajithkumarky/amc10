<Problem>
For how many values of the constant $k$ will the polynomial $x^2 + kx + 36$ have two distinct integer roots?
</Problem>

<Solution>
If the polynomial $x^2 + kx + 36$ has integer roots $r$ and $s$, then by Vieta's formulas:
$$r + s = -k \quad \text{and} \quad rs = 36.$$

We need $r \neq s$ and both $r, s$ are integers with $rs = 36$.

List all pairs of distinct integers whose product is 36:

| $r$ | $s$ | $rs$ |
|-----|-----|------|
| 1 | 36 | 36 |
| 2 | 18 | 36 |
| 3 | 12 | 36 |
| 4 | 9 | 36 |
| 6 | — | (would need $s=6$, but then $r=s$, not distinct) |
| $-1$ | $-36$ | 36 |
| $-2$ | $-18$ | 36 |
| $-3$ | $-12$ | 36 |
| $-4$ | $-9$ | 36 |

Each of these 8 unordered pairs gives a distinct value of $k = -(r+s)$:
$$k = -(1+36) = -37, \quad -(2+18) = -20, \quad -(3+12) = -15, \quad -(4+9) = -13,$$
$$k = -(-1-36) = 37, \quad -(-2-18) = 20, \quad -(-3-12) = 15, \quad -(-4-9) = 13.$$

That's $8$ distinct values of $k$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
