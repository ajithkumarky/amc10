<Problem>
How many of the first ten numbers of the sequence $121, 11211, 1112111, \ldots$ are prime numbers?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The $n$th term of the sequence consists of $n$ ones, then a two, then $n$ ones. So:
- Term 1: $121 = 11 \times 11$
- Term 2: $11211 = 3 \times 3737 = 3 \times 37 \times 101$

Let's find a general factoring pattern. The $n$th term can be written as:
$$a_n = \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n} \cdot 10^{n+1} + 2 \cdot 10^n + \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n} = \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n}(10^{n+1} + 1) + 2 \cdot 10^n.$$

A cleaner approach: notice that
$$a_n = \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n+1} \cdot 10^n + \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n+1} - \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n+1} + \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n} \cdot 10^n + 2 \cdot 10^n + \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n}.$$

Actually, observe that $a_n = \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{2n+1} + 10^n = R_{2n+1} + 10^n$, where $R_k = \dfrac{10^k - 1}{9}$ is the repunit with $k$ ones.

We can also write $a_n = (10^n + 1) \cdot \underbrace{11\cdots1}_{n+1}$. Check: for $n=1$, $(10+1)(11) = 11 \times 11 = 121$. For $n=2$, $(100+1)(111) = 101 \times 111 = 11211$. Yes!

So $a_n = (10^n + 1) \cdot R_{n+1}$, where $R_{n+1} \geq 11 > 1$ and $10^n + 1 \geq 11 > 1$ for all $n \geq 1$.

This means every term is a product of two integers both greater than 1, so none of the first ten terms (or any term) is prime.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
