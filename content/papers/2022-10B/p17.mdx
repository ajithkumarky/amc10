<Problem>
One of the following numbers is not divisible by any prime number less than $10.$ Which is it?

$\textbf{(A) } 2^{606}-1 \qquad\textbf{(B) } 2^{606}+1 \qquad\textbf{(C) } 2^{607}-1 \qquad\textbf{(D) } 2^{607}+1\qquad\textbf{(E) } 2^{607}+3^{607}$
</Problem>

<Solution>
The primes less than $10$ are $2, 3, 5, 7$. All five answer choices are odd (powers of $2$ plus or minus odd numbers), so none is divisible by $2$. We check divisibility by $3$, $5$, and $7$ for each option.

**Divisibility by 3:** Note $2 \equiv -1 \pmod{3}$, so $2^n \equiv (-1)^n \pmod{3}$.
- (A) $2^{606} - 1 \equiv (-1)^{606} - 1 = 1 - 1 = 0 \pmod{3}$ → divisible by 3.
- (B) $2^{606} + 1 \equiv 1 + 1 = 2 \pmod{3}$ → not divisible by 3.
- (C) $2^{607} - 1 \equiv (-1)^{607} - 1 = -1 - 1 = -2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ → not divisible by 3.
- (D) $2^{607} + 1 \equiv -1 + 1 = 0 \pmod{3}$ → divisible by 3.
- (E) $2^{607} + 3^{607} \equiv (-1)^{607} + 0 = -1 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ → not divisible by 3.

So (A) and (D) are out. We focus on (B), (C), (E).

**Divisibility by 5:** The order of $2 \pmod 5$ is $4$ (since $2^4 = 16 \equiv 1$). So $2^n \pmod 5$ cycles with period 4: $2,4,3,1,2,4,3,1,\ldots$
- $606 = 4 \cdot 151 + 2$, so $2^{606} \equiv 2^2 = 4 \pmod 5$.
- $607 = 4 \cdot 151 + 3$, so $2^{607} \equiv 2^3 = 3 \pmod 5$.
- $3^{607} \pmod 5$: order of $3 \pmod 5$ is $4$ ($3^4 = 81 \equiv 1$). $607 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, so $3^{607} \equiv 3^3 = 27 \equiv 2 \pmod 5$.

Checking:
- (B) $2^{606} + 1 \equiv 4 + 1 = 5 \equiv 0 \pmod 5$ → divisible by 5.
- (C) $2^{607} - 1 \equiv 3 - 1 = 2 \pmod 5$ → not divisible by 5.
- (E) $2^{607} + 3^{607} \equiv 3 + 2 = 5 \equiv 0 \pmod 5$ → divisible by 5.

So (B) and (E) are eliminated. Only **(C)** remains. Let's confirm (C) is not divisible by 7.

**Divisibility by 7:** The order of $2 \pmod 7$ is $3$ ($2^3 = 8 \equiv 1$). $607 = 3 \cdot 202 + 1$, so $2^{607} \equiv 2^1 = 2 \pmod 7$. Thus $2^{607} - 1 \equiv 2 - 1 = 1 \pmod 7$ → not divisible by 7. ✓

So $2^{607} - 1$ is not divisible by $2, 3, 5,$ or $7$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
