<Problem>
Jason rolls three fair standard six-sided dice. Then he looks at the rolls and chooses a subset of the dice (possibly empty, possibly all three dice) to reroll. After rerolling, he wins if and only if the sum of the numbers face up on the three dice is exactly $7$. Jason always plays to optimize his chances of winning. What is the probability that he chooses to reroll exactly two of the dice?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Jason wants to maximize the probability that the final sum is 7. He will choose the reroll strategy that gives the highest probability of success given what he currently sees.

**Setup.** After the initial roll, Jason sees values $(a, b, c)$. He can keep any subset and reroll the rest. Let $S$ denote the current sum and the kept dice sum $K$. If he keeps a subset summing to $k$ and rerolls $r$ dice, his win probability is $P(\text{sum of } r \text{ dice} = 7 - k)$.

Let $p(r, s)$ = probability that $r$ fair dice sum to $s$.

**Precompute win probabilities by kept sum $k$:**

- Keep all 3 (reroll 0): win iff current sum is 7. Probability = 1 if sum = 7, else 0.
- Keep 2 dice (reroll 1): need the 1 die to show $7 - k$. Win prob = $\frac{1}{6}$ if $1 \le 7-k \le 6$ (i.e., $1 \le k \le 6$), else 0.
- Keep 1 die (reroll 2): need 2 dice to sum to $7 - k$. Win prob = $p(2, 7-k)$.
- Keep none (reroll all 3): need 3 dice to sum to 7. Win prob = $p(3, 7) = \frac{15}{216} = \frac{5}{72}$.

$p(2, s)$ for $s = 2, \ldots, 12$: number of ways = $(s-1)$ for $s \le 7$ and $(13-s)$ for $s \ge 7$ (divided by 36).

For keep-1 scenarios, kept value is $k \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$, need $7 - k \in \{1, \ldots, 6\}$. $p(2, 7-k) = \frac{7-k-1}{36} = \frac{6-k}{36}$ for $k \le 5$ (since $7-k \ge 2$), and $p(2, 6) = \frac{5}{36}$ for $k=1$; actually $p(2,s) = (s-1)/36$ for $s \le 7$. So $p(2, 7-k) = (6-k)/36$.

- $k=1$: $p(2,6) = 5/36$
- $k=2$: $p(2,5) = 4/36$
- $k=3$: $p(2,4) = 3/36$
- $k=4$: $p(2,3) = 2/36$
- $k=5$: $p(2,2) = 1/36$
- $k=6$: $p(2,1) = 0$

For keep-2, the best win probability is $1/6$ (keep 2 dice summing to $k \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$).

**Jason's optimal strategy:** Given the initial roll $(a,b,c)$, Jason compares:
- $P_0 = \mathbf{1}[\text{sum} = 7]$
- Best $P_{\text{keep 2}} = \max_{i,j} \frac{1}{6} \cdot \mathbf{1}[1 \le 7 - (d_i + d_j) \le 6]$, where $d_i + d_j \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$
- Best $P_{\text{keep 1}} = \max_i \frac{6 - d_i}{36}$, achieved by keeping the smallest die (value 1 is best: $5/36$)
- $P_{\text{keep 0}} = 5/72 \approx 0.0694$

Comparing: $1/6 \approx 0.167 > 5/36 \approx 0.139 > 5/72 \approx 0.069$.

So if Jason can keep 2 dice summing to $k \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$, he prefers rerolling 1 die over rerolling 2, since $1/6 > (6-k)/36$.

Jason rerolls exactly 2 dice when:
- The optimal action is to keep exactly 1 die (and reroll 2), meaning there exists a die to keep such that keeping it gives a higher win probability than all other options.

Specifically, Jason rerolls 2 when the best strategy is "keep 1 die" — which happens when:
1. The current sum is not 7 (otherwise keep all),
2. No pair of dice sums to a value in $\{1,\ldots,6\}$ (otherwise keep a pair giving higher win prob),
3. Keeping 1 die gives higher probability than rerolling all 3.

Condition 2: a pair sums to $\le 6$ iff the pair has sum $\le 6$. The minimum sum of a pair of standard dice is $1+1=2$. So any pair with both dice $\le 5$ and sum $\le 6$ qualifies.

Actually let me reconsider: Jason can keep any subset. He rerolls exactly 2 when the optimal number of rerolls is 2.

Keep 2 (reroll 1) wins with prob $1/6$ when there's a pair summing to $k \in \{1,\ldots,6\}$.
- Minimum pair sum: $1+1 = 2$, maximum that qualifies: pairs with sum $\le 6$.

So Jason would reroll just 1 die (keep 2) whenever some pair sums to $\le 6$. Jason rerolls exactly 2 dice only when NO pair of dice sums to $\le 6$, i.e., every pair sums to $\ge 7$.

Every pair sums to $\ge 7$ means: for all $i \ne j$, $d_i + d_j \ge 7$.

For three dice values $a \le b \le c$, the minimum pair sum is $a + b \ge 7$, so $a + b \ge 7$. Since $a \ge 1$ and $b \ge 1$, and both are at most 6, $a + b \ge 7$ means at minimum $a \ge 1, b \ge 6$ (i.e., $a = 1, b = 6$) or larger. For $a + b \ge 7$ with $a \le b$: if $a = 1$, then $b \ge 6$, so $b = 6$; if $a = 2$, $b \ge 5$; if $a = 3$, $b \ge 4$; if $a = 4$, $b \ge 3$, so $b \ge 4$; etc. Actually with $a \le b$: $(a,b)$ can be $(1,6),(2,5),(2,6),(3,4),(3,5),(3,6),(4,4),(4,5),(4,6),(5,5),(5,6),(6,6)$.

So we need $a + b \ge 7$ where $a = \min$ of the three dice. This means even the two smallest values must sum to $\ge 7$.

Also, we still need to check: given every pair sums to $\ge 7$, is rerolling 2 dice better than rerolling all 3 or keeping all? If the current sum $\ne 7$ (reroll 0 wins with prob 0) and no pair is $\le 6$, then the best keep-1 choice gives win prob at most $(6-1)/36 = 5/36$ (keeping a die showing 1), which is $> 5/72$, so keep-1 (reroll 2) beats reroll-all.

But wait — if the current sum is 7, Jason keeps all (rerolls 0). Let's check: if all pairs sum to $\ge 7$ and the total sum is 7, is that possible? $a + b + c = 7$ and $a + b \ge 7$ implies $c \le 0$, which is impossible. So if all pairs sum to $\ge 7$, the sum cannot be 7.

**So Jason rerolls exactly 2 dice iff all three pairs sum to $\ge 7$, i.e., the two smallest values sum to $\ge 7$.**

(This also assumes keeping 1 die beats rerolling all 3, which we showed above as long as there exists a die showing $\le 5$. If all three dice show 6, then $a + b = 12 \ge 7$, but keeping a 6 gives $p(2, 1) = 0$; Jason would then reroll all 3 with prob $5/72$. Actually keeping die = 6 means need remaining two to sum to 1, which is impossible. So for $(6,6,6)$, Jason should reroll all 3.)

Hmm, let me reconsider $(6,6,6)$: pairs sum $= 12 \ge 7$, but keeping any single 6 gives 0 win probability, while rerolling all 3 gives $5/72 > 0$. So Jason rerolls all 3, NOT exactly 2.

**Revised condition:** Jason rerolls exactly 2 when: all pairs sum to $\ge 7$ AND there exists a die value $d$ such that keeping that die gives win probability $> 5/72$, i.e., $(6-d)/36 > 5/72$, i.e., $6-d > 5/2$, i.e., $d < 3.5$, i.e., $d \le 3$.

So at least one die must be $\le 3$ for keep-1 to beat reroll-all.

Also check whether keeping the best single die is better than a 2-die keep when all pair sums $\ge 7$: we established there's no pair with sum $\le 6$, so no 2-die keep gives win prob $> 0$ for rerolling 1... wait, keep 2 means need the 1 remaining die to show $7 - k$ where $k$ is the pair sum. If pair sum $k \ge 7$, then $7 - k \le 0$, which is impossible for a die. So win prob from keeping a pair with sum $\ge 7$ is 0. Great — so keep-1 strictly beats keep-2 in this scenario (as long as some die $\le 3$).

**Condition for rerolling exactly 2:** Minimum pair sum $\ge 7$ AND at least one die $\le 3$.

Since $a \le b \le c$ and $a + b \ge 7$: $a \ge 7 - b \ge 7 - 6 = 1$ (always), and $a + b \ge 7$. For $a \le 3$: $a = 1$: need $b \ge 6 \Rightarrow b = 6$, then $c \ge 6 \Rightarrow c = 6$. Outcome: $(1,6,6)$.

$a = 2$: need $b \ge 5$. With $a + b \ge 7$ and $a = 2$: $b \ge 5$. So $b \in \{5,6\}$, $c \ge b$.
- $(2,5,c)$ with $c \ge 5$: $c \in \{5,6\}$.
- $(2,6,6)$.

$a = 3$: need $b \ge 4$. $b \in \{4,5,6\}$, $c \ge b$.
- $(3,4,c)$: $c \ge 4$, $c \in \{4,5,6\}$.
- $(3,5,c)$: $c \ge 5$, $c \in \{5,6\}$.
- $(3,6,6)$.

So the unordered outcomes $(a,b,c)$ with $a \le b \le c$, $a + b \ge 7$, $a \le 3$:
- $(1,6,6)$
- $(2,5,5), (2,5,6), (2,6,6)$
- $(3,4,4), (3,4,5), (3,4,6), (3,5,5), (3,5,6), (3,6,6)$

Count ordered outcomes:
- $(1,6,6)$: $3!/2! = 3$ arrangements.
- $(2,5,5)$: 3; $(2,5,6)$: 6; $(2,6,6)$: 3.
- $(3,4,4)$: 3; $(3,4,5)$: 6; $(3,4,6)$: 6; $(3,5,5)$: 3; $(3,5,6)$: 6; $(3,6,6)$: 3.

Total ordered outcomes: $3 + 3 + 6 + 3 + 3 + 6 + 6 + 3 + 6 + 3 = 42$.

But wait — for $(1,6,6)$, the die showing 1 is the one to keep (gives $p(2,6) = 5/36$); for $(2,5,5)$, keep the 2 (gives $p(2,5) = 4/36$); etc. All these cases have $a \le 3$, confirming keep-1 is optimal.

Probability = $\frac{42}{216} = \frac{7}{36}$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
