<Problem>
Jason rolls three fair standard six-sided dice. Then he looks at the rolls and chooses a subset of the dice (possibly empty, possibly all three dice) to reroll. After rerolling, he wins if and only if the sum of the numbers face up on the three dice is exactly $7$. Jason always plays to optimize his chances of winning. What is the probability that he chooses to reroll exactly two of the dice?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Setting up win probabilities.** If Jason keeps a subset of dice summing to $k$ and rerolls $r$ remaining dice, his win probability is $P(\text{r dice sum to } 7-k)$. Let $p(r,s)$ denote this.

- Reroll 0 (keep all): win iff current sum is 7.
- Reroll 1 (keep 2 summing to $k$): need one die to show $7-k$, so win prob $= \tfrac{1}{6}$ when $1 \le k \le 6$, else 0.
- Reroll 2 (keep 1 die showing $k$): need two dice to sum to $7-k$. Since $p(2,s) = (s-1)/36$ for $s \le 7$, win prob $= (6-k)/36$.
- Reroll 3 (keep none): $p(3,7) = 15/216 = 5/72$.

**Comparing strategies.** The key comparison:
$$\tfrac{1}{6} \approx 0.167 \;>\; \tfrac{5}{36} \approx 0.139 \;>\; \tfrac{5}{72} \approx 0.069.$$

So keeping a pair (reroll 1) with sum $\le 6$ is optimal whenever possible. Jason rerolls exactly 2 dice only when no pair sums to $\le 6$ — i.e., every pair sums to $\ge 7$ — AND keeping one die beats rerolling all three.

**When every pair sums to $\ge 7$:** If the pair sum $k \ge 7$, then $7-k \le 0$, so keeping a pair gives 0 win probability. Also, if all pairs sum to $\ge 7$, the total sum is $\ge 7 + \min(\text{third die}) \ge 8 > 7$, so keeping all three also gives 0. This leaves: keep 1 die (reroll 2) vs. reroll all 3.

Keeping the smallest die $a$ gives win prob $(6-a)/36$. This beats reroll-all ($5/72$) when $(6-a)/36 > 5/72$, i.e., $12-2a > 5$, i.e., $a \le 3$.

**Condition for rerolling exactly 2:** The two smallest dice satisfy $a + b \ge 7$, and $a \le 3$.

**Counting ordered outcomes** with $a \le b \le c$, $a+b \ge 7$, $a \le 3$:

| $(a,b,c)$ unordered | Ordered arrangements |
|---|---|
| $(1,6,6)$ | 3 |
| $(2,5,5)$, $(2,5,6)$, $(2,6,6)$ | $3+6+3 = 12$ |
| $(3,4,4)$, $(3,4,5)$, $(3,4,6)$, $(3,5,5)$, $(3,5,6)$, $(3,6,6)$ | $3+6+6+3+6+3 = 27$ |

Total: $3 + 12 + 27 = 42$ ordered outcomes.

$$P = \frac{42}{216} = \frac{7}{36}.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
