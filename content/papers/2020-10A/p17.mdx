<Problem>
Define

$$P(x) = (x - 1^2)(x - 2^2) \cdots (x - 100^2).$$

How many integers $n$ are there such that $P(n) \le 0$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
$P(x)$ is a degree-100 polynomial with roots at $1^2, 2^2, \ldots, 100^2$ (i.e., $1, 4, 9, 16, \ldots, 10000$). These are 100 distinct perfect squares.

**When is $P(n) = 0$?** Exactly at the 100 roots: $n \in \{1, 4, 9, 16, \ldots, 10000\}$.

**When is $P(n) < 0$?** The product of 100 factors is negative when an odd number of factors are negative. As $n$ increases through the real line, the sign of $P(n)$ changes at each root. Since the leading coefficient is positive and the degree is even, $P(n) > 0$ for very large or very small $n$.

The sign pattern in the intervals between consecutive roots (ordered $1 < 4 < 9 < \cdots < 10000$):

- $n < 1$: all 100 factors negative $\Rightarrow$ product positive (even number of negatives)
- $1 < n < 4$: 99 factors negative $\Rightarrow$ product negative (valid)
- $4 < n < 9$: 98 factors negative $\Rightarrow$ positive
- Continuing: the intervals $(1^2, 2^2), (3^2, 4^2), (5^2, 6^2), \ldots, (99^2, 100^2)$ give a negative product (odd number of negative factors).

There are exactly 50 such valid intervals: $(1,4), (9,16), (25,36), \ldots, (9801, 10000)$, i.e., the intervals $((2k-1)^2, (2k)^2)$ for $k = 1, 2, \ldots, 50$.

**Count the integers in each valid interval $((2k-1)^2, (2k)^2)$:**

The number of integers strictly between $(2k-1)^2$ and $(2k)^2$ is

$$(2k)^2 - (2k-1)^2 - 1 = (4k^2) - (4k^2 - 4k + 1) - 1 = 4k - 2.$$

**Sum over all 50 intervals:**

$$\sum_{k=1}^{50} (4k - 2) = 4 \cdot \frac{50 \cdot 51}{2} - 2 \cdot 50 = 4 \cdot 1275 - 100 = 5100 - 100 = 5000.$$

**Add the 100 roots where $P(n) = 0$:**

$$5000 + 100 = 5100.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
