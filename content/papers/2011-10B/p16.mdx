<Problem>
A dart board is a regular octagon divided into regions as shown. Suppose that a dart thrown at the board is equally likely to land anywhere on the board. What is the probability that the dart lands within the center square?

The octagon is divided by lines connecting midpoints of opposite sides, creating a center square surrounded by triangular and rectangular regions.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place the regular octagon with side length $s = \sqrt{2}$ using coordinates $A=(0,1)$, $B=(1,0)$, $C=(1+\sqrt{2},0)$, $D=(2+\sqrt{2},1)$, $E=(2+\sqrt{2},1+\sqrt{2})$, $F=(1+\sqrt{2},2+\sqrt{2})$, $G=(1,2+\sqrt{2})$, $H=(0,1+\sqrt{2})$.

The four dividing lines are $x=1$, $x=1+\sqrt{2}$, $y=1$, and $y=1+\sqrt{2}$, forming a center square with side $\sqrt{2}$ and area $(\sqrt{2})^2 = 2$.

The octagon fits inside the $(2+\sqrt{2})\times(2+\sqrt{2})$ square with four corner right triangles of legs $1$ removed, giving total area:
$$(2+\sqrt{2})^2 - 4\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}(1)(1) = (6+4\sqrt{2}) - 2 = 4+4\sqrt{2}.$$

The probability is:
$$\frac{2}{4+4\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{2(1+\sqrt{2})} = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{(\sqrt{2})^2-1^2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{2}.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
