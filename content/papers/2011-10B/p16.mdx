<Problem>
A dart board is a regular octagon divided into regions as shown. Suppose that a dart thrown at the board is equally likely to land anywhere on the board. What is the probability that the dart lands within the center square?

The octagon is divided by lines connecting midpoints of opposite sides, creating a center square surrounded by triangular and rectangular regions.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the side length of the regular octagon be $s$. Place the octagon with vertices at the coordinates corresponding to side length $1$.

For a regular octagon with side length $1$, the vertices are at positions like $(1, 0)$, $\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{1}, \ldots\right)$ — more precisely, using the coordinates from the problem: $A=(0,1)$, $B=(1,0)$, $C=(1+\sqrt{2},0)$, $D=(2+\sqrt{2},1)$, $E=(2+\sqrt{2},1+\sqrt{2})$, $F=(1+\sqrt{2},2+\sqrt{2})$, $G=(1,2+\sqrt{2})$, $H=(0,1+\sqrt{2})$.

The side length here is $1$ (e.g., $AB = \sqrt{1^2+1^2}/\sqrt{...}$ — actually $AB = \sqrt{(1-0)^2+(0-1)^2} = \sqrt{2}$, so the side length is $\sqrt{2}$; the horizontal/vertical sides have length $\sqrt{2}$ as well since $BC = \sqrt{2}$... let me recompute: $BC$ goes from $(1,0)$ to $(1+\sqrt{2},0)$, so $|BC| = \sqrt{2}$. And $AB$ goes from $(0,1)$ to $(1,0)$, so $|AB| = \sqrt{2}$. Yes, all sides have length $\sqrt{2}$).

The diagonals drawn connect: $A$ to $D$, $B$ to $G$, $C$ to $F$, $E$ to $H$.

The center square is formed by the intersection of these four lines. By symmetry, the center square has vertices at the midpoints. The center of the octagon is at $(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, 1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})$.

The diagonal $AD$ goes from $(0,1)$ to $(2+\sqrt{2},1)$ — that's a horizontal line $y=1$. The diagonal $EH$ goes from $(2+\sqrt{2},1+\sqrt{2})$ to $(0,1+\sqrt{2})$ — horizontal line $y = 1+\sqrt{2}$. The diagonal $BG$ goes from $(1,0)$ to $(1,2+\sqrt{2})$ — vertical line $x=1$. The diagonal $CF$ goes from $(1+\sqrt{2},0)$ to $(1+\sqrt{2},2+\sqrt{2})$ — vertical line $x = 1+\sqrt{2}$.

The center square is the region $1 \le x \le 1+\sqrt{2}$, $1 \le y \le 1+\sqrt{2}$, which has side length $\sqrt{2}$ and area $(\sqrt{2})^2 = 2$.

The area of the regular octagon with side length $\sqrt{2}$ is $2(1+\sqrt{2})s^2$ where $s$ is the side length: $2(1+\sqrt{2})(\sqrt{2})^2 = 2(1+\sqrt{2})(2) = 4(1+\sqrt{2}) = 4 + 4\sqrt{2}$.

Alternatively, the octagon spans from $x=0$ to $x=2+\sqrt{2}$ and $y=0$ to $y=2+\sqrt{2}$. Its area equals the area of the $(2+\sqrt{2})^2$ square minus four corner right triangles each with legs $1$:
$$(2+\sqrt{2})^2 - 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1)(1) = (4 + 4\sqrt{2} + 2) - 2 = 4 + 4\sqrt{2}.$$

The probability is:
$$\frac{2}{4+4\sqrt{2}} = \frac{2}{4(1+\sqrt{2})} = \frac{1}{2(1+\sqrt{2})} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{(\sqrt{2}+1)(\sqrt{2}-1)} = \frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{2}.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
