<Problem>
Circles with centers $P$, $Q$, and $R$, having radii $1$, $2$, and $3$, respectively, lie on the same side of line $l$ and are tangent to $l$ at $P'$, $Q'$, and $R'$, respectively, with $Q'$ between $P'$ and $R'$. The circle with center $Q$ is externally tangent to each of the other two circles. What is the area of triangle $PQR$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Set up coordinates with $l$ as the $x$-axis. The circles are tangent to $l$, so their centers are at heights equal to their radii: $P = (p, 1)$, $Q = (0, 2)$ (placing $Q$ at $x=0$), $R = (r, 3)$.

**Finding $p$:** $Q$ and $P$ are externally tangent, so $PQ = 2 + 1 = 3$.
$$PQ^2 = p^2 + (2-1)^2 = p^2 + 1 = 9 \implies p^2 = 8 \implies p = \pm 2\sqrt{2}.$$

Since $P'$ is to the left of $Q'$ (i.e., $p < 0$): $p = -2\sqrt{2}$.

**Finding $r$:** $Q$ and $R$ are externally tangent, so $QR = 2 + 3 = 5$.
$$QR^2 = r^2 + (3-2)^2 = r^2 + 1 = 25 \implies r^2 = 24 \implies r = 2\sqrt{6}$$
(positive since $R'$ is to the right of $Q'$).

**Area of triangle $PQR$** with $P=(-2\sqrt{2}, 1)$, $Q=(0,2)$, $R=(2\sqrt{6}, 3)$:

Using the formula $A = \frac{1}{2}|x_P(y_Q - y_R) + x_Q(y_R - y_P) + x_R(y_P - y_Q)|$:

$$A = \frac{1}{2}\bigl|(-2\sqrt{2})(2-3) + 0(3-1) + 2\sqrt{6}(1-2)\bigr|$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}\bigl|(-2\sqrt{2})(-1) + 0 + 2\sqrt{6}(-1)\bigr|$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}\bigl|2\sqrt{2} - 2\sqrt{6}\bigr|$$
$$= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2(\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}) = \sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
