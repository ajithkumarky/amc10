<Problem>
A quadrilateral is inscribed in a circle of radius $200\sqrt{2}$. Three of the sides of this quadrilateral have length $200$. What is the length of the fourth side?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the quadrilateral be $ABCD$ inscribed in a circle of radius $R = 200\sqrt{2}$, with $AB = BC = CD = 200$.

**Finding the central angles:** By the extended law of sines, for a chord of length $\ell$ in a circle of radius $R$, the central angle $\theta$ satisfies $\ell = 2R\sin(\theta/2)$.

For each side of length $200$:
$$200 = 2(200\sqrt{2})\sin(\theta/2) \implies \sin(\theta/2) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}.$$

So $\theta/2 = \arcsin\!\left(\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)$, meaning each of the three equal sides subtends the same central angle $\theta$.

**Finding $AD$:** The four central angles sum to $2\pi$:
$$3\theta + \phi = 2\pi \implies \phi = 2\pi - 3\theta.$$

The fourth side $AD = 2R\sin(\phi/2) = 2R\sin\!\left(\pi - \dfrac{3\theta}{2}\right) = 2R\sin\!\left(\dfrac{3\theta}{2}\right)$.

We have $\sin(\theta/2) = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}$, so $\cos(\theta/2) = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{8}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2\sqrt{2}}$.

$\sin\theta = 2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2) = 2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{4}$.

$\cos\theta = 1 - 2\sin^2(\theta/2) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

$\sin\!\left(\dfrac{3\theta}{2}\right) = \sin\!\left(\theta + \dfrac{\theta}{2}\right) = \sin\theta\cos(\theta/2) + \cos\theta\sin(\theta/2)$

$= \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{4} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{2\sqrt{2}} + \dfrac{3}{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{7}{8\sqrt{2}} + \dfrac{3}{8\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{10}{8\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{5}{4\sqrt{2}}$.

Therefore:
$$AD = 2R\sin\!\left(\frac{3\theta}{2}\right) = 2 \cdot 200\sqrt{2} \cdot \frac{5}{4\sqrt{2}} = 400\sqrt{2} \cdot \frac{5}{4\sqrt{2}} = 400 \cdot \frac{5}{4} = 500.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
