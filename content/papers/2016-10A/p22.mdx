<Problem>
For some positive integer $n$, the number $110n^3$ has $110$ positive integer divisors, including $1$ and the number $110n^3$. How many positive integer divisors does the number $81n^4$ have?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since $110 = 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 11$, write $n = 2^a \cdot 5^b \cdot 11^c \cdot k$ with $\gcd(k, 110) = 1$. Then
$$110n^3 = 2^{1+3a} \cdot 5^{1+3b} \cdot 11^{1+3c} \cdot k^3,$$
so the divisor count is $(2+3a)(2+3b)(2+3c)\cdot\tau(k^3) = 110$.

Each factor $(2+3a) \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, and $110 = 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 11$ has all three factors $\equiv 2 \pmod{3}$. The only way to write $110$ as a product of three factors each $\equiv 2\pmod{3}$ with $\tau(k^3) = 1$ (i.e., $k = 1$) is $(2+3a, 2+3b, 2+3c) = (2, 5, 11)$ in some order, giving exponents $(a,b,c) = (0,1,3)$ in some assignment.

For the divisor count of $81n^4$: since $81 = 3^4$ and $n$ involves only the primes $2, 5, 11$ with exponents $(0,1,3)$ in some order,
$$81n^4 = 3^4 \cdot p^4 \cdot q^{12}$$
where $\{p,q\}$ are two of $\{2, 5, 11\}$ with exponents $4$ and $12$. The divisor count is $(4+1)(4+1)(12+1) = 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 13 = 325$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
