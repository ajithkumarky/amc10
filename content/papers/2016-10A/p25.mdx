<Problem>
How many ordered triples $(x, y, z)$ of positive integers satisfy $\text{lcm}(x,y) = 72$, $\text{lcm}(x,z) = 600$, and $\text{lcm}(y,z) = 900$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Factor: $72 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2$, $600 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^2$, $900 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^2$. Write $x = 2^a 3^b 5^c$, $y = 2^d 3^e 5^f$, $z = 2^g 3^h 5^i$. The three lcm conditions translate independently for each prime.

**Prime 2:** $\max(a,d)=3$, $\max(a,g)=3$, $\max(d,g)=2$. Since $\max(d,g)=2$, both $d,g \leq 2$, forcing $a=3$ to satisfy the first two conditions. The constraint $\max(d,g)=2$ requires $d,g \in \{0,1,2\}$ with at least one equal to $2$: that is $3^2 - 2^2 = 5$ pairs.

**Prime 3:** $\max(b,e)=2$, $\max(b,h)=1$, $\max(e,h)=2$. Since $\max(b,h)=1$, both $b,h \leq 1$, forcing $e=2$. The condition $\max(b,h)=1$ requires at least one of $b,h$ to equal $1$, so $(b,h) \in \{0,1\}^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$: $3$ pairs.

**Prime 5:** $\max(c,f)=0$, $\max(c,i)=2$, $\max(f,i)=2$. From $\max(c,f)=0$: $c=f=0$. Then $\max(0,i)=2$ forces $i=2$: $1$ choice.

**Total:** $5 \times 3 \times 1 = 15$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
