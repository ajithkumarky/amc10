<Problem>
A four-term sequence is formed by adding each term of a four-term arithmetic sequence of positive integers to the corresponding term of a four-term geometric sequence of positive integers. The first three terms of the resulting four-term sequence are $57$, $60$, and $91$. What is the fourth term of this sequence?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the arithmetic sequence be $a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d$ and the geometric sequence be $b, br, br^2, br^3$, where all terms are positive integers.

The combined sequence gives:
$$a + b = 57, \quad (a+d) + br = 60, \quad (a+2d) + br^2 = 91.$$

Let $x = a+b = 57$, $y = (a+d)+br = 60$, $z = (a+2d)+br^2 = 91$.

Compute:
$$y - x = d + b(r-1) = 3,$$
$$z - y = d + b(r^2 - r) = d + br(r-1) = 31.$$

Dividing: $\dfrac{d + br(r-1)}{d + b(r-1)} = \dfrac{31}{3}$.

Let $u = d$ and $v = b(r-1)$. Then:
$$u + v = 3, \quad u + rv = 31.$$

Subtracting: $v(r-1) = 28$, so $b(r-1)^2 = 28$ (since $v = b(r-1)$).

From $u + v = 3$: $d + b(r-1) = 3$.

Since all terms must be positive integers and $b(r-1)^2 = 28$, the factorizations of $28$ as a perfect square factor times remainder:
- $b=1, (r-1)^2=28$: not a perfect square.
- $b=4, (r-1)^2=7$: not a perfect square.
- $b=7, (r-1)^2=4$: $r-1 = 2$, so $r=3$, $b=7$. Check: $b(r-1) = 14$, then $d = 3-14 = -11$.
- $b=28, (r-1)^2=1$: $r-1=1$, $r=2$, $b=28$. Then $b(r-1) = 28$, $d = 3-28 = -25$.

We need all four arithmetic terms to be positive integers. For the geometric sequence all terms must be positive: $b > 0$ and $r > 0$ ✓ for both candidates.

**Case $r=3$, $b=7$, $d=-11$:** From $a + b = 57$: $a = 50$. Arithmetic sequence: $50, 39, 28, 17$ (all positive ✓). Geometric: $7, 21, 63, 189$. Check: $50+7=57$ ✓, $39+21=60$ ✓, $28+63=91$ ✓. Fourth term: $17+189=206$.

**Case $r=2$, $b=28$, $d=-25$:** From $a+28=57$: $a=29$. Arithmetic: $29, 4, -21, \ldots$ — negative terms! Invalid.

The only valid case gives a fourth term of $\mathbf{206}$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
