<Problem>
Let $R$, $S$, and $T$ be squares that have vertices at lattice points in the coordinate plane, together with their interiors. The bottom edge of each square is on the $x$-axis. The left edge of $R$ and the right edge of $S$ are on the $y$-axis, and $R$ contains $\dfrac{9}{4}$ as many lattice points as does $S$. The top two vertices of $T$ are in $R \cup S$, and $T$ contains $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the lattice points contained in $R \cup S$. The fraction of lattice points in $S$ that are in $S \cap T$ is $27$ times the fraction of lattice points in $R$ that are in $R \cap T$. What is the minimum possible value of the edge length of $R$ plus the edge length of $S$ plus the edge length of $T$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Setup.** Let $r, s, t$ be the edge lengths of $R$, $S$, $T$ (positive integers). A square of side $n$ with lattice-point vertices contains $(n+1)^2$ lattice points.

**Condition 1** ($R$ has $\frac{9}{4}$ times as many lattice points as $S$):
$$(r+1)^2 = \tfrac{9}{4}(s+1)^2 \implies r+1 = \tfrac{3}{2}(s+1).$$
For integrality, $s+1$ must be even; write $s+1 = 2m$, so $r+1 = 3m$, giving $s = 2m-1$, $r = 3m-1$.

**Lattice points in $R \cup S$.** $R$ occupies $x \in [0,r]$ and $S$ occupies $x \in [-s,0]$; they share the $y$-axis column $x=0$, $y \in [0, \min(r,s)] = [0, 2m-1]$, which is $2m$ lattice points. By inclusion-exclusion:
$$|R \cup S| = (3m)^2 + (2m)^2 - 2m = 13m^2 - 2m.$$

**Condition 2** ($T$ contains $\frac{1}{4}$ of $|R \cup S|$):
$$(t+1)^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}(13m^2-2m).$$
For the right side to be an integer, $m(13m-2) \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$. If $m$ is odd, $m(13m-2) \equiv 1 \cdot 3 = 3 \pmod{4}$, which fails. So $m$ must be even; write $m = 2k$, giving $s = 4k-1$, $r = 6k-1$, and:
$$(t+1)^2 = \tfrac{52k^2-4k}{4} = k(13k-1).$$

**Condition 3 (the $27\times$ condition).** Square $T$ straddles the $y$-axis; let its left edge be at $x = -a$ and right edge at $x = t-a$. Then:
$$|S \cap T| = (a+1)(t+1), \quad |R \cap T| = (t-a+1)(t+1).$$
The condition $\dfrac{|S \cap T|}{|S|} = 27 \cdot \dfrac{|R \cap T|}{|R|}$ becomes (with $r+1=3m$, $s+1=2m$):
$$\frac{a+1}{4m^2} = \frac{3(t-a+1)}{m^2} \implies a+1 = 12(t-a+1) \implies 13a = 12t+11.$$
For $a$ to be a non-negative integer, $12t \equiv -11 \equiv 2 \pmod{13}$, i.e., $t \equiv 11 \pmod{13}$. Write $t = 13j+11$; then $a = 12j+11$, and $t - a = j \geq 0$ ✓.

**Combining.** With $t+1 = 13j+12$ and $(t+1)^2 = k(13k-1)$, and $\gcd(k,13k-1) = 1$, we need $k = A^2$ and $13k-1 = B^2$ for some positive integers $A, B$. This gives the negative Pell equation:
$$B^2 - 13A^2 = -1.$$
The continued fraction of $\sqrt{13} = [3;\overline{1,1,1,1,6}]$ has period 5 (odd), so solutions exist. Computing convergents: $h_4/k_4 = 18/5$, and $18^2 - 13 \cdot 5^2 = 324 - 325 = -1$ ✓. The fundamental solution is $(B,A) = (18,5)$.

This gives $k = A^2 = 25$, $B = 18$, $t+1 = AB = 90$, $t = 89$. Verify: $t \equiv 89 \equiv 11 \pmod{13}$ ✓, so $j = 6$.

With $k=25$: $m = 2k = 50$, $r = 3m-1 = 149$, $s = 2m-1 = 99$. The next solution to the negative Pell equation is much larger, giving $r+s+t \gg 337$.

Therefore, the minimum value is $r + s + t = 149 + 99 + 89 = \mathbf{337}$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
