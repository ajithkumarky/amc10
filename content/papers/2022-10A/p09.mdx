<Problem>
A rectangle is partitioned into $5$ regions as shown. Each region is to be painted a solid color — red, orange, yellow, blue, or green — so that regions that touch are painted different colors, and colors can be used more than once. How many different colorings are possible?

The figure shows a $3 \times 2$ grid of rectangles where: the bottom row has three side-by-side regions (left, middle, right), and the top row has two regions split differently (the left top region spans above regions 1 and part of 2; the right top region spans the rest). Adjacencies: region 1 touches 2 and 4; region 2 touches 1, 3, 4, and 5; region 3 touches 2 and 5; region 4 touches 1, 2, and 5; region 5 touches 2, 3, and 4.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Label the five regions as in the figure: bottom-left (1), bottom-middle (2), bottom-right (3), top-left (4), top-right (5).

Adjacency list:
- 1 is adjacent to 2 and 4.
- 2 is adjacent to 1, 3, 4, 5.
- 3 is adjacent to 2 and 5.
- 4 is adjacent to 1, 2, 5.
- 5 is adjacent to 2, 3, 4.

Count by choosing colors in order 2, 4, 1, 5, 3.

**Region 2:** 5 choices (any color).

**Region 4** (adjacent to 2): 4 choices (anything but the color of 2).

**Region 1** (adjacent to 2 and 4): if regions 2 and 4 share the same color, 4 choices; if they differ, 3 choices. But we've fixed them to differ (4 chose $\neq$ 2's color), so region 1 has 3 choices.

**Region 5** (adjacent to 2, 4): must differ from 2 and 4, which are different from each other, so 3 choices.

**Region 3** (adjacent to 2 and 5): must differ from 2 and 5. Are 2 and 5 the same? Region 5 $\neq$ region 2, so they differ: 3 choices.

Total: $5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 540$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
