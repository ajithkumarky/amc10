<Problem>
Quadrilateral $ABCD$ with side lengths $AB=7$, $BC=24$, $CD=20$, $DA=15$ is inscribed in a circle. The area interior to the circle but exterior to the quadrilateral can be written in the form $\dfrac{a\pi - b}{c}$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are positive integers such that $a$ and $c$ have no common prime factor. What is $a+b+c$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Step 1: Find the area of the quadrilateral using Brahmagupta's formula.**

The semiperimeter is $s = \tfrac{7+24+20+15}{2} = \tfrac{66}{2} = 33$.

$$[ABCD] = \sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)} = \sqrt{(33-7)(33-24)(33-20)(33-15)}$$
$$= \sqrt{26 \cdot 9 \cdot 13 \cdot 18} = \sqrt{26 \cdot 13 \cdot 9 \cdot 18}.$$

Note $26 \cdot 13 = 338$ and $9 \cdot 18 = 162$, so the product is $338 \cdot 162 = 54756$.

Check: $\sqrt{54756}$. We have $234^2 = 54756$, so $[ABCD] = 234$.

**Step 2: Find the circumradius using the diagonal.**

Split the quadrilateral along diagonal $AC$. Because $ABCD$ is cyclic, opposite angles are supplementary: $\angle A + \angle C = 180°$.

In triangle $ABC$: use the law of cosines to find $AC$.
In triangle $ACD$: the diagonal $AC$ is the same, and we can relate the two triangles.

Let $\angle DAB = \alpha$. By the law of cosines in $\triangle ABD$... actually, let's use a cleaner approach.

Since $ABCD$ is cyclic, we can check if any triangle formed is a right triangle. Try $\triangle ABC$ with sides $7, 24$: $7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$, so $AC = 25$ and $\angle ABC = 90°$.

Since $\angle ABC = 90°$, the diagonal $AC$ is a diameter of the circumscribed circle. So the circumradius is $R = \tfrac{25}{2}$.

**Step 3: Find the area of the circle.**

$$\text{Circle area} = \pi R^2 = \pi \cdot \frac{625}{4} = \frac{625\pi}{4}.$$

**Step 4: Compute the region's area.**

$$\text{Area} = \frac{625\pi}{4} - 234 = \frac{625\pi - 936}{4}.$$

Check that $\gcd(625, 4) = 1$ ✓ (625 is odd).

So $a = 625$, $b = 936$, $c = 4$, giving:

$$a + b + c = 625 + 936 + 4 = 1565.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
