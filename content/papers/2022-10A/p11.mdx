<Problem>
Ted mistakenly wrote $2^m \cdot \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{4096}}$ as $2 \cdot \sqrt[m]{\dfrac{1}{4096}}$. What is the sum of all real numbers $m$ for which these two expressions have the same value?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Note $4096 = 2^{12}$, so $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{4096}} = 2^{-6}$ and $\sqrt[m]{\dfrac{1}{4096}} = 2^{-12/m}$.

The original expression: $2^m \cdot 2^{-6} = 2^{m-6}$.

Ted's expression: $2 \cdot 2^{-12/m} = 2^{1 - 12/m}$.

Setting them equal:
$$m - 6 = 1 - \frac{12}{m}$$
$$m - 7 = -\frac{12}{m}$$
$$m(m-7) = -12$$
$$m^2 - 7m + 12 = 0$$
$$(m-3)(m-4) = 0.$$

So $m = 3$ or $m = 4$. Sum $= 3 + 4 = 7$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
