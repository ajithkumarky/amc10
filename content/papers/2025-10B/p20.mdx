<Problem>
Four congruent semicircles are inscribed in a square of side length 1 so that their diameters are on the sides of the square, one endpoint of each diameter is at a vertex of the square, and adjacent semicircles are tangent to each other. A small circle centered at the center of the square is tangent to each of the four semicircles, as shown below.

The diameter of the small circle can be written as $(\sqrt{a} + b)(\sqrt{c} + d)$, where $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$ are integers. What is $a + b + c + d$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Setup.** Place the unit square with vertices at $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(0,1)$. By 4-fold symmetry the four semicircles each have radius $r$, with the bottom one centered at $(r,0)$ bulging upward, and the others obtained by $90°$ rotation.

**Finding $r$.** Adjacent semicircles (e.g., bottom centered at $(r,0)$ and right centered at $(1,r)$) are externally tangent, so the distance between their centers equals $2r$:
$$\sqrt{(1-r)^2+r^2}=2r \implies 1-2r+2r^2=4r^2 \implies 2r^2+2r-1=0.$$
Taking the positive root: $r = \dfrac{-1+\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}$.

**Radius of the small circle.** The small circle centered at $\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)$ with radius $\rho$ is externally tangent to each semicircle (the flat side of each semicircle faces outward, so the small circle in the center touches the curved arc from outside). For the bottom semicircle:
$$\sqrt{\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}-r\Bigr)^2+\tfrac{1}{4}} = r+\rho.$$

Compute the left side with $\tfrac{1}{2}-r = \dfrac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2}$:
$$\left(\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2+\frac{1}{4} = \frac{(2-\sqrt{3})^2+1}{4} = \frac{8-4\sqrt{3}}{4} = 2-\sqrt{3}.$$

Since $2-\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{(\sqrt{3}-1)^2}{2}$, we get $\sqrt{2-\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sqrt{2}}$.

Therefore $\rho = \sqrt{2-\sqrt{3}} - r = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sqrt{2}} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2} = (\sqrt{3}-1)\!\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$.

**Diameter.** 
$$2\rho = (\sqrt{3}-1)\cdot\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = (\sqrt{3}-1)\cdot\frac{(2-\sqrt{2})\sqrt{2}}{2} = (\sqrt{3}-1)(\sqrt{2}-1).$$

This matches the form $(\sqrt{a}+b)(\sqrt{c}+d)$ with $a=3,\ b=-1,\ c=2,\ d=-1$, giving:
$$a+b+c+d = 3+(-1)+2+(-1) = 3.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
