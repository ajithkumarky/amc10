<Problem>
In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = 10$, $AC = 18$, and $\angle B = 130^\circ$. Let $O$ be the center of the circle containing points $A$, $B$, $C$. What is the degree measure of $\angle CAO$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since $O$ is the circumcenter, $OA = OB = OC = R$ (circumradius).

Triangle $OAB$ is isosceles with $OA = OB$, so $\angle OAB = \angle OBA$.

The central angle $\angle AOB = 2\angle ACB$ (inscribed angle theorem, since both subtend arc $AB$). But we don't know $\angle ACB$ directly.

Instead, use the fact that $\angle B = 130^\circ$ is an inscribed angle subtending arc $AC$ (not containing $B$). By the inscribed angle theorem, the central angle subtending the same arc is:
$$\angle AOC = 2 \angle ABC = 2 \times 130^\circ = 260^\circ.$$

But that's the reflex angle. The non-reflex central angle is $360^\circ - 260^\circ = 100^\circ$.

Since $OA = OC$, triangle $OAC$ is isosceles. The base angles are:
$$\angle OAC = \angle OCA = \frac{180^\circ - 100^\circ}{2} = 40^\circ.$$

Therefore $\angle CAO = 40^\circ$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
