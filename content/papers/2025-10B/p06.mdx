<Problem>
The line $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + 1$ divides the square region defined by $0 \leq x \leq 2$ and $0 \leq y \leq 2$ into an upper region and a lower region. The line $x = a$ divides the lower region into two regions of equal area. Then $a$ can be written as $\sqrt{s} - t$, where $s$ and $t$ are positive integers. What is $s + t$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
First, find the area of the lower region (below the line $y = \frac{1}{3}x + 1$ within the square).

The line $y = \frac{1}{3}x + 1$ passes through $(0, 1)$ and $(2, \frac{5}{3})$. At $x = 0$, $y = 1$; at $x = 2$, $y = \frac{5}{3}$. Both are within the square since $1 \leq \frac{5}{3} \leq 2$.

The lower region is bounded by $y = 0$ (bottom), $x = 0$ (left), $x = 2$ (right), and $y = \frac{1}{3}x + 1$ (top). Its area is:

$$\int_0^2 \left(\frac{1}{3}x + 1\right) dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{6} + x\right]_0^2 = \frac{4}{6} + 2 = \frac{2}{3} + 2 = \frac{8}{3}.$$

Half of this is $\frac{4}{3}$.

Now the vertical line $x = a$ divides the lower region. The left sub-region (from $x = 0$ to $x = a$) has area:

$$\int_0^a \left(\frac{1}{3}x + 1\right) dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{6} + x\right]_0^a = \frac{a^2}{6} + a.$$

Set this equal to $\frac{4}{3}$:

$$\frac{a^2}{6} + a = \frac{4}{3}.$$

Multiply through by $6$:

$$a^2 + 6a = 8$$
$$a^2 + 6a - 8 = 0.$$

Using the quadratic formula:

$$a = \frac{-6 + \sqrt{36 + 32}}{2} = \frac{-6 + \sqrt{68}}{2} = \frac{-6 + 2\sqrt{17}}{2} = \sqrt{17} - 3.$$

So $a = \sqrt{17} - 3$, giving $s = 17$ and $t = 3$, thus $s + t = 20$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
