<Problem>
In the figure shown below, $ABCDE$ is a regular pentagon and $AG = 1$. What is $FG + JH + CD$?

The figure shows regular pentagon $ABCDE$ with all five diagonals drawn, forming a smaller regular pentagon $FGHIJ$ inside. Vertex $A$ is at the top, with $B$ at upper-right, $C$ at lower-right, $D$ at lower-left, $E$ at upper-left. The five diagonals intersect pairwise to create inner pentagon vertices: $F$ on diagonals $AD$ and $BE$, $G$ on diagonals $BE$ and $CA$, $H$ on diagonals $CA$ and $BD$, $I$ on diagonals $DB$ and $EC$, and $J$ on diagonals $EC$ and $DA$. The given length $AG = 1$ is the segment along diagonal $CA$ from vertex $A$ to intersection point $G$.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $\phi = \dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ be the golden ratio. In a regular pentagon with side length $s$, each diagonal has length $\phi s$, and two intersecting diagonals divide each other in the ratio $\phi : 1$. Point $G$ lies on diagonal $CA$ (from vertex $C$ to vertex $A$), so $AG$ is the **shorter** piece: $AG = \dfrac{s}{\phi} = s(\phi - 1)$. Since $AG = 1$, we get $s = \phi = \dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$.

The five diagonals of the outer pentagon form a smaller regular pentagon inside with side length $\dfrac{s}{\phi^2} = \dfrac{1}{\phi} = \phi - 1 = \dfrac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}$.

From the figure, $FG$ is a **side** of the inner pentagon and $JH$ is a **diagonal** of the inner pentagon:
$$FG = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}, \qquad JH = \phi \cdot FG = \phi(\phi-1) = \phi^2 - \phi = (\phi+1)-\phi = 1.$$

$CD$ is a side of the outer pentagon: $CD = s = \dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$.

Therefore:
$$FG + JH + CD = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} + 1 + \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2} = \frac{2\sqrt{5}}{2} + 1 = \sqrt{5} + 1 = 1 + \sqrt{5}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
