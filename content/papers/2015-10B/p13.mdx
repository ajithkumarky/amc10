<Problem>
The line $12x + 5y = 60$ forms a triangle with the coordinate axes. What is the sum of the lengths of the altitudes of this triangle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The line $12x + 5y = 60$ intersects:
- The $x$-axis at $(5, 0)$ (set $y=0$: $12x = 60$, $x = 5$).
- The $y$-axis at $(0, 12)$ (set $x=0$: $5y = 60$, $y = 12$).

The triangle has vertices $A = (0,0)$, $B = (5,0)$, $C = (0,12)$.

**Side lengths:**
- $AB = 5$ (along $x$-axis)
- $AC = 12$ (along $y$-axis)
- $BC = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$

**Area:** $\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{2}(5)(12) = 30$.

**Altitudes:** For each altitude $h$ to side of length $s$: $h = \dfrac{2 \cdot \text{Area}}{s}$.

- Altitude to $AB$ (length 5): $h_1 = \dfrac{60}{5} = 12$
- Altitude to $AC$ (length 12): $h_2 = \dfrac{60}{12} = 5$
- Altitude to $BC$ (length 13): $h_3 = \dfrac{60}{13}$

**Sum:**
$$h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 12 + 5 + \frac{60}{13} = 17 + \frac{60}{13} = \frac{221}{13} + \frac{60}{13} = \frac{281}{13}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
