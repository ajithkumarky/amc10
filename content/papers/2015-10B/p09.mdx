<Problem>
The shaded region below is called a shark's fin falcata, a figure studied by Leonardo da Vinci. It is bounded by the portion of the circle of radius $3$ and center $(0,0)$ that lies in the first quadrant, the portion of the circle with radius $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and center $\left(0,\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$ that lies in the first quadrant, and the line segment from $(0,0)$ to $(3,0)$. What is the area of the shark's fin falcata?

The region is shaped like a shark's fin: the outer boundary is a quarter-circle of radius 3 (from $(3,0)$ to $(0,3)$), the inner boundary curves back via a semicircle of radius $\tfrac{3}{2}$ centered at $\left(0,\tfrac{3}{2}\right)$, and the base is the segment along the $x$-axis from $(0,0)$ to $(3,0)$.
</Problem>

<Solution>
The shark's fin falcata is bounded by:
- **Outer arc:** quarter-circle of radius 3, center $(0,0)$, from $(3,0)$ to $(0,3)$.
- **Inner arc:** the portion of the circle with radius $\tfrac{3}{2}$, center $\left(0,\tfrac{3}{2}\right)$, in the first quadrant. This circle passes through $(0,0)$ (since the distance from center to origin is $\tfrac{3}{2}$) and $(0,3)$ (distance $= 3 - \tfrac{3}{2} = \tfrac{3}{2}$). So the inner arc goes from $(0,3)$ back to $(0,0)$ — it's a semicircle of radius $\tfrac{3}{2}$.
- **Base:** segment along the $x$-axis.

**Area computation:**

Area of quarter-circle of radius 3:
$$\frac{1}{4}\pi(3)^2 = \frac{9\pi}{4}.$$

The inner semicircle of radius $\tfrac{3}{2}$ has area:
$$\frac{1}{2}\pi\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{9\pi}{4} = \frac{9\pi}{8}.$$

The shark's fin region = (quarter-circle) $-$ (semicircle of inner curve):
$$\frac{9\pi}{4} - \frac{9\pi}{8} = \frac{18\pi}{8} - \frac{9\pi}{8} = \frac{9\pi}{8}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
