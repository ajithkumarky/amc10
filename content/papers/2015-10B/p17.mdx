<Problem>
When the centers of the faces of the right rectangular prism shown below are joined to create an octahedron, what is the volume of the octahedron?

The prism has edge lengths $3$, $4$, and $5$.
</Problem>

<Solution>
The prism has dimensions $3 \times 4 \times 5$. Place it with one corner at the origin, so vertices at $(0,0,0)$ to $(5,4,3)$.

The centers of the six faces are:
- Two faces perpendicular to $x$: $\left(0, 2, \tfrac{3}{2}\right)$ and $\left(5, 2, \tfrac{3}{2}\right)$
- Two faces perpendicular to $y$: $\left(\tfrac{5}{2}, 0, \tfrac{3}{2}\right)$ and $\left(\tfrac{5}{2}, 4, \tfrac{3}{2}\right)$
- Two faces perpendicular to $z$: $\left(\tfrac{5}{2}, 2, 0\right)$ and $\left(\tfrac{5}{2}, 2, 3\right)$

The octahedron formed by these six points is not regular. We can compute its volume using the formula for an octahedron with three pairs of opposite vertices along the axes.

Centering at the midpoint $\left(\tfrac{5}{2}, 2, \tfrac{3}{2}\right)$, the six vertices are at distances $\pm\tfrac{5}{2}$ in $x$, $\pm 2$ in $y$, and $\pm\tfrac{3}{2}$ in $z$ from the center.

For a combinatorial octahedron with semi-axes $p, q, r$ (the vertices are at $(\pm p, 0, 0)$, $(0, \pm q, 0)$, $(0, 0, \pm r)$ relative to center), the volume is:
$$V = \frac{4}{3}pqr.$$

Here $p = \tfrac{5}{2}$, $q = 2$, $r = \tfrac{3}{2}$.

$$V = \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{5}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{3}{2} = \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{5 \cdot 2 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2} = \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{30}{4} = \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{15}{2} = \frac{60}{6} = 10.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
