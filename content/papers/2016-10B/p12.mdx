<Problem>
Two different numbers are selected at random from $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ and multiplied together. What is the probability that the product is even?
</Problem>

<Solution>
It's easier to find the probability that the product is **odd** and subtract from $1$.

The product is odd only when both selected numbers are odd. The odd numbers in $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ are $1, 3, 5$ — that's $3$ odd numbers.

Number of ways to choose $2$ numbers from $5$: $\dbinom{5}{2} = 10$.

Number of ways to choose $2$ odd numbers: $\dbinom{3}{2} = 3$.

$P(\text{odd product}) = \dfrac{3}{10}$.

$P(\text{even product}) = 1 - \dfrac{3}{10} = \dfrac{7}{10} = 0.7$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
