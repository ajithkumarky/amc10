<Problem>
Let $f(x) = \sum_{k=2}^{10}(\lfloor kx \rfloor - k\lfloor x \rfloor)$, where $\lfloor r \rfloor$ denotes the greatest integer less than or equal to $r$. How many distinct values does $f(x)$ assume for $x \ge 0$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Write $x = n + \theta$ where $n = \lfloor x \rfloor \ge 0$ and $\theta \in [0,1)$ is the fractional part. Then

$$\lfloor kx \rfloor - k\lfloor x \rfloor = (kn + \lfloor k\theta \rfloor) - kn = \lfloor k\theta \rfloor,$$

so $f(x) = \sum_{k=2}^{10} \lfloor k\theta \rfloor$ depends only on $\theta = \{x\}$.

**Range of $f$.** At $\theta = 0$, every term is $0$, so $f = 0$. For $\theta \in [9/10, 1)$, every $\lfloor k\theta \rfloor = k-1$, giving $f = \sum_{k=2}^{10}(k-1) = 1+2+\cdots+9 = 45$. So $f$ takes integer values in $\{0, 1, \ldots, 45\}$ — that is $46$ potential values — but may skip some.

**When values are skipped.** As $\theta$ increases through $[0,1)$, $f$ is non-decreasing and jumps only at breakpoints $\theta = p/q$ (in lowest terms, $q \in \{2,\ldots,10\}$). At such a breakpoint, every $k$ that is a multiple of $q$ contributes a unit jump, so the jump size equals the number of multiples of $q$ in $\{2,3,\ldots,10\}$. A jump of size $j$ skips $j - 1$ integer values.

**Counting skipped values** (by denominator $q$ in lowest terms):

| $q$ | Multiples of $q$ in $[2,10]$ | Jump | Fractions $p/q\in(0,1)$: $\phi(q)$ | Values skipped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | $2,4,6,8,10$ | 5 | 1 | $1\times 4 = 4$ |
| 3 | $3,6,9$ | 3 | 2 | $2\times 2 = 4$ |
| 4 | $4,8$ | 2 | 2 | $2\times 1 = 2$ |
| 5 | $5,10$ | 2 | 4 | $4\times 1 = 4$ |
| 6 | $6$ | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| 7 | $7$ | 1 | 6 | 0 |
| 8 | $8$ | 1 | 4 | 0 |
| 9 | $9$ | 1 | 6 | 0 |
| 10 | $10$ | 1 | 4 | 0 |

No two breakpoints coincide (each is a distinct reduced fraction). Total values skipped $= 4+4+2+4 = 14$.

**Distinct values** $= 46 - 14 = 32$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
