<Problem>
The sum of an infinite geometric series is a positive number $S$, and the second term in the series is $1$. What is the smallest possible value of $S$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the first term be $a$ and the common ratio be $r$. For the series to converge with positive sum, we need $|r| < 1$ and $a > 0$.

The second term is $ar = 1$, so $a = \dfrac{1}{r}$.

The sum is $S = \dfrac{a}{1-r} = \dfrac{1/r}{1-r} = \dfrac{1}{r(1-r)}$.

We need $a > 0$ and the sum is positive, so $r > 0$ (otherwise $a = 1/r < 0$). Also $r < 1$ for convergence. So $0 < r < 1$.

Minimize $S = \dfrac{1}{r(1-r)}$ is equivalent to maximizing $f(r) = r(1-r) = r - r^2$ for $0 < r < 1$.

By AM-GM or calculus: $f(r) = r(1-r) \le \left(\dfrac{r + (1-r)}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$, with maximum at $r = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

So the maximum of $r(1-r)$ is $\tfrac{1}{4}$, giving minimum $S = \dfrac{1}{1/4} = 4$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
