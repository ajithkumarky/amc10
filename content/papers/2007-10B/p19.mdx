<Problem>
The wheel shown is spun twice. The wheel has 6 equal sectors labeled $1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 9$. The randomly determined numbers opposite the pointer are recorded. The first number is divided by $4,$ and the second number is divided by $5.$ The first remainder designates a column, and the second remainder designates a row on the checkerboard shown (columns and rows each labeled $1$–$3$ and $1$–$4$ respectively). What is the probability that the pair of numbers designates a shaded square?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Column distribution (first spin mod 4):** The six wheel values give remainders $1, 2, 3, 2, 3, 1$, so each column 1, 2, 3 is equally likely with probability $\tfrac{1}{3}$.

**Row distribution (second spin mod 5):** The remainders are $1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 4$, so row 1 has probability $\tfrac{1}{3}$, row 2 has probability $\tfrac{1}{3}$, row 3 has probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$, row 4 has probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$.

**Shaded squares from the diagram:** The 6 shaded cells are $(1,3)$, $(2,4)$, $(2,2)$, $(3,3)$, $(1,1)$, $(3,1)$.

**Total probability:**
$$P = \frac{1}{3}\Bigl[P(\text{row}=3) + P(\text{row}=4) + P(\text{row}=2) + P(\text{row}=3) + P(\text{row}=1) + P(\text{row}=1)\Bigr]$$
$$= \frac{1}{3}\!\left[\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{3}\right] = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{2} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
