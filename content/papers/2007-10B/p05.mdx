<Problem>
In a certain land, all Arogs are Brafs, all Crups are Brafs, all Dramps are Arogs, and all Crups are Dramps. Which of the following statements is implied by these facts?

$\textbf{(A)}$ All Dramps are Brafs and are Crups.

$\textbf{(B)}$ All Brafs are Crups and are Dramps.

$\textbf{(C)}$ All Arogs are Crups and are Dramps.

$\textbf{(D)}$ All Crups are Arogs and are Brafs.

$\textbf{(E)}$ All Arogs are Dramps and some Arogs may not be Crups.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Map the given rules as set inclusions:
- Dramps $\subseteq$ Arogs
- Arogs $\subseteq$ Brafs
- Crups $\subseteq$ Dramps
- Crups $\subseteq$ Brafs

From "all Crups are Dramps" and "all Dramps are Arogs," it follows that all Crups are Arogs. Combined with "all Arogs are Brafs," all Crups are also Brafs. So **all Crups are Arogs and are Brafs**, which is choice (D).

The other choices fail: (A) is wrong because Dramps need not be Crups; (B) is wrong because not all Brafs need be Crups; (C) is wrong because Arogs need not be Dramps (the inclusion runs the other way); (E) is wrong because we cannot conclude Arogs are Dramps.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
