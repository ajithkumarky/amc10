<Problem>
Let $n$ be a $5$-digit number, and let $q$ and $r$ be the quotient and the remainder, respectively, when $n$ is divided by $100$. For how many values of $n$ is $q + r$ divisible by $11$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A 5-digit number $n$ satisfies $10000 \leq n \leq 99999$. When $n$ is divided by $100$:
$$n = 100q + r, \quad 0 \leq r \leq 99.$$

Here $q$ is the first three digits of $n$ (so $100 \leq q \leq 999$) and $r$ is the last two digits (so $0 \leq r \leq 99$).

We need $q + r \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$.

Since $n = 100q + r = 99q + (q + r)$, and $99q \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$ (since $99 = 9 \times 11$), we get $n \equiv q + r \pmod{11}$.

So the condition $11 \mid (q + r)$ is equivalent to $11 \mid n$.

The number of 5-digit multiples of $11$: 
- Smallest: $\lceil 10000/11 \rceil \cdot 11$. $10000 / 11 = 909.09\ldots$, so smallest is $910 \times 11 = 10010$.
- Largest: $\lfloor 99999/11 \rfloor \cdot 11$. $99999 / 11 = 9090.8\ldots$, so largest is $9090 \times 11 = 99990$.

Count: $9090 - 910 + 1 = 8181$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
