<Problem>
A large equilateral triangle is constructed by using toothpicks to create rows of small equilateral triangles. For example, with $3$ rows of small congruent equilateral triangles, there are $5$ small triangles in the base row. How many toothpicks would be needed to construct a large equilateral triangle if the base row consists of $2003$ small equilateral triangles?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Number of rows.** A large triangle with $k$ rows has $2k-1$ small triangles in its base row. Setting $2k-1 = 2003$ gives $k = 1002$ rows.

**Count by upward-pointing triangles.** The small triangles come in two orientations: upward-pointing $\triangle$ and downward-pointing $\nabla$. Row $i$ (counting from the top) contains exactly $i$ upward-pointing triangles, so the total number of upward triangles is

$$1 + 2 + \cdots + 1002 = \frac{1002 \cdot 1003}{2} = 502{,}503.$$

**Why this counts every toothpick once.** Each of the three sides of an upward-pointing triangle is shared only with a *downward*-pointing triangle (or is on the outer boundary) — never with another upward triangle. So if we give each upward triangle its $3$ toothpicks, every toothpick in the figure is counted exactly once.

Therefore the number of toothpicks is

$$3 \times 502{,}503 = 1{,}507{,}509.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
