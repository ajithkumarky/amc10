<Problem>
Sally has five red cards numbered $1$ through $5$ and four blue cards numbered $3$ through $6$. She stacks the cards so that the colors alternate and so that the number on each red card divides evenly into the number on each neighboring blue card. What is the sum of the numbers on the middle three cards?
</Problem>

<Solution>
There are 5 red and 4 blue cards, with colors alternating. Since reds and blues alternate and there are 5 reds and 4 blues, the arrangement must be: R B R B R B R B R (red on top and bottom).

Positions 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 are red (cards 1–5 in some order); positions 2, 4, 6, 8 are blue (cards 3–6 in some order). Each blue card must be divisible by both neighboring red cards.

Let the stack be $R_1, B_1, R_2, B_2, R_3, B_3, R_4, B_4, R_5$. The constraints are:
- $R_1 \mid B_1$, $R_2 \mid B_1$
- $R_2 \mid B_2$, $R_3 \mid B_2$
- $R_3 \mid B_3$, $R_4 \mid B_3$
- $R_4 \mid B_4$, $R_5 \mid B_4$

Blue cards are 3, 4, 5, 6. For each blue card, consider which pairs of red cards can neighbor it:
- Blue 3: divisible by red 1 and red 3 only (from $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, divisors of 3 are 1 and 3).
- Blue 4: divisors from $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$: 1, 2, 4.
- Blue 5: divisors from $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$: 1, 5.
- Blue 6: divisors from $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$: 1, 2, 3.

Blue 5 must be adjacent to red 1 and red 5 (the only divisors of 5 in our red set). So blue 5 sits between reds 1 and 5, or between reds 5 and 1. This means one end has red 1 or red 5, with blue 5 next to both.

Blue 3 must be adjacent to red 1 and red 3. But red 1 is already neighboring blue 5. If red 1 is at position 1, then blue 5 is at position 2, and red 5 is at position 3. Then blue 3 needs red 1 on one side — but red 1 is at position 1 and only neighbors position 2. So blue 3 cannot also neighbor red 1 unless red 1 is interior. Let red 5 be at position 1: blue 5 is at position 2, red 1 is at position 3. Then blue 3 (needing red 1 and red 3) is at position 4, with red 3 at position 5.

So far: $5, 5\text{(blue)}, 1, 3\text{(blue)}, 3, \ldots$

Remaining reds: $\{2, 4\}$, remaining blues: $\{4, 6\}$.

Positions 6, 7, 8, 9: $B_3, R_4, B_4, R_5$. Red 3 (at position 5) must divide $B_3$.
- $B_3 = 6$: $3 \mid 6$, and $R_4 \mid 6$. $R_4 \in \{2, 4\}$: $2 \mid 6$ yes, $4 \nmid 6$. So $R_4 = 2$ and $B_3 = 6$.
- Remaining: $B_4 = 4$, $R_5 = 4$. Check: $R_4 = 2 \mid B_4 = 4$ yes, $R_5 = 4 \mid B_4 = 4$ yes.

The stack is: $5, 5, 1, 3, 3, 6, 2, 4, 4$ (using card number, color).

Cards: $R=5, B=5, R=1, B=3, R=3, B=6, R=2, B=4, R=4$.

The middle three cards (positions 4, 5, 6) are: Blue 3, Red 3, Blue 6.

Sum $= 3 + 3 + 6 = 12$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
