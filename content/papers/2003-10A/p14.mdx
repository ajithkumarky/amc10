<Problem>
Let $n$ be the largest integer that is the product of exactly 3 distinct prime numbers $d$, $e$, and $10d+e$, where $d$ and $e$ are single digits. What is the sum of the digits of $n$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We need $d$, $e$, and $10d + e$ all prime, with $d$ and $e$ single digits (1–9).

Since $d$ and $e$ are single-digit primes, they come from $\{2, 3, 5, 7\}$.

To maximize $n = d \cdot e \cdot (10d + e)$, we want $d$ large (since it contributes most to $10d + e$) and $e$ large. Try combinations where $10d + e$ is also prime:

- $d = 7, e = 7$: $10(7) + 7 = 77 = 7 \times 11$. Not prime.
- $d = 7, e = 3$: $73$ is prime. $n = 7 \cdot 3 \cdot 73 = 1533$.
- $d = 7, e = 5$: $75 = 3 \times 25$. Not prime.
- $d = 7, e = 2$: $72 = 8 \times 9$. Not prime.
- $d = 5, e = 7$: $57 = 3 \times 19$. Not prime.
- $d = 5, e = 3$: $53$ is prime. $n = 5 \cdot 3 \cdot 53 = 795$.
- $d = 3, e = 7$: $37$ is prime. $n = 3 \cdot 7 \cdot 37 = 777$.

The largest is $n = 1533$ (from $d = 7$, $e = 3$, $10d + e = 73$).

Sum of digits of $1533$: $1 + 5 + 3 + 3 = 12$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
