<Problem>
Let $d$ and $e$ denote the solutions of $2x^{2}+3x-5=0$. What is the value of $(d-1)(e-1)$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
By Vieta's formulas for $2x^2 + 3x - 5 = 0$:
$$d + e = -\frac{3}{2}, \quad de = \frac{-5}{2}.$$

Expanding the expression:
$$(d-1)(e-1) = de - (d+e) + 1 = -\frac{5}{2} - \left(-\frac{3}{2}\right) + 1 = -\frac{5}{2} + \frac{3}{2} + 1 = -1 + 1 = 0.$$

This makes sense because $x = 1$ is a root: $2(1)^2 + 3(1) - 5 = 0$. So $d = 1$ or $e = 1$, making $(d-1)(e-1) = 0$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
