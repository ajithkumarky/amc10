<Problem>
For the consumer, a single discount of $n\%$ is more advantageous than any of the following discounts:

(1) two successive $15\%$ discounts

(2) three successive $10\%$ discounts

(3) a $25\%$ discount followed by a $5\%$ discount

What is the smallest possible positive integer value of $n$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Compute the effective discount factor (fraction of original price remaining) for each option:

(1) Two successive $15\%$ discounts: $(0.85)(0.85) = 0.7225$. Discount $= 1 - 0.7225 = 0.2775 = 27.75\%$.

(2) Three successive $10\%$ discounts: $(0.90)^3 = 0.729$. Discount $= 1 - 0.729 = 0.271 = 27.1\%$.

(3) $25\%$ then $5\%$ discount: $(0.75)(0.95) = 0.7125$. Discount $= 1 - 0.7125 = 0.2875 = 28.75\%$.

A single discount of $n\%$ leaves fraction $1 - \frac{n}{100}$ of the price. For it to be more advantageous than all three options, the remaining price must be less than all three remainders:
$$1 - \frac{n}{100} < \min(0.7225, 0.729, 0.7125) = 0.7125.$$

So $\frac{n}{100} > 0.2875$, meaning $n > 28.75$. The smallest positive integer is $n = 29$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
