<Problem>
What is the greatest power of $2$ that is a factor of $10^{1002} - 4^{501}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Write each term as a power of 2:

$10^{1002} = (2 \cdot 5)^{1002} = 2^{1002} \cdot 5^{1002}$.

$4^{501} = (2^2)^{501} = 2^{1002}$.

So:
$$10^{1002} - 4^{501} = 2^{1002} \cdot 5^{1002} - 2^{1002} = 2^{1002}(5^{1002} - 1).$$

Now find $v_2(5^{1002} - 1)$, the 2-adic valuation of $5^{1002} - 1$.

Use the Lifting the Exponent Lemma (LTE): for odd prime $p$ and $p \mid a - b$, $v_p(a^n - b^n) = v_p(a - b) + v_p(n)$. For $p = 2$ and even $n$: $v_2(a^n - b^n) = v_2(a - b) + v_2(a + b) + v_2(n) - 1$.

Here $a = 5$, $b = 1$, $n = 1002$:
- $v_2(5 - 1) = v_2(4) = 2$
- $v_2(5 + 1) = v_2(6) = 1$
- $v_2(1002) = v_2(2 \cdot 501) = 1$

So $v_2(5^{1002} - 1) = 2 + 1 + 1 - 1 = 3$.

Therefore $v_2(10^{1002} - 4^{501}) = 1002 + 3 = 1005$.

The greatest power of 2 dividing $10^{1002} - 4^{501}$ is $2^{1005}$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
