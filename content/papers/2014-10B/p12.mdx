<Problem>
The largest divisor of $2{,}014{,}000{,}000$ is itself. What is its fifth largest divisor?
</Problem>

<Solution>
First factorize $2{,}014{,}000{,}000$.

$2{,}014{,}000{,}000 = 2014 \times 10^6 = 2014 \times 2^6 \times 5^6$.

$2014 = 2 \times 1007 = 2 \times 19 \times 53$.

So $2{,}014{,}000{,}000 = 2^7 \times 5^6 \times 19 \times 53$.

To find the largest divisors, we remove one prime factor at a time from the full number. The divisors just below the number itself are obtained by dividing by the smallest prime factors:

- Divide by $2$: $\frac{2014000000}{2} = 1{,}007{,}000{,}000$
- Divide by $5$: $\frac{2014000000}{5} = 402{,}800{,}000$
- Divide by $19$: $\frac{2014000000}{19} = 106{,}000{,}000$
- Divide by $53$: $\frac{2014000000}{53} = 38{,}000{,}000$

Ordering these: $1{,}007{,}000{,}000 > 402{,}800{,}000 > 106{,}000{,}000 > 38{,}000{,}000$.

The 1st largest divisor is $2{,}014{,}000{,}000$ itself.
The 2nd largest is $1{,}007{,}000{,}000$ (divide by $2$).
The 3rd largest is $402{,}800{,}000$ (divide by $5$).
The 4th largest: the next candidates come from dividing by $2 \times 5 = 10$ or $2^2 = 4$:
- Divide by $4$: $\frac{2014000000}{4} = 503{,}500{,}000$
- Divide by $10$: $\frac{2014000000}{10} = 201{,}400{,}000$

$503{,}500{,}000 > 402{,}800{,}000$? No, $503{,}500{,}000 > 402{,}800{,}000$, so this is actually larger than our 3rd. Let me redo in order.

All divisors of the form $N/d$ for small $d$:
- $d = 1$: $2{,}014{,}000{,}000$
- $d = 2$: $1{,}007{,}000{,}000$
- $d = 4$: $503{,}500{,}000$
- $d = 5$: $402{,}800{,}000$
- $d = 8$: $251{,}750{,}000$
- $d = 10$: $201{,}400{,}000$
- $d = 16$: $125{,}875{,}000$
- $d = 19$: $106{,}000{,}000$

Ordering: $2{,}014{,}000{,}000 > 1{,}007{,}000{,}000 > 503{,}500{,}000 > 402{,}800{,}000 > 251{,}750{,}000 > \ldots$

The 5th largest divisor is $\dfrac{2{,}014{,}000{,}000}{8} = 251{,}750{,}000$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
