<Problem>
Orvin went to the store with just enough money to buy $30$ balloons. When he arrived, he discovered that the store had a special sale on balloons: buy $1$ balloon at the regular price and get a second at $\dfrac{1}{3}$ off the regular price. What is the greatest number of balloons Orvin could buy?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the regular price of one balloon be $p$. Orvin has $30p$ dollars total.

Under the sale, each pair of balloons costs $p + \dfrac{2p}{3} = \dfrac{5p}{3}$.

With $30p$ dollars, Orvin can buy $\dfrac{30p}{\frac{5p}{3}} = 30p \cdot \dfrac{3}{5p} = 18$ pairs, which is $36$ balloons.

To check: if he buys 18 pairs, he spends $18 \cdot \dfrac{5p}{3} = 30p$, exactly his budget. So he can buy exactly $36$ balloons.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
