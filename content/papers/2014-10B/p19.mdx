<Problem>
Two concentric circles have radii $1$ and $2$. Two points on the outer circle are chosen independently and uniformly at random. What is the probability that the chord joining the two points intersects the inner circle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Fix the first point $P = (2, 0)$ on the outer circle by rotational symmetry. Let the second point be $Q = (2\cos\phi, 2\sin\phi)$ with $\phi$ uniform on $[0, 2\pi)$.

The chord $PQ$ intersects the inner circle (radius 1) if and only if the distance from the center $O$ to the chord is less than 1. Using the triangle area formula: $\text{Area}(\triangle OPQ) = \frac{1}{2}|PQ|\cdot d = \frac{1}{2}|OP||OQ||\sin\phi| = 2|\sin\phi|$, so

$$d = \frac{4|\sin\phi|}{|PQ|}.$$

Since $|PQ|^2 = 8(1-\cos\phi)$ and applying the half-angle identities $1-\cos\phi = 2\sin^2(\phi/2)$ and $|\sin\phi| = 2|\sin(\phi/2)||\cos(\phi/2)|$,

$$d = \frac{4\cdot 2|\sin(\phi/2)||\cos(\phi/2)|}{\sqrt{16\sin^2(\phi/2)}} = 2|\cos(\phi/2)|.$$

The chord meets the inner circle when $d < 1$, i.e., $|\cos(\phi/2)| < \tfrac{1}{2}$. With $\phi/2 \in [0,\pi)$, this holds when $\phi/2 \in (\pi/3,\, 2\pi/3)$, equivalently $\phi \in (2\pi/3,\, 4\pi/3)$, an arc of length $2\pi/3$.

$$P = \frac{2\pi/3}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{3}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
