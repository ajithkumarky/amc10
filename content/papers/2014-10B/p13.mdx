<Problem>
Six regular hexagons surround a regular hexagon of side length $1$ as shown. What is the area of $\triangle ABC$, where $A$, $B$, $C$ are vertices of alternate outer hexagons?

The figure shows a central regular hexagon of side 1 surrounded by six identical regular hexagons (each of side 1), one on each side. Points $A$, $B$, $C$ are the outermost vertices of three alternating surrounding hexagons, forming a large equilateral triangle.
</Problem>

<Solution>
The center of the configuration is the origin. Each surrounding hexagon shares a side with the central hexagon.

The center of the central hexagon is at the origin. Its vertices are at distance $1$ from the center. The centers of the surrounding hexagons are at distance $2$ from the origin (they touch side-to-side with the center hexagon, and each hexagon center is $\sqrt{3}$ from its own side, so center-to-center distance is $2 \times \frac{\sqrt{3} \cdot 1}{\sqrt{3}} = 2$... let me compute directly).

For a regular hexagon of side $1$, the center-to-side distance (apothem) is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Two adjacent hexagons share a side, so their centers are $2 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}$ apart.

Place the central hexagon at the origin. Its surrounding hexagon centers are at distance $\sqrt{3}$ in the 6 directions (at angles $0°, 60°, 120°, \ldots$).

Points $A$, $B$, $C$ are vertices of alternating surrounding hexagons. By the Asymptote code, $A = 2\,\text{dir}(180)$, $B = 2\,\text{dir}(60)$, $C = 2\,\text{dir}(300)$ — these are at distance $2$ from the origin at $120°$ apart, forming an equilateral triangle.

The side length of triangle $ABC$: $|AB| = |2\,\text{dir}(60) - 2\,\text{dir}(180)|$.

$2\,\text{dir}(60) = (1, \sqrt{3})$, $2\,\text{dir}(180) = (-2, 0)$.

$|AB| = \sqrt{(1-(-2))^2 + (\sqrt{3}-0)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 3} = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$.

Area of equilateral triangle with side $s = 2\sqrt{3}$:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} s^2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 12 = 3\sqrt{3}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
