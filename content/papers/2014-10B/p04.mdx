<Problem>
Susie pays for $4$ muffins and $3$ bananas. Calvin spends twice as much paying for $2$ muffins and $16$ bananas. A muffin is how many times as expensive as a banana?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $m$ be the price of a muffin and $b$ be the price of a banana. Susie pays $4m + 3b$. Calvin pays $2m + 16b$, and that equals twice what Susie pays:
$$2m + 16b = 2(4m + 3b) = 8m + 6b.$$

Simplify:
$$16b - 6b = 8m - 2m \implies 10b = 6m \implies \frac{m}{b} = \frac{10}{6} = \frac{5}{3}.$$

A muffin costs $\dfrac{5}{3}$ times as much as a banana.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
