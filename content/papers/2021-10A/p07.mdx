<Problem>
Tom has a collection of $13$ snakes, $4$ of which are purple and $5$ of which are happy. He observes that

- all of his happy snakes can add,
- none of his purple snakes can subtract, and
- all of his snakes that can't subtract also can't add.

Which of these conclusions can be drawn about Tom's snakes?

**(A)** Purple snakes can add.

**(B)** Purple snakes are happy.

**(C)** Snakes that can add are purple.

**(D)** Happy snakes are not purple.

**(E)** Happy snakes can't subtract.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let's trace the logical chain using the three given statements:

1. Happy $\Rightarrow$ can add.
2. Purple $\Rightarrow$ cannot subtract.
3. Cannot subtract $\Rightarrow$ cannot add.

Combining (2) and (3): Purple $\Rightarrow$ cannot subtract $\Rightarrow$ cannot add.

So purple snakes **cannot add**.

Now consider a happy snake. By (1), it can add. But we just showed purple snakes cannot add. Therefore a happy snake cannot be purple.

This means **happy snakes are not purple**, which is choice (D).

Let's check the other options are not necessarily true:
- (A) says purple snakes can add — but we showed they can't.
- (B) says purple snakes are happy — not forced; we only know the reverse fails.
- (C) says snakes that can add are purple — false, since happy snakes can add but are not purple.
- (E) says happy snakes can't subtract — not deducible; happy snakes can add but we have no info about subtracting for them.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
