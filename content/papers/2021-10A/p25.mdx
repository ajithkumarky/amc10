<Problem>
How many ways are there to place $3$ indistinguishable red chips, $3$ indistinguishable blue chips, and $3$ indistinguishable green chips in the squares of a $3 \times 3$ grid so that no two chips of the same color are directly adjacent to each other, either vertically or horizontally?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Label the 9 cells by position; cells are adjacent if they share a side. We need a proper coloring of the $3\times3$ grid graph with colors R, B, G such that each color appears exactly 3 times.

**Identifying the center constraint.** The center cell (cell 5) has 4 neighbors: the 4 edge-midpoint cells $\{2,4,6,8\}$. The color placed at cell 5 cannot appear in any of those 4 cells, so the other 2 copies of that color must come from the 4 corner cells $\{1,3,7,9\}$. Any two corners are non-adjacent (corners only touch edge-midpoint cells), so any pair of corners is a valid choice. This gives $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ ways to place the remaining 2 copies of the center's color.

**Coloring the remaining 6 cells.** After fixing one color (say R) on cell 5 and 2 corners, the 6 remaining cells — the 4 edge-midpoints plus the 2 unchosen corners — must receive 3 B's and 3 G's with no two same-colored neighbors. The induced subgraph on these 6 cells is bipartite (a subgraph of the checkerboard grid), and its two parts each have exactly 3 cells, so there are exactly 2 proper 2-colorings (swap B and G between the two parts).

**Counting.** Choose which of the 3 colors occupies the center: 3 ways. Choose which 2 of the 4 corners share that color: $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ ways. Assign the remaining 2 colors to the 6 remaining cells: 2 ways.

$$\text{Total} = 3 \times 6 \times 2 = 36.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
