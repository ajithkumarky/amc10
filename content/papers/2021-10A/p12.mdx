<Problem>
Two right circular cones with vertices facing down contain the same amount of liquid. The radii of the tops of the liquid surfaces are $3\text{ cm}$ and $6\text{ cm}$. Into each cone is dropped a spherical marble of radius $1\text{ cm}$, which sinks to the bottom and is completely submerged without spilling any liquid. What is the ratio of the rise of the liquid level in the narrow cone to the rise of the liquid level in the wide cone?

(The narrow cone has liquid surface radius $3$ cm; the wide cone has liquid surface radius $6$ cm. Both cones are vertex-down. The marble displaces liquid, raising the level in each cone.)
</Problem>

<Solution>
The marble has volume $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi(1)^3 = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ cm$^3$.

When the marble sinks to the bottom of each cone, it displaces $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ cm$^3$ of liquid upward.

For a cone with vertex at the bottom, the liquid forms a smaller cone similar to the whole cone. If the liquid surface has radius $r$ and height $h$, then $r = kh$ for some constant $k$ depending on the cone's proportions. The volume of liquid is $V = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \dfrac{\pi k^2 h^3}{3}$.

We want to find the small rise $\Delta h$ in height when the volume increases by $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.

Differentiating $V = \dfrac{\pi k^2}{3} h^3$:

$$\Delta V \approx \pi k^2 h^2 \,\Delta h = \pi r^2 \,\Delta h,$$

where $r$ is the current radius of the liquid surface.

For the narrow cone ($r = 3$):
$$\frac{4\pi}{3} = \pi (3)^2 \Delta h_{\text{narrow}} \implies \Delta h_{\text{narrow}} = \frac{4/3}{9} = \frac{4}{27}.$$

For the wide cone ($r = 6$):
$$\frac{4\pi}{3} = \pi (6)^2 \Delta h_{\text{wide}} \implies \Delta h_{\text{wide}} = \frac{4/3}{36} = \frac{4}{108} = \frac{1}{27}.$$

The ratio of the rises is:
$$\frac{\Delta h_{\text{narrow}}}{\Delta h_{\text{wide}}} = \frac{4/27}{1/27} = 4:1.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
