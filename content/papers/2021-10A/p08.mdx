<Problem>
When a student multiplied the number $66$ by the repeating decimal $\underline{1}.\overline{\underline{a}\,\underline{b}}$, where $a$ and $b$ are digits, he did not notice the repeating bar and just multiplied $66$ times $\underline{1}.\underline{a}\,\underline{b}$. Later he found that his answer is $0.5$ less than the correct answer. What is the $2$-digit number $\underline{a}\,\underline{b}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the two-digit number $\overline{ab}$ equal $n$ (so $10 \le n \le 99$).

The repeating decimal is $1.\overline{ab} = 1 + \dfrac{n}{99}$.

The terminating decimal the student used is $1.ab = 1 + \dfrac{n}{100}$.

The student's answer was $0.5$ less than the correct answer:

$$66\left(1 + \frac{n}{99}\right) - 66\left(1 + \frac{n}{100}\right) = 0.5$$

$$66n\left(\frac{1}{99} - \frac{1}{100}\right) = 0.5$$

$$66n \cdot \frac{1}{9900} = 0.5$$

$$\frac{66n}{9900} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$66n = 4950$$

$$n = 75.$$

So $\underline{a}\,\underline{b} = 75$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
