<Problem>
What is the least possible value of $(xy-1)^2 + (x+y)^2$ for real numbers $x$ and $y$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Expand the expression:

$$(xy-1)^2 + (x+y)^2 = x^2y^2 - 2xy + 1 + x^2 + 2xy + y^2 = x^2y^2 + x^2 + y^2 + 1.$$

So we want to minimize $x^2y^2 + x^2 + y^2 + 1$.

Factor cleverly: $x^2y^2 + x^2 + y^2 + 1 = (x^2+1)(y^2+1)$.

Since $x^2 + 1 \ge 1$ and $y^2 + 1 \ge 1$ for all real $x, y$, the product satisfies

$$(x^2+1)(y^2+1) \ge 1.$$

Equality holds when $x = 0$ and $y = 0$, giving $(0-1)^2 + (0+0)^2 = 1$.

The minimum value is $\boxed{1}$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
