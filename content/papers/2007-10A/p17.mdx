<Problem>
Suppose that $m$ and $n$ are positive integers such that $75m = n^{3}$. What is the minimum possible value of $m + n$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Factor: $75 = 3 \cdot 5^2$. For $75m = n^3$, we need $n^3$ to be divisible by $3 \cdot 5^2$. Since $n^3$ is a perfect cube, all prime exponents in $n^3$ must be multiples of 3.

Currently $75 = 3^1 \cdot 5^2$. We need the exponent of 3 to be a multiple of 3, and the exponent of 5 to be a multiple of 3. The smallest such values are exponents 3 and 3 respectively, giving $n^3 = 3^3 \cdot 5^3 = (15)^3$.

So $n = 15$ and $m = \dfrac{n^3}{75} = \dfrac{3375}{75} = 45$.

Therefore $m + n = 45 + 15 = 60$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
