<Problem>
Triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$ are isosceles with $AB = BC$ and $AD = DC$. Point $D$ is inside triangle $ABC$, angle $ABC$ measures 40 degrees, and angle $ADC$ measures 140 degrees. What is the degree measure of angle $BAD$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since triangle $ABC$ is isosceles with $AB = BC$ and $\angle ABC = 40°$, the base angles are equal:
$$\angle BAC = \angle BCA = \frac{180° - 40°}{2} = 70°.$$

Since triangle $ADC$ is isosceles with $AD = DC$ and $\angle ADC = 140°$, the base angles are:
$$\angle DAC = \angle DCA = \frac{180° - 140°}{2} = 20°.$$

By symmetry, since $AB = BC$ and $AD = DC$, both triangles are symmetric about the perpendicular bisector of $AC$. Point $D$ lies on this line of symmetry, so $\angle DAC = \angle DCA = 20°$.

Therefore:
$$\angle BAD = \angle BAC - \angle DAC = 70° - 20° = 50°.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
