<Problem>
Four identical squares and one rectangle are placed together to form one large square. The length of the rectangle is how many times as large as its width?

The figure shows a large square of side $4$ units formed by placing four small squares (each of side $1$) along the top edge, with the remaining region below them being the rectangle.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the small squares each have side length $s$. Since four of them sit across the top of the large square, the large square has side $4s$. The rectangle fills the rest: it has width $4s$ and height $4s - s = 3s$.

The ratio of length to width is $\dfrac{4s}{3s} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
