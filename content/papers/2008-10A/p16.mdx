<Problem>
Points $A$ and $B$ lie on a circle centered at $O$, and $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$. A second circle is internally tangent to the first and tangent to both $\overline{OA}$ and $\overline{OB}$. What is the ratio of the area of the smaller circle to that of the larger circle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the large circle have radius $R$ and center $O$. The smaller circle is tangent to rays $OA$ and $OB$, so its center $I$ lies on the angle bisector of the $60^\circ$ angle, at the midpoint ray.

Since $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$, the half-angle is $30^\circ$. The small circle has radius $r$ and its center is at distance $d$ from $O$ along the bisector. The circle is tangent to $\overline{OA}$ (the ray), so $d \sin 30^\circ = r$, giving $d = 2r$.

The small circle is also internally tangent to the large circle, so the distance from $O$ to $I$ plus $r$ equals $R$:
$$d + r = R \implies 2r + r = R \implies r = \frac{R}{3}.$$

The ratio of areas is:
$$\frac{\pi r^2}{\pi R^2} = \frac{r^2}{R^2} = \frac{1}{9}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
