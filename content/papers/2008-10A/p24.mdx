<Problem>
Let $k={2008}^{2}+{2}^{2008}$. What is the units digit of $k^2+2^k$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Find the units digit of $k$.**

- $2008^2$: units digit of $8^2 = 64$ is $4$.
- $2^{2008}$: the units digits of powers of 2 cycle with period 4: $2,4,8,6,\ldots$ Since $2008 \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, the units digit of $2^{2008}$ is $6$.
- Units digit of $k = 4 + 6 = 10$, so units digit of $k$ is $0$.

**Find the units digit of $k^2$.**

Units digit of $k$ is $0$, so units digit of $k^2$ is $0$.

**Find the units digit of $2^k$.**

We need $k \pmod{4}$. Since $k$ ends in $0$, we have $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ (any number ending in $0$ is divisible by 10, hence by 4 — we need to verify: $k = 2008^2 + 2^{2008}$). 

$2008^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ (since $2008$ is even). $2^{2008} \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$ (since $2008 \geq 2$). So $k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, and the units digit of $2^k$ is $6$.

**Units digit of $k^2 + 2^k$:** $0 + 6 = 6$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
