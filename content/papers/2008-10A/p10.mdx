<Problem>
Each of the sides of a square $S_1$ with area $16$ is bisected, and a smaller square $S_2$ is constructed using the bisection points as vertices. The same process is carried out on $S_2$ to construct an even smaller square $S_3$. What is the area of $S_3$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
When you connect midpoints of a square's sides, the resulting square has half the area of the original. This is because each side of the new square is a diagonal of a $\frac{s}{2} \times \frac{s}{2}$ sub-square, giving side length $\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}$ and area $\frac{s^2}{2}$.

Starting with area $16$:
- $S_2$ has area $\frac{16}{2} = 8$.
- $S_3$ has area $\frac{8}{2} = 4$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
