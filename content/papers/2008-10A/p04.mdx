<Problem>
Suppose that $\tfrac{2}{3}$ of $10$ bananas are worth as much as $8$ oranges. How many oranges are worth as much as $\tfrac{1}{2}$ of $5$ bananas?
</Problem>

<Solution>
From the given: $\frac{2}{3} \times 10 = \frac{20}{3}$ bananas are worth $8$ oranges, so $1$ banana is worth $\frac{8}{\frac{20}{3}} = \frac{8 \cdot 3}{20} = \frac{6}{5}$ oranges.

We want the value of $\frac{1}{2} \times 5 = \frac{5}{2}$ bananas in oranges:
$$\frac{5}{2} \times \frac{6}{5} = 3 \text{ oranges.}$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
