<Problem>
Let $x$ and $y$ be two-digit integers such that $y$ is obtained by reversing the digits of $x$. The integers $x$ and $y$ satisfy $x^2 - y^2 = m^2$ for some positive integer $m$. What is $x + y + m$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Write $x = 10a + b$ and $y = 10b + a$ where $a, b$ are digits with $a \geq 1$ (so $x$ is a valid two-digit number) and $b \geq 1$ (so $y$ is also a valid two-digit number). Also $x > y$ requires $a > b$.

$$x^2 - y^2 = (x+y)(x-y) = (11a + 11b)(9a - 9b) = 99(a+b)(a-b).$$

We need $99(a+b)(a-b) = m^2$. Since $99 = 9 \times 11$:
$$m^2 = 9 \cdot 11 \cdot (a+b)(a-b).$$

For this to be a perfect square, $11(a+b)(a-b)$ must be a perfect square. Thus $11 \mid (a+b)(a-b)$.

Since $a$ and $b$ are single digits with $a > b \geq 1$ and $a \leq 9$:
- $a - b \leq 8$ and $a + b \leq 17$.

So $11 \mid (a+b)$ or $11 \mid (a-b)$.

Since $a - b \leq 8 < 11$, we need $11 \mid (a+b)$, so $a + b = 11$.

Then $(a+b)(a-b) = 11(a-b)$, and $m^2 = 9 \cdot 11 \cdot 11 \cdot (a-b) = 9 \cdot 121 \cdot (a-b)$.

For $m^2$ to be a perfect square, $(a-b)$ must be a perfect square. With $a + b = 11$ and $1 \leq b < a \leq 9$: possible values of $a - b$ are $1, 3, 5, 7, 9$ — but $a-b$ must be a perfect square, so $a - b \in \{1, 9\}$.

- $a - b = 9$, $a + b = 11$: $a = 10$. Not a digit. ✗
- $a - b = 1$, $a + b = 11$: $a = 6$, $b = 5$. So $x = 65$, $y = 56$. ✓

$$m^2 = 9 \cdot 121 \cdot 1 = 1089 \implies m = 33.$$

Check: $65^2 - 56^2 = 4225 - 3136 = 1089 = 33^2$. ✓

$$x + y + m = 65 + 56 + 33 = 154.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
