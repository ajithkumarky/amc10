<Problem>
The quadratic equation $x^2 + mx + n = 0$ has roots that are twice those of $x^2 + px + m = 0$, and none of $m$, $n$, and $p$ is zero. What is the value of $n/p$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the roots of $x^2 + px + m = 0$ be $r$ and $s$. By Vieta's formulas:
$$r + s = -p, \quad rs = m.$$

The roots of $x^2 + mx + n = 0$ are $2r$ and $2s$:
$$2r + 2s = -m \implies 2(-p) = -m \implies m = 2p.$$
$$(2r)(2s) = n \implies 4rs = n \implies n = 4m.$$

Therefore:
$$\frac{n}{p} = \frac{4m}{p} = \frac{4 \cdot 2p}{p} = 8.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
