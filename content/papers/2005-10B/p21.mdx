<Problem>
Forty slips are placed into a hat, each bearing a number $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $5$, $6$, $7$, $8$, $9$, or $10$, with each number entered on four slips. Four slips are drawn from the hat at random and without replacement. Let $p$ be the probability that all four slips bear the same number. Let $q$ be the probability that two of the slips bear a number $a$ and the other two bear a number $b \neq a$. What is the value of $\dfrac{q}{p}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The total number of ways to draw $4$ slips from $40$ is $\binom{40}{4}$, which appears in both $p$ and $q$ and cancels in the ratio.

**Favorable for $p$:** Choose which number all four slips show: $10$ ways. Then choose all $4$ slips of that number: $\binom{4}{4} = 1$ way. So the count is $10$.

**Favorable for $q$:** Choose the two distinct numbers $a$ and $b$: $\binom{10}{2} = 45$ ways. Choose $2$ slips from the $4$ bearing $a$: $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ ways. Choose $2$ slips from the $4$ bearing $b$: $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ ways. Total count: $45 \times 6 \times 6 = 1620$.

$$\frac{q}{p} = \frac{1620}{10} = 162.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
