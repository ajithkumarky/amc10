<Problem>
A subset $B$ of the set of integers from $1$ to $100$, inclusive, has the property that no two elements of $B$ sum to $125$. What is the maximum possible number of elements in $B$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Pair up integers that sum to $125$: $(25, 100), (26, 99), (27, 98), \ldots, (62, 63)$. This gives pairs $(25+k, 100-k)$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, 37$, which is $38$ pairs.

The remaining integers from $1$ to $24$ (that is, $\{1, 2, \ldots, 24\}$) have no partner summing to $125$ (since $125 - 24 = 101 > 100$ and $125 - 1 = 124 > 100$). All $24$ of these can be included freely.

From each of the $38$ pairs, we can include at most one element, contributing at most $38$.

Maximum size of $B$: $24 + 38 = 62$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
