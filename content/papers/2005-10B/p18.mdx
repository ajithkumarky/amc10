<Problem>
All of David's telephone numbers have the form $555$-$abc$-$defg$, where $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, $e$, $f$, and $g$ are distinct digits and in increasing order, and none is either $0$ or $1$. How many different telephone numbers can David have?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We need to choose $7$ distinct digits from $\{2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9\}$ (8 available digits) and arrange them in increasing order — but the order is forced once we choose the digits. So the number of telephone numbers equals the number of ways to choose $7$ digits from $8$:
$$\binom{8}{7} = 8.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
