<Problem>
Twelve fair dice are rolled. What is the probability that the product of the numbers on the top faces is prime?
</Problem>

<Solution>
For the product of twelve dice rolls to be prime, the product must equal a prime number. The only prime faces available are $2$, $3$, and $5$. For the overall product to be prime, exactly one die must show a prime ($2$, $3$, or $5$) and all other eleven dice must show $1$.

**Probability a single die shows $1$:** $\frac{1}{6}$.

**Probability a single die shows a prime:** $\frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$ (faces $2$, $3$, $5$).

**Choose which of the $12$ dice shows the prime:** $\binom{12}{1} = 12$ ways.

$$P = 12 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^{11} = \frac{12}{2} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^{11} = 6 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^{11} = \left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^{10}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
