<Problem>
An envelope contains eight bills: $2$ ones, $2$ fives, $2$ tens, and $2$ twenties. Two bills are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that their sum is $\textdollar 20$ or more?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Total ways to choose $2$ bills from $8$: $\binom{8}{2} = 28$.

Pairs summing to at least $\textdollar 20$:
- **Two twenties:** $\binom{2}{2} = 1$. Sum $= \textdollar 40$. ✓
- **Twenty + ten:** $2 \times 2 = 4$. Sum $= \textdollar 30$. ✓
- **Twenty + five:** $2 \times 2 = 4$. Sum $= \textdollar 25$. ✓
- **Twenty + one:** $2 \times 2 = 4$. Sum $= \textdollar 21$. ✓
- **Two tens:** $\binom{2}{2} = 1$. Sum $= \textdollar 20$. ✓
- **Ten + five:** $2 \times 2 = 4$. Sum $= \textdollar 15$. ✗
- **Ten + one:** sum $= \textdollar 11$. ✗ (and other smaller pairs also fail)

Favorable: $1 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 1 = 14$.

Probability: $\dfrac{14}{28} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
