<Problem>
Two equilateral triangles are contained in a square whose side length is $2\sqrt{3}$. The bases of these triangles are the opposite sides of the square, and their intersection is a rhombus. What is the area of the rhombus?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place the square with corners at $(0,0)$, $(2\sqrt{3},0)$, $(2\sqrt{3},2\sqrt{3})$, $(0,2\sqrt{3})$. Each equilateral triangle has side $2\sqrt{3}$ and height $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot 2\sqrt{3}=3$.

**Triangle 1** (base on bottom, apex up): vertices $(0,0)$, $(2\sqrt{3},0)$, $(\sqrt{3},3)$. Its non-base sides are:
- Left: $y = \sqrt{3}\,x$
- Right: $y = -\sqrt{3}\,x + 6$

**Triangle 2** (base on top, apex down): vertices $(0,2\sqrt{3})$, $(2\sqrt{3},2\sqrt{3})$, $(\sqrt{3},2\sqrt{3}-3)$. Its non-base sides are:
- Left: $y = -\sqrt{3}\,x + 2\sqrt{3}$
- Right: $y = \sqrt{3}\,x + 2\sqrt{3} - 6$

The intersection region is bounded by all four of these lines (the bases lie outside the overlapping region). Lines with the same slope come in parallel pairs; the four vertices of the rhombus are the four cross-intersections:

- (Left T1) $\cap$ (Left T2): $\sqrt{3}x = -\sqrt{3}x+2\sqrt{3} \Rightarrow x=1,\;y=\sqrt{3}$, giving $(1,\sqrt{3})$.
- (Right T1) $\cap$ (Right T2): $-\sqrt{3}x+6 = \sqrt{3}x+2\sqrt{3}-6 \Rightarrow x=2\sqrt{3}-1,\;y=\sqrt{3}$, giving $(2\sqrt{3}-1,\sqrt{3})$.
- (Left T1) $\cap$ (Right T1): the apex of T1 at $(\sqrt{3},3)$, which lies inside T2.
- (Left T2) $\cap$ (Right T2): the apex of T2 at $(\sqrt{3},2\sqrt{3}-3)$, which lies inside T1.

The rhombus has diagonals:
- Horizontal: from $(1,\sqrt{3})$ to $(2\sqrt{3}-1,\sqrt{3})$, length $2\sqrt{3}-2$.
- Vertical: from $(\sqrt{3},2\sqrt{3}-3)$ to $(\sqrt{3},3)$, length $6-2\sqrt{3}$.

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}(2\sqrt{3}-2)(6-2\sqrt{3}) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2(\sqrt{3}-1)\cdot 2(3-\sqrt{3}) = 2(\sqrt{3}-1)(3-\sqrt{3}).$$

Expanding: $(\sqrt{3}-1)(3-\sqrt{3}) = 3\sqrt{3}-3-3+\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}-6$, so Area $= 2(4\sqrt{3}-6) = 8\sqrt{3}-12$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
