<Problem>
A dessert chef prepares the dessert for every day of a week starting with Sunday. The dessert each day is either cake, pie, ice cream, or pudding. The same dessert may not be served two days in a row. There must be cake on Friday because of a birthday. How many different dessert menus for the week are possible?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The days are Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday (positions 1 through 7). Friday is position 6, and it must be cake.

We count from Sunday to Saturday, enforcing no two consecutive days the same and cake on Friday.

**Count forward from Sunday to Thursday (days 1–5), ending on some non-cake dessert on Thursday:**

Days 1–5 form a chain with no two consecutive the same, and day 5 (Thursday) must not be cake (since Friday is cake).

For a chain of length $n$ with 4 choices where consecutive differ, the number of sequences is $4 \cdot 3^{n-1}$. But we need the last element to not be cake specifically.

By symmetry among the 4 desserts, the number of sequences of length $n$ ending in any specific non-first dessert type equals:

Total sequences of length $n$ from any start: $4 \cdot 3^{n-1}$.

Among those, $\frac{1}{4} \cdot 4 \cdot 3^{n-1} = 3^{n-1}$ end in cake (by symmetry since all choices are equivalent).

So sequences of length 5 not ending in cake: $4 \cdot 3^4 - 3^4 = 3 \cdot 3^4 = 3^5 = 243$.

**Saturday (day 7):** must differ from Friday (cake), so 3 choices.

**Total:** $243 \times 3 = 729$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
