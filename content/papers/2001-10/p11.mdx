<Problem>
Consider a dark center square surrounded by rings of unit squares. The first ring around the center square contains $8$ unit squares. The second ring contains $16$ unit squares. If we continue this process, the number of unit squares in the $100$th ring is
</Problem>

<Solution>
The $k$th ring consists of the unit squares in the $(2k+1) \times (2k+1)$ square minus those in the $(2k-1) \times (2k-1)$ square.

The number of squares in the $k$th ring is:
$$(2k+1)^2 - (2k-1)^2.$$

Using the difference of squares: $(2k+1+2k-1)(2k+1-2k+1) = (4k)(2) = 8k$.

For the 100th ring: $8 \times 100 = 800$.

We can verify: ring 1 has $8(1) = 8$ squares ✓, ring 2 has $8(2) = 16$ squares ✓.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
