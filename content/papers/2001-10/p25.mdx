<Problem>
How many positive integers not exceeding $2001$ are multiples of $3$ or $4$ but not $5$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We use inclusion-exclusion. Let:
- $A$ = multiples of 3 up to 2001
- $B$ = multiples of 4 up to 2001
- $C$ = multiples of 5 up to 2001

We want $|A \cup B| - |A \cup B \cap C|$, or equivalently $|(A \cup B) \setminus C|$.

**Step 1: Count multiples of 3 or 4 up to 2001.**

$$|A| = \left\lfloor \frac{2001}{3} \right\rfloor = 667$$
$$|B| = \left\lfloor \frac{2001}{4} \right\rfloor = 500$$
$$|A \cap B| = \left\lfloor \frac{2001}{12} \right\rfloor = 166$$
$$|A \cup B| = 667 + 500 - 166 = 1001.$$

**Step 2: Subtract those also divisible by 5.**

We need multiples of (3 or 4) AND 5, i.e., multiples of 15 or 20.

$$|\text{mult of 15}| = \left\lfloor \frac{2001}{15} \right\rfloor = 133$$
$$|\text{mult of 20}| = \left\lfloor \frac{2001}{20} \right\rfloor = 100$$
$$|\text{mult of 60}| = \left\lfloor \frac{2001}{60} \right\rfloor = 33$$
$$|\text{mult of 15 or 20}| = 133 + 100 - 33 = 200.$$

**Step 3: Final count.**

$$1001 - 200 = 801.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
