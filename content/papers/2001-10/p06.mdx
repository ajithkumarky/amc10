<Problem>
Let $P(n)$ and $S(n)$ denote the product and the sum, respectively, of the digits of the integer $n$. For example, $P(23) = 6$ and $S(23) = 5$. Suppose $N$ is a two-digit number such that $N = P(N) + S(N)$. What is the units digit of $N$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $N$ have tens digit $a$ and units digit $b$, so $N = 10a + b$ with $1 \leq a \leq 9$ and $0 \leq b \leq 9$.

Then $P(N) = ab$ and $S(N) = a + b$, so the condition becomes:
$$10a + b = ab + a + b.$$

Simplifying: $10a = ab + a$, so $9a = ab$, giving $9 = b$ (since $a \neq 0$).

So the units digit of $N$ is $\mathbf{9}$. (Any two-digit number ending in 9 works: for example, $N = 19$ gives $P(19) + S(19) = 9 + 10 = 19$. $\checkmark$)

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
