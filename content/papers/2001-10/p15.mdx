<Problem>
A street has parallel curbs $40$ feet apart. A crosswalk bounded by two parallel stripes crosses the street at an angle. The length of the curb between the stripes is $15$ feet and each stripe is $50$ feet long. Find the distance, in feet, between the stripes.
</Problem>

<Solution>
The crosswalk is a parallelogram. The two parallel stripes are its lateral sides (each $50$ ft long), and the two segments of curb between the stripes are its bases ($15$ ft each). The street width (distance between the curbs) is $40$ ft, which is the height of the parallelogram measured perpendicular to the curbs.

The area of the parallelogram can be computed two ways:
- Using the curb segments as bases with height $= 40$ ft (the street width):
$$\text{Area} = 15 \times 40 = 600 \text{ sq ft}.$$
- Using the stripes as bases with height $= d$ (the distance between the stripes):
$$\text{Area} = 50 \times d.$$

Setting equal: $50d = 600$, so $d = 12$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
