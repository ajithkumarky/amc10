<Problem>
Pat wants to buy four donuts from an ample supply of three types of donuts: glazed, chocolate, and powdered. How many different selections are possible?
</Problem>

<Solution>
This is a "stars and bars" problem: we want the number of ways to choose a total of 4 donuts from 3 types, where order doesn't matter and repetition is allowed.

The number of ways to choose $n$ items from $k$ types with repetition is $\dbinom{n+k-1}{k-1}$.

Here $n = 4$ and $k = 3$:
$$\binom{4 + 3 - 1}{3 - 1} = \binom{6}{2} = 15.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
