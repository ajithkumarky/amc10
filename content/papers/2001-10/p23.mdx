<Problem>
A box contains exactly five chips, three red and two white. Chips are randomly removed one at a time without replacement until all the red chips are drawn or all the white chips are drawn. What is the probability that the last chip drawn is white?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Consider all $5!/(3!\,2!)=10$ equally likely orderings of the 5 chips. The process stops the moment every chip of one color has been drawn. The key observation is:

The last chip drawn is white $\iff$ the process stops by exhausting the white chips $\iff$ the 2nd white chip is drawn before the 3rd red chip $\iff$ the 5th chip (last in the full ordering) is red.

Why? If the 5th chip is red, then the 3rd red sits at position 5, while the 2nd white must be at some position $\leq 4$; we stop at the 2nd white, so the last chip drawn is white. Conversely, if the 5th chip is white, the 2nd white is at position 5 and the 3rd red is at some position $\leq 4$; we stop at the 3rd red, so the last chip drawn is red.

Since each of the 5 chips is equally likely to be last, the probability the 5th chip is red is $\dfrac{3}{5}$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
