<Problem>
A telephone number has the form $ABC\text{-}DEF\text{-}GHIJ$, where each letter represents a different digit. The digits in each part of the number are in decreasing order; that is, $A > B > C$, $D > E > F$, and $G > H > I > J$. Furthermore, $D$, $E$, and $F$ are consecutive even digits; $G$, $H$, $I$, and $J$ are consecutive odd digits; and $A + B + C = 9$. Find $A$.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since all 10 digits must appear exactly once, we identify each part systematically.

**Consecutive odd digits $G > H > I > J$:** The odd digits are $1,3,5,7,9$. The two sets of four consecutive odd digits are $\{9,7,5,3\}$ and $\{7,5,3,1\}$, giving $GHIJ = 9753$ or $7531$.

**Consecutive even digits $D > E > F$:** The even digits are $0,2,4,6,8$. The three possible sets are $\{8,6,4\}$, $\{6,4,2\}$, and $\{4,2,0\}$.

**Finding $ABC$ with $A+B+C=9$:** Try $GHIJ = 9753$. For each even triple, the remaining three digits (for $ABC$) are:
- $DEF = 864$: remaining $\{0,1,2\}$, sum $= 3$. No.
- $DEF = 642$: remaining $\{0,1,8\}$, sum $= 9$. Yes!
- $DEF = 420$: remaining $\{1,6,8\}$, sum $= 15$. No.

The unique solution is $GHIJ = 9753$, $DEF = 642$, and $ABC$ uses $\{8,1,0\}$ in decreasing order: $A = 8$, $B = 1$, $C = 0$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
