<Problem>
Ms. Carroll promised that anyone who got all the multiple choice questions right on the upcoming exam would receive an A on the exam. Which of these statements necessarily follows logically?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The original statement is: "If [all multiple choice right] then [receives an A]." In logic: $P \Rightarrow Q$.

The only statement that follows logically from $P \Rightarrow Q$ is its **contrapositive**: $\neg Q \Rightarrow \neg P$, i.e., "If [does not receive an A] then [did not get all multiple choice right]," which means he got at least one wrong.

Check the choices:
- **(A)** says "got all wrong" — too strong; the contrapositive only says at least one wrong.
- **(B)** says "at least one multiple choice wrong" — this is exactly $\neg P$, the contrapositive. **Correct.**
- **(C)** says "at least one wrong → no A" — this is the inverse of the inverse, not guaranteed (Ms. Carroll might give A for other reasons).
- **(D)** says "A → all right" — the converse; not necessarily true.
- **(E)** says "A → at least one right" — true but trivially implied by (B) and not really what the contrapositive gives directly.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
