<Problem>
Sides $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{AC}$ of equilateral triangle $ABC$ are tangent to a circle at points $B$ and $C$ respectively. What fraction of the area of $\triangle ABC$ lies outside the circle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the side length of the equilateral triangle be $s = 1$.

Since $AB$ and $AC$ are tangent to the circle at $B$ and $C$, the center $O$ of the circle lies on the angle bisector from $A$, which is also the altitude. The radius $r$ is perpendicular to $AB$ at $B$ and to $AC$ at $C$.

In $\triangle ABC$, angle $A = 60°$. The tangent from $A$ to the circle has length $|AB| = 1$. The distance from $A$ to $O$ is $\dfrac{r}{\sin 30°} = \dfrac{r}{1/2} = 2r$ (since $\angle OAB = 30°$ in the right triangle $OAB$).

Also, $|AB|^2 = |AO|^2 - r^2$ (tangent-radius relation): $1 = 4r^2 - r^2 = 3r^2$, so $r = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.

**Area of $\triangle ABC$:** $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} s^2 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$.

**The circle sector inside $\triangle ABC$:** The center $O$ is outside the triangle (since the circle is tangent externally at $B$ and $C$ with $O$ beyond the triangle on the $A$ side). Wait — let's reconsider the geometry.

$O$ is on the ray from $A$ through the midpoint of $BC$, at distance $2r = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$ from $A$. The altitude of the equilateral triangle from $A$ is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Since $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 1.155 > \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866$, the center $O$ is outside the triangle beyond $BC$.

The circle intersects the triangle. The arc inside the triangle subtends angle $\angle BOC$ at $O$.

$\angle BOA = 90° - 30° = 60°$ (in right triangle $OBA$: $\angle OAB = 30°$). So $\angle BOC = 2 \times 60° = 120°$.

The region inside $\triangle ABC$ but inside the circle is a circular sector of angle $120° = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and radius $r = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, minus the triangle $OBC$... Actually it's the circular segment. Since $O$ is outside the triangle and the arc $BC$ (the minor arc inside the triangle) subtends $120°$ at $O$:

Area of circular sector $BOC = \dfrac{1}{2}r^2 \cdot \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{9}$.

Area of triangle $OBC$: base $BC = 1$, height from $O$ to $BC$. $O$ is at distance $AO - $ (altitude of $\triangle ABC$) $= \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{4-3}{2\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}$ beyond $BC$. So height from $O$ to $BC$ is $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}$.

Area of $\triangle OBC = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{1}{4\sqrt{3}}$.

The circular segment inside $\triangle ABC$ (between chord $BC$ and the arc) has area:
$$\text{Sector area} - \text{Triangle } OBC = \dfrac{\pi}{9} - \dfrac{1}{4\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\pi}{9} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{12}.$$

Fraction outside circle $= \dfrac{\text{Area of } \triangle ABC - \text{segment}}{\text{Area of } \triangle ABC} = 1 - \dfrac{\frac{\pi}{9} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{12}}{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}}$.

$$= 1 - \frac{\frac{\pi}{9}}{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}} + \frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{12}}{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}} = 1 - \frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{3} - \frac{4\pi}{9\sqrt{3}} = \frac{4}{3} - \frac{4\sqrt{3}\pi}{27}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
