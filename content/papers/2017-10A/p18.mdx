<Problem>
Amelia has a coin that lands heads with probability $\tfrac{1}{3}$, and Blaine has a coin that lands on heads with probability $\tfrac{2}{5}$. Amelia and Blaine alternately toss their coins until someone gets a head; the first one to get a head wins. All coin tosses are independent. Amelia goes first. The probability that Amelia wins is $\tfrac{p}{q}$, where $p$ and $q$ are relatively prime positive integers. What is $q-p$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $A$ = probability Amelia wins. Amelia wins on her first toss with probability $\frac{1}{3}$. If she misses (probability $\frac{2}{3}$) and Blaine also misses (probability $\frac{3}{5}$), the situation resets.

$$A = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{5} \cdot A = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5} A.$$

$$A - \frac{2}{5}A = \frac{1}{3}$$
$$\frac{3}{5}A = \frac{1}{3}$$
$$A = \frac{5}{9}.$$

So $p = 5$, $q = 9$, and $q - p = 9 - 5 = 4$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
