<Problem>
Joy has $30$ thin rods, one each of every integer length from $1$ cm through $30$ cm. She places the rods with lengths $3$ cm, $7$ cm, and $15$ cm on a table. She then wants to choose a fourth rod that she can put with these three to form a quadrilateral with positive area. How many of the remaining rods can she choose as the fourth rod?
</Problem>

<Solution>
For four lengths to form a quadrilateral with positive area, the necessary and sufficient condition is that each side is strictly less than the sum of the other three sides.

Since we want the quadrilateral condition, the binding constraint is: each side $< $ sum of the other three. The hardest constraint to satisfy is for the longest side.

Let the fourth rod have length $d$ with $d \notin \{3, 7, 15\}$ and $1 \le d \le 30$.

The four lengths are $3, 7, 15, d$.

**Case 1: $d < 15$** (so 15 is the longest).
Need $15 < 3 + 7 + d = 10 + d$, i.e., $d > 5$.
So $d \in \{6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14\}$ — that's $8$ values (excluding $7$ from $\{6,7,...,14\}$, but $d \neq 7$ since she already used it). So $d \in \{6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14\}$: $8$ values.

**Case 2: $d = 15$** — already used, not available.

**Case 3: $d > 15$** (so $d$ is the longest).
Need $d < 3 + 7 + 15 = 25$, i.e., $d \le 24$.
So $d \in \{16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24\}$: $9$ values.

**Case 4: $d \le 5$** — fails the condition (e.g., $d=5$: need $15 < 10+5=15$, which is not strict). All of $\{1,2,4,5\}$ (excluding $3$) fail.

Also need $d \neq 3$ (already used). $d=3$ would make 15 the longest: $15 < 3+7+3=13$? No, fails.

Total valid rods: $8 + 9 = 17$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
