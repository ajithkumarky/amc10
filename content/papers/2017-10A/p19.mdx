<Problem>
Alice refuses to sit next to either Bob or Carla. Derek refuses to sit next to Eric. How many ways are there for the five of them to sit in a row of 5 chairs under these conditions?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Use complementary counting. Total arrangements: $5! = 120$.

Define events: $X$ = Alice sits next to Bob, $Y$ = Alice sits next to Carla, $Z$ = Derek sits next to Eric. We want arrangements avoiding all three.

**Single events (glue two people into one block):**

$|X| = |Y| = 4! \times 2 = 48$ and $|Z| = 4! \times 2 = 48$.

**Pairwise intersections:**

$|X \cap Y|$: Alice sits next to both Bob and Carla, so she is sandwiched as B-A-C or C-A-B. This 3-person block (2 internal orders) occupies 3 consecutive seats; the remaining 2 people fill the last 2 seats in $2!$ ways:
$$|X \cap Y| = 2 \times 3 \times 2 = 12.$$

$|X \cap Z|$: AB-block (2 orders) and DE-block (2 orders), with Carla as the third item — 3 items arranged in $3!$ ways:
$$|X \cap Z| = 3! \times 2 \times 2 = 24. \quad |Y \cap Z| = 24 \text{ similarly.}$$

**Triple intersection:**

$|X \cap Y \cap Z|$: The 3-person BAC-block (2 orders) and the DE-block (2 orders) fill all 5 seats in exactly $2!$ arrangements:
$$|X \cap Y \cap Z| = 2! \times 2 \times 2 = 8.$$

**Inclusion-exclusion:**

$$|X \cup Y \cup Z| = (48+48+48) - (12+24+24) + 8 = 144 - 60 + 8 = 92.$$

Valid arrangements: $120 - 92 = 28$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
