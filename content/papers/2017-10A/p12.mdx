<Problem>
Let $S$ be a set of points $(x,y)$ in the coordinate plane such that two of the three quantities $3,~x+2,$ and $y-4$ are equal and the third of the three quantities is no greater than this common value. Which of the following is a correct description for $S$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
There are three cases based on which two quantities are equal and which is the smallest.

**Case 1:** $3 = x+2$ and $y-4 \le 3$.
Then $x = 1$ and $y \le 7$. This is a ray: the portion of the vertical line $x=1$ going downward from $(1, 7)$.

**Case 2:** $3 = y-4$ and $x+2 \le 3$.
Then $y = 7$ and $x \le 1$. This is a ray: the portion of the horizontal line $y=7$ going leftward from $(1, 7)$.

**Case 3:** $x+2 = y-4$ and $3 \le x+2$ (i.e., $x \ge 1$).
Then $y = x + 6$ with $x \ge 1$. This is a ray starting at $(1, 7)$ going up-right along the line $y = x+6$.

All three rays start at the common endpoint $(1, 7)$ (where all three quantities equal $3$). Together they form three rays with a common endpoint.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
