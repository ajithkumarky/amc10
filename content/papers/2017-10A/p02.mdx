<Problem>
Pablo buys popsicles for his friends. The store sells single popsicles for $\$1$ each, $3$-popsicle boxes for $\$2$ each, and $5$-popsicle boxes for $\$3$. What is the greatest number of popsicles that Pablo can buy with $\$8$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
To maximize popsicles, compare price per popsicle: a single costs $\$1$ each (1 per dollar), a 3-pack costs $\$2$ (1.5 per dollar), and a 5-pack costs $\$3$ (about 1.67 per dollar). So 5-packs give the best value.

With $\$8$: buy two 5-packs for $\$6$ (10 popsicles), leaving $\$2$. With the remaining $\$2$, buy one 3-pack (3 popsicles). Total: $10 + 3 = 13$ popsicles.

Can we do better? Two 5-packs + two singles = $\$6 + \$2 = \$8$ gives $10 + 2 = 12$ popsicles. One 5-pack + two 3-packs + single = $\$3 + \$4 + \$1 = \$8$ gives $5 + 6 + 1 = 12$. None beats 13.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
