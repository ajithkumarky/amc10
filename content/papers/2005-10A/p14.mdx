<Problem>
How many three-digit numbers satisfy the property that the middle digit is the average of the first and the last digits?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the three-digit number have digits $a$, $b$, $c$ where $a \in \{1,\ldots,9\}$, $b,c \in \{0,\ldots,9\}$, and $b = \frac{a+c}{2}$.

For $b$ to be an integer, $a+c$ must be even, meaning $a$ and $c$ have the same parity.

**Case 1: $a$ and $c$ are both even.**
$a \in \{2,4,6,8\}$ (4 choices; $a \neq 0$) and $c \in \{0,2,4,6,8\}$ (5 choices). Each pair gives a valid $b = (a+c)/2 \in [1, 8.5]$, so $b \in \{1,\ldots,8\}$, always a digit. Count: $4 \times 5 = 20$.

**Case 2: $a$ and $c$ are both odd.**
$a \in \{1,3,5,7,9\}$ (5 choices) and $c \in \{1,3,5,7,9\}$ (5 choices). Each pair gives $b = (a+c)/2$; the maximum is $(9+9)/2=9$ and minimum is $(1+1)/2=1$, all valid digits. Count: $5 \times 5 = 25$.

Total: $20 + 25 = 45$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
