<Problem>
Two circles of radius $5$ are externally tangent to each other and are internally tangent to a circle of radius $13$ at points $A$ and $B$, as shown. The distance $AB$ can be written in the form $\tfrac{m}{n}$, where $m$ and $n$ are relatively prime positive integers. What is $m + n$?

  <circle cx="-4" cy="-4.5" r="5"/>

</svg>

<Solution>
Let the large circle have center $O$ at the origin with radius $13$. Let the two small circles have centers $O_1$ and $O_2$, each with radius $5$.

**Locating the centers:**

Since each small circle is internally tangent to the large circle, the distance from $O$ to each small center is $13 - 5 = 8$.

Since the two small circles are externally tangent to each other, the distance between their centers is $5 + 5 = 10$.

By symmetry, place $O_1 = (-d, h)$ and $O_2 = (d, h)$ for some $d, h$ with:
- $d^2 + h^2 = 64$ (distance from $O$ to each center is $8$)
- $2d = 10$, so $d = 5$

Then $h^2 = 64 - 25 = 39$, so $h = -\sqrt{39}$ (centers are below the $x$-axis by symmetry with the tangent points $A$ and $B$ further below).

Actually, let's place the centers symmetrically: $O_1 = (-5, -\sqrt{39})$ and $O_2 = (5, -\sqrt{39})$.

**Finding points $A$ and $B$:**

Each small circle is internally tangent to the large circle. The tangent point lies on the line from $O$ through the small circle's center, on the far side from $O$.

For $O_1 = (-5, -\sqrt{39})$: The unit vector from $O$ to $O_1$ is $\frac{1}{8}(-5, -\sqrt{39})$.
Point $A$ is at distance $13$ from $O$ in this direction:
$$A = \frac{13}{8}(-5, -\sqrt{39}) = \left(-\frac{65}{8}, -\frac{13\sqrt{39}}{8}\right).$$

By symmetry, $B = \left(\frac{65}{8}, -\frac{13\sqrt{39}}{8}\right)$.

**Distance $AB$:**

$$AB = \left|\frac{65}{8} - \left(-\frac{65}{8}\right)\right| = \frac{130}{8} = \frac{65}{4}.$$

So $m = 65$, $n = 4$, $\gcd(65, 4) = 1$.

$$m + n = 65 + 4 = 69.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
