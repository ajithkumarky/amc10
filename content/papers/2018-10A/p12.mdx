<Problem>
How many ordered pairs of real numbers $(x, y)$ satisfy the following system of equations?
$$x + 3y = 3$$
$$\big||x| - |y|\big| = 1$$
</Problem>

<Solution>
From the first equation: $x = 3 - 3y$.

Substitute into the second: $\big||3 - 3y| - |y|\big| = 1$.

We consider cases based on the signs of $3 - 3y$ and $y$.

**Case 1: $y \geq 0$ and $3 - 3y \geq 0$, i.e., $0 \leq y \leq 1$.**

$|3 - 3y| = 3 - 3y$ and $|y| = y$, so:
$$|3 - 3y - y| = |3 - 4y| = 1.$$
$3 - 4y = 1 \Rightarrow y = \frac{1}{2}$ (in range). $\checkmark$
$3 - 4y = -1 \Rightarrow y = 1$ (in range, at boundary). $\checkmark$

**Case 2: $y < 0$ (so $3 - 3y > 3 > 0$).**

$|3 - 3y| = 3 - 3y$ and $|y| = -y$, so:
$$|3 - 3y - (-y)| = |3 - 2y| = 1.$$
Since $y < 0$, $3 - 2y > 3 > 1$, so $|3 - 2y| = 3 - 2y = 1 \Rightarrow y = 1$. But $y < 0$ — contradiction. No solutions.

**Case 3: $y > 1$ (so $3 - 3y < 0$).**

$|3 - 3y| = 3y - 3$ and $|y| = y$, so:
$$|3y - 3 - y| = |2y - 3| = 1.$$
$2y - 3 = 1 \Rightarrow y = 2$ (in range $y > 1$). $\checkmark$
$2y - 3 = -1 \Rightarrow y = 1$ (boundary, not in $y > 1$; already covered). 

**Checking all solutions:**

- $y = \frac{1}{2}$: $x = 3 - \frac{3}{2} = \frac{3}{2}$. Pair $\left(\frac{3}{2}, \frac{1}{2}\right)$.
- $y = 1$: $x = 3 - 3 = 0$. Pair $(0, 1)$.
- $y = 2$: $x = 3 - 6 = -3$. Pair $(-3, 2)$.

That gives $3$ ordered pairs.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
