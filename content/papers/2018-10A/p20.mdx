<Problem>
A scanning code consists of a $7 \times 7$ grid of squares, with some of its squares colored black and the rest colored white. There must be at least one square of each color in this grid of $49$ squares. A scanning code is called $\textit{symmetric}$ if its look does not change when the entire square is rotated by a multiple of $90^\circ$ counterclockwise around its center, nor when it is reflected across a line joining opposite corners or a line joining midpoints of opposite sides. What is the total number of possible symmetric scanning codes?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A scanning code is symmetric under the full dihedral group $D_4$ (all symmetries of the square: $4$ rotations and $4$ reflections). Under this group, the $49$ squares are partitioned into orbits (groups of squares that must all have the same color).

**Finding the orbits:**

For a $7 \times 7$ grid with $D_4$ symmetry:
- The center square forms an orbit of size $1$.
- Squares on the axes of symmetry (but not the center) form orbits. For a $7 \times 7$ grid (odd dimensions), the middle row, middle column, and diagonals have special behavior.
- Other squares form orbits of size $8$.

Label positions $(i, j)$ with $i, j \in \{-3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ (so the center is $(0,0)$).

**Orbit types:**
1. **Center $(0,0)$**: orbit of size $1$. (1 orbit)
2. **Axis squares (on the 4 axes of symmetry but not center)**: The 4 axes are the two diagonals and the horizontal/vertical through the center.
   - Horizontal axis: $(0, \pm 1), (0, \pm 2), (0, \pm 3)$ — but under $D_4$, $(0, k)$ maps to $(k, 0)$ (rotation by $90^\circ$). So $\{(0,k), (k,0), (0,-k), (-k,0)\}$ are in one orbit of size $4$ for $k = 1, 2, 3$. (3 orbits of size 4)
   - Diagonal axis: $(k, k)$ maps to $(-k, k)$ (reflection), then to $(-k, -k)$, then $(k, -k)$. So $\{(k,k), (-k,k), (-k,-k), (k,-k)\}$ form orbits of size $4$ for $k = 1, 2, 3$. (3 orbits of size 4)
3. **Off-axis squares**: All other $(i, j)$ with $i \neq 0$, $j \neq 0$, $|i| \neq |j|$ form orbits of size $8$. 

**Counting orbits of size 8:**

Total squares $= 49$.
Squares in size-1 orbits: $1$ (the center).
Squares in size-4 orbits: $6 \times 4 = 24$ (from the 6 orbits above).
Squares in size-8 orbits: $49 - 1 - 24 = 24$, giving $24/8 = 3$ orbits of size $8$.

Total orbits $= 1 + 6 + 3 = 10$.

**Counting symmetric codes:**

Each orbit can independently be colored black or white, giving $2^{10} = 1024$ colorings. But we exclude the $2$ all-same-color cases (all black or all white) since we need at least one of each color.

$$1024 - 2 = 1022.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
