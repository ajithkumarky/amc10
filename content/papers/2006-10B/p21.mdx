<Problem>
For a particular peculiar pair of dice, the probabilities of rolling $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, $5$, and $6$ on each die are in the ratio $1:2:3:4:5:6$. What is the probability of rolling a total of $7$ on the two dice?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The probabilities sum to 1. With ratio $1:2:3:4:5:6$, the total ratio is $1+2+3+4+5+6 = 21$. So $P(\text{roll } k) = \dfrac{k}{21}$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$.

The pairs summing to 7 are $(1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1)$.

$$P(\text{sum} = 7) = \sum_{k=1}^{6} P(k) \cdot P(7-k) = \sum_{k=1}^{6} \frac{k}{21} \cdot \frac{7-k}{21} = \frac{1}{441}\sum_{k=1}^{6} k(7-k).$$

Compute $\sum_{k=1}^{6} k(7-k)$:
- $k=1$: $1 \cdot 6 = 6$
- $k=2$: $2 \cdot 5 = 10$
- $k=3$: $3 \cdot 4 = 12$
- $k=4$: $4 \cdot 3 = 12$
- $k=5$: $5 \cdot 2 = 10$
- $k=6$: $6 \cdot 1 = 6$

Sum $= 56$.

$$P(\text{sum} = 7) = \frac{56}{441} = \frac{8}{63}.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
