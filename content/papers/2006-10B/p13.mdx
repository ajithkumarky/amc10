<Problem>
Joe and JoAnn each bought 12 ounces of coffee in a 16-ounce cup. Joe drank 2 ounces of his coffee and then added 2 ounces of cream. JoAnn added 2 ounces of cream, stirred the coffee well, and then drank 2 ounces. What is the resulting ratio of the amount of cream in Joe's coffee to that in JoAnn's coffee?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Joe's cup:** He drinks 2 oz of pure coffee, leaving 10 oz of coffee. He then adds 2 oz of cream. Joe ends with 12 oz total: 10 oz coffee and **2 oz cream**.

**JoAnn's cup:** She adds 2 oz of cream to 12 oz of coffee, giving 14 oz total with a cream fraction of $\dfrac{2}{14} = \dfrac{1}{7}$. She then drinks 2 oz of this well-mixed mixture, removing $2 \times \dfrac{1}{7} = \dfrac{2}{7}$ oz of cream. JoAnn ends with $2 - \dfrac{2}{7} = \dfrac{12}{7}$ oz of cream.

The ratio of cream in Joe's cup to cream in JoAnn's cup:
$$\frac{2}{12/7} = \frac{2 \times 7}{12} = \frac{14}{12} = \frac{7}{6}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
