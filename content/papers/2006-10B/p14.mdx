<Problem>
Let $a$ and $b$ be the roots of the equation $x^2 - mx + 2 = 0$. Suppose that $a + \dfrac{1}{b}$ and $b + \dfrac{1}{a}$ are the roots of the equation $x^2 - px + q = 0$. What is $q$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
By Vieta's formulas for $x^2 - mx + 2 = 0$: $a + b = m$ and $ab = 2$.

The product of the roots of the second equation is $q$:
$$q = \left(a + \frac{1}{b}\right)\left(b + \frac{1}{a}\right) = ab + 1 + 1 + \frac{1}{ab} = ab + 2 + \frac{1}{ab}.$$

Substituting $ab = 2$:
$$q = 2 + 2 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{9}{2}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
