<Problem>
Rhombus $ABCD$ is similar to rhombus $BFDE$. The area of rhombus $ABCD$ is $24$ and $\angle BAD = 60^\circ$. What is the area of rhombus $BFDE$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the side length of rhombus $ABCD$ be $s$. Its area is $s^2 \sin 60^\circ = 24$.

**Diagonals of $ABCD$:** In a rhombus, $\angle BAD = 60^\circ$ means $\angle ABC = 120^\circ$.

Shorter diagonal $BD$ (bisected by the $60^\circ$ vertex angles):
$$BD^2 = s^2 + s^2 - 2s^2\cos 60^\circ = 2s^2\!\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = s^2, \quad \text{so } BD = s.$$

Longer diagonal $AC$ (opposite to $\angle BAD = 60^\circ$, spanning the obtuse angle):
$$AC^2 = s^2 + s^2 - 2s^2\cos 120^\circ = 2s^2\!\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 3s^2, \quad \text{so } AC = s\sqrt{3}.$$

**Identifying the diagonal of $BFDE$:** The rhombus $BFDE$ is inscribed with $B$ and $D$ as opposite vertices, so $BD$ is a diagonal of $BFDE$. Since $BFDE$ is smaller than $ABCD$ and similar to it, the diagonal $BD = s$ of $BFDE$ corresponds to the longer diagonal $AC = s\sqrt{3}$ of $ABCD$.

**Ratio of similarity:**
$$k = \frac{BD}{AC} = \frac{s}{s\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}.$$

**Area of $BFDE$:**
$$[\text{BFDE}] = k^2 \cdot [\text{ABCD}] = \frac{1}{3} \times 24 = 8.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
