<Problem>
A square of area 40 is inscribed in a semicircle as shown. The square sits with its base on the diameter of the semicircle, and the two upper corners of the square touch the semicircle. What is the area of the semicircle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The square has area 40, so its side length is $\sqrt{40} = 2\sqrt{10}$.

Place the center of the semicircle at the origin. The square's base lies along the diameter, centered at the origin, so the base runs from $(-\sqrt{10}, 0)$ to $(\sqrt{10}, 0)$. The top two corners of the square are at $(\sqrt{10}, 2\sqrt{10})$ and $(-\sqrt{10}, 2\sqrt{10})$.

Each top corner lies on the semicircle of radius $r$, so:
$$r^2 = (\sqrt{10})^2 + (2\sqrt{10})^2 = 10 + 40 = 50.$$

The area of the semicircle is $\dfrac{\pi r^2}{2} = \dfrac{50\pi}{2} = 25\pi$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
