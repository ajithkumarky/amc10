<Problem>
Let $N$ be the product of all the positive integer divisors of $42$. What is the units digit of $N$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The prime factorization of $42$ is $2 \times 3 \times 7$, which has $(1+1)(1+1)(1+1) = 8$ divisors:

$$1,\; 2,\; 3,\; 6,\; 7,\; 14,\; 21,\; 42.$$

A useful trick: divisors of $n$ pair up as $(d, n/d)$, each pair multiplying to $n$. With 8 divisors, we get 4 pairs:

$$1 \times 42 = 42, \quad 2 \times 21 = 42, \quad 3 \times 14 = 42, \quad 6 \times 7 = 42.$$

So $N = 42^4$.

To find the units digit of $42^4$: the units digit of powers of $42$ equals the units digit of powers of $2$, which cycles $2, 4, 8, 6, 2, 4, \ldots$ with period 4. Since $4 \equiv 0 \pmod{4}$, we use the 4th value in the cycle: $6$.

The units digit of $N$ is $6$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
