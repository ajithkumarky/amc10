<Problem>
In the following expression, Melanie changed some of the plus signs to minus signs:

$$1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + \cdots + 97 + 99$$

When the new expression was evaluated, it was negative. What is the least number of plus signs that Melanie could have changed to minus signs?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The original sum is $1+3+5+\cdots+99$. This is the sum of the first 50 odd numbers, equal to $50^2 = 2500$.

If Melanie flips a term $v$ from $+$ to $-$, the total decreases by $2v$. If she flips $k$ terms with values $v_1 \ge v_2 \ge \cdots \ge v_k$ (choosing the largest to minimize $k$), the new sum is $2500 - 2(v_1+\cdots+v_k)$.

For this to be negative, we need $v_1+\cdots+v_k > 1250$.

To flip as few terms as possible, pick the $k$ largest odd numbers: $99, 97, 95, \ldots$ Their sum is $99k - 2(0+1+\cdots+(k-1)) = 99k - k(k-1) = k(100-k)$.

- $k=14$: $14 \cdot 86 = 1204 \le 1250$. Not enough.
- $k=15$: $15 \cdot 85 = 1275 > 1250$. This works.

The least number of changes is $\boxed{15}$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
