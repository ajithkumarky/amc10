<Problem>
Quadrilateral $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, and $E$ is the midpoint of the side $\overline{AD}$. Let $F$ be the intersection of lines $EB$ and $AC$. What is the ratio of the area of quadrilateral $CDEF$ to the area of triangle $CFB$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place coordinates: $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,0)$, $C=(1,1)$, $D=(0,1)$. Then $E$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AD}$, so $E=(0,\tfrac{1}{2})$.

Find $F$, the intersection of lines $EB$ and $AC$.

Line $AC$: from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$, parametrized as $(s,s)$.

Line $EB$: from $E=(0,\tfrac{1}{2})$ to $B=(1,0)$. Direction $(1,-\tfrac{1}{2})$, so points are $(t,\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{t}{2})$.

Set equal: $s=t$ and $s = \tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{s}{2}$, giving $\tfrac{3s}{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}$, so $s=\tfrac{1}{3}$.

Thus $F=\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr)$.

Area of quadrilateral $CDEF$ (vertices $C=(1,1)$, $D=(0,1)$, $E=(0,\tfrac{1}{2})$, $F=(\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3})$) by shoelace:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl|(1\cdot1 - 0\cdot1)+(0\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}-0\cdot1)+(0\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}-\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2})+(\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot1-1\cdot\tfrac{1}{3})\bigr|$$
$$= \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl|1 + 0 - \tfrac{1}{6} + 0\bigr| = \tfrac{5}{12}.$$

Area of triangle $CFB$ (vertices $C=(1,1)$, $F=(\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{1}{3})$, $B=(1,0)$):

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl|1(\tfrac{1}{3}-0)+\tfrac{1}{3}(0-1)+1(1-\tfrac{1}{3})\bigr| = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl|\tfrac{1}{3}-\tfrac{1}{3}+\tfrac{2}{3}\bigr| = \tfrac{1}{3}.$$

Ratio:

$$\frac{\tfrac{5}{12}}{\tfrac{1}{3}} = \frac{5}{12}\cdot 3 = \frac{5}{4}.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
