<Problem>
How many non-similar triangles have angles whose degree measures are distinct positive integers in arithmetic progression?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the three angles be $a - d$, $a$, $a + d$ with $d > 0$ (since they must be distinct) and all positive integers.

Their sum equals $180°$:
$$(a-d) + a + (a+d) = 3a = 180 \implies a = 60.$$

So the angles are $60 - d$, $60$, $60 + d$ for positive integer $d$.

Constraints:
- All angles are positive: $60 - d > 0 \Rightarrow d < 60$.
- All angles are distinct positive integers: $d \geq 1$.
- Largest angle less than 180: $60 + d < 180 \Rightarrow d < 120$ (already satisfied by $d < 60$).

So $d$ can be $1, 2, 3, \ldots, 59$, giving **59** non-similar triangles.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
