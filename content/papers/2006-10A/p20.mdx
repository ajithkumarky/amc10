<Problem>
Six distinct positive integers are randomly chosen between 1 and 2006, inclusive. What is the probability that some pair of these integers has a difference that is a multiple of 5?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Every integer has a remainder of 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4 when divided by 5 — that is, it belongs to one of 5 residue classes mod 5.

We are choosing 6 integers. By the **Pigeonhole Principle**, with 6 integers and only 5 residue classes, at least two of the chosen integers must share the same residue class mod 5. Two integers with the same remainder mod 5 have a difference divisible by 5.

This holds for **every** possible selection of 6 integers — it is guaranteed, not just probable. Therefore the probability is $1$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
