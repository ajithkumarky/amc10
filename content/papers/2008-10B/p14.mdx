<Problem>
Triangle $OAB$ has $O = (0,0)$, $B = (5,0)$, and $A$ in the first quadrant. In addition, $\angle ABO = 90^\circ$ and $\angle AOB = 30^\circ$. Suppose that $OA$ is rotated $90^\circ$ counterclockwise about $O$. What are the coordinates of the image of $A$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since $\angle AOB = 30^\circ$ and $B = (5, 0)$, the point $A$ lies along the ray from $O$ at angle $30^\circ$.

With $\angle ABO = 90^\circ$, triangle $OAB$ is right-angled at $B$, so $AB \perp OB$. Since $OB$ lies along the $x$-axis, $AB$ is vertical. Thus $A$ has the same $x$-coordinate as $B$, giving $A = (5, y)$ for some $y > 0$.

Since $\angle AOB = 30^\circ$: $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{AB}{OB} = \frac{y}{5}$, so $y = 5\tan 30^\circ = \frac{5}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

Thus $A = \left(5, \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}\right)$.

Rotating a point $(x, y)$ by $90^\circ$ counterclockwise gives $(-y, x)$:
$$A' = \left(-\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}, 5\right).$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
