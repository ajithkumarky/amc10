<Problem>
Three red beads, two white beads, and one blue bead are placed in line in random order. What is the probability that no two neighboring beads are the same color?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Total arrangements: $\frac{6!}{3!\,2!\,1!} = \frac{720}{12} = 60$.

For no two neighbors to share a color, consecutive beads must differ. Since there are 3 red beads, no two reds can be adjacent, so the reds must occupy positions from the set $\{1,3,5\}$ or $\{1,3,6\}$ or $\{1,4,6\}$ or $\{2,4,6\}$ (alternating or spread positions).

Let the 6 positions be labeled 1–6. The red beads go in 3 of the 6 positions with no two adjacent; the remaining 3 positions get 2 white and 1 blue.

Count placements of 3 non-adjacent reds in 6 positions: the valid position sets are $\{1,3,5\}$, $\{1,3,6\}$, $\{1,4,6\}$, $\{2,4,6\}$. That's 4 sets.

(Check: $\{2,4,6\}$, $\{1,3,5\}$ are obvious; $\{1,3,6\}$: positions 3 and 6 differ by 3, fine; $\{1,4,6\}$: positions 4 and 6 differ by 2, fine. Any others? $\{2,4,5\}$: 4 and 5 adjacent — no. $\{1,2,4\}$: 1 and 2 adjacent — no. So exactly 4.)

For each such red placement, the remaining 3 positions receive 2 white and 1 blue with no two whites adjacent (since white-white would be neighboring same-color beads). For each of the 4 red placements, check how many of the $\frac{3!}{2!} = 3$ arrangements of W, W, B in the 3 remaining positions avoid adjacent whites.

The 3 remaining positions in each case are non-consecutive (since the reds fill alternating-style slots). Let's verify for $\{1,3,5\}$: remaining positions are $2, 4, 6$ — none adjacent to each other. Any arrangement of W, W, B in those 3 positions works since positions 2, 4, 6 are mutually non-adjacent. So all 3 arrangements are valid.

For $\{1,3,6\}$: remaining positions are $2, 4, 5$ — positions 4 and 5 are adjacent. The arrangement that puts W in both 4 and 5 is invalid. The 3 arrangements of (W, W, B) in positions $(2,4,5)$ are: (W,W,B), (W,B,W), (B,W,W). The first puts both W's at positions 4 and 5 — adjacent, invalid. The other two are valid. So 2 valid arrangements.

By symmetry, $\{1,4,6\}$ also gives 2 valid arrangements (remaining positions 2, 3, 5 — positions 2 and 3 are adjacent; arrangement B,W,W at these is invalid). And $\{2,4,6\}$ gives 3 valid arrangements (remaining positions 1, 3, 5 — none adjacent).

Total valid arrangements: $3 + 2 + 2 + 3 = 10$.

Probability: $\frac{10}{60} = \frac{1}{6}$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
