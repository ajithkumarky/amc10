<Problem>
Ten chairs are evenly spaced around a round table and numbered clockwise from $1$ through $10$. Five married couples are to sit in the chairs with men and women alternating, and no one is to sit either next to or across from his/her spouse. How many seating arrangements are possible?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since chairs are numbered (labeled), positions are distinguishable — no rotational equivalence to divide out.

**Step 1: Assign genders to seats.** With alternating genders, men occupy either the odd seats $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ or the even seats $\{2,4,6,8,10\}$. That gives $2$ gender assignments.

**Step 2: Arrange men.** The 5 men can be placed in their 5 seats in $5! = 120$ ways.

**Step 3: Count valid women arrangements.** Fix the men's positions. Each woman must avoid sitting next to or across from her husband. In the labeled table, each odd seat $2k-1$ is adjacent to even seats $2k-2$ and $2k$ (mod 10), and directly across from even seat $2k+4$ (mod 10). So each woman has exactly 3 forbidden even seats.

By careful inclusion-exclusion (or the ménage-type formula for this circular forbidden structure), the number of permutations of 5 women into 5 even seats avoiding all forbidden pairs equals $2$.

**Total:** $2 \times 120 \times 2 = 480$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
