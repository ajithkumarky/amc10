<Problem>
A sequence of three real numbers forms an arithmetic progression with a first term of $9$. If $2$ is added to the second term and $20$ is added to the third term, the three resulting numbers form a geometric progression. What is the smallest possible value for the third term of the geometric progression?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the arithmetic progression be $9, 9+d, 9+2d$ for some common difference $d$.

After the modifications, the geometric progression is $9, 9+d+2, 9+2d+20 = 9, d+11, 2d+29$.

For a geometric progression, the square of the middle term equals the product of the outer terms:
$$(d+11)^2 = 9(2d+29).$$
$$d^2 + 22d + 121 = 18d + 261.$$
$$d^2 + 4d - 140 = 0.$$
$$(d+14)(d-10) = 0.$$
$$d = -14 \text{ or } d = 10.$$

The third term of the geometric progression is $2d + 29$:
- $d = -14$: third term $= 2(-14) + 29 = 1$.
- $d = 10$: third term $= 2(10) + 29 = 49$.

The smallest possible value is $1$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
