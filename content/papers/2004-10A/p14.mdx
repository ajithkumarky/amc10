<Problem>
The average value of all the pennies, nickels, dimes, and quarters in Paula's purse is $20$ cents. If she had one more quarter, the average would be $21$ cents. How many dimes does she have in her purse?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $n$ be the number of coins currently in her purse and $S$ the total value in cents. Then:
$$\frac{S}{n} = 20 \implies S = 20n.$$

With one more quarter ($25$ cents):
$$\frac{S + 25}{n + 1} = 21 \implies S + 25 = 21(n+1) = 21n + 21.$$

Substituting $S = 20n$:
$$20n + 25 = 21n + 21 \implies n = 4.$$

So Paula has $4$ coins worth a total of $80$ cents. The only way to make $80$ cents with $4$ coins using pennies, nickels, dimes, and quarters, where the average is $20$ cents each, is four $20$-cent... but there's no $20$-cent coin. We need four coins summing to $80$ cents.

With $4$ quarters: $100 \neq 80$. Try $3$ quarters $+ 1$ nickel $= 80$ cents. Yes! $3(25) + 5 = 80$. This uses $0$ dimes.

(No other combination of 4 coins from pennies/nickels/dimes/quarters sums to $80$ and contains $0$ dimes — verify: $2$ quarters $+$ dime $+$ something: $50+10+x=80 \Rightarrow x=20$, not a valid coin. $2$ quarters $+$ dime $+$ dime: $70 \neq 80$. The solution $3$ quarters $+$ nickel is the unique valid one.)

Paula has $0$ dimes.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
