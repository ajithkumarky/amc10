<Problem>
Two distinct lines pass through the center of three concentric circles of radii $3$, $2$, and $1$. The area of the shaded region in the diagram is $\dfrac{8}{13}$ of the area of the unshaded region. The two lines form an acute angle $\theta$ (in radians) at the center, creating sectors. The shading alternates: within the innermost circle ($r=1$), the sectors along the acute angle are shaded; between $r=1$ and $r=2$, the sectors along the obtuse angle are shaded; between $r=2$ and $r=3$, the sectors along the acute angle are shaded. What is $\theta$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $\theta$ be the acute angle between the two lines ($0 < \theta < \pi/2$). The two lines divide each ring into sectors subtending angles $\theta$ and $\pi - \theta$; the two lines together create two $\theta$-sectors and two $(\pi-\theta)$-sectors, so the "acute" fraction of any disk is $\dfrac{2\theta}{2\pi} = \dfrac{\theta}{\pi}$ and the "obtuse" fraction is $\dfrac{\pi - \theta}{\pi}$.

The three regions and their shaded fractions are:
- Inner disk ($r \leq 1$, area $\pi$): acute sectors shaded, shaded area $= \theta$.
- Middle annulus ($1 < r \leq 2$, area $3\pi$): obtuse sectors shaded, shaded area $= 3(\pi - \theta)$.
- Outer annulus ($2 < r \leq 3$, area $5\pi$): acute sectors shaded, shaded area $= 5\theta$.

Total shaded $= \theta + 3(\pi - \theta) + 5\theta = 3\theta + 3\pi$.

Total area $= 9\pi$, so total unshaded $= 9\pi - (3\theta + 3\pi) = 6\pi - 3\theta$.

Setting shaded $= \dfrac{8}{13} \times$ unshaded:
$$3\theta + 3\pi = \frac{8}{13}(6\pi - 3\theta).$$
$$13(3\theta + 3\pi) = 8(6\pi - 3\theta).$$
$$39\theta + 39\pi = 48\pi - 24\theta.$$
$$63\theta = 9\pi \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{7}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
