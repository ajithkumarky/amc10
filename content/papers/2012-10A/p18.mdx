<Problem>
The closed curve in the figure is made up of $9$ congruent circular arcs each of length $\frac{2\pi}{3}$, where each of the centers of the corresponding circles is among the vertices of a regular hexagon of side $2$. What is the area enclosed by the curve?

The curve has $6$ outward-bulging arcs (two arcs centered at each of $3$ alternating hexagon vertices) and $3$ inward-cutting arcs (one arc centered at each of the other $3$ vertices).
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Step 1: Find the arc radius.** From the Asymptote source, the arc radius is half the hexagon side: $r = 1$. The arc length is $r\theta = 1 \cdot \frac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ ✓, so $\theta = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ ($120°$).

**Step 2: Decompose the area.** Start with the regular hexagon of side $2$, which has area $6\sqrt{3}$.

The $6$ outward arcs each add a circular segment, and the $3$ inward arcs each remove a circular segment. Each segment corresponds to a $120°$ arc of a unit circle, with segment area:
$$\text{sector} - \text{triangle} = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{3} - \sin 120°\right) = \tfrac{\pi}{3} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}.$$

**Step 3: Account for triangular corner regions.** Each of the $3$ outward-bulge vertices hosts $2$ arcs that together, along with the vertex itself, enclose a small equilateral triangle of side $1$ (area $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$) that lies outside the hexagon but inside the curve. These $3$ triangles must be added separately.

**Step 4: Total area.**
$$6\sqrt{3} + 6\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right) - 3\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right) + 3\!\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$$
$$= 6\sqrt{3} + 3\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right) + \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}$$
$$= 6\sqrt{3} + \pi - \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} + \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4} = \pi + 6\sqrt{3}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
