<Problem>
Externally tangent circles with centers at points $A$ and $B$ have radii of lengths $5$ and $3$, respectively. A line externally tangent to both circles intersects ray $AB$ at point $C$. What is $BC$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
An external tangent to two circles meets the line through the centers at a point $C$ that divides the segment externally in the ratio of the radii.

Since the circles are externally tangent, $AB = 5 + 3 = 8$. Point $C$ lies on ray $AB$ beyond $B$ (the smaller circle), and satisfies $\frac{CA}{CB} = \frac{5}{3}$ by similar triangles (the perpendiculars from $A$ and $B$ to the tangent line have lengths 5 and 3 respectively).

Let $BC = x$. Then $CA = x + 8$, and:
$$\frac{x + 8}{x} = \frac{5}{3} \implies 3(x+8) = 5x \implies 3x + 24 = 5x \implies x = 12.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
