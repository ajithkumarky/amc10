<Problem>
Mary divides a circle into $12$ sectors. The central angles of these sectors, measured in degrees, are all integers and they form an arithmetic sequence. What is the degree measure of the smallest possible sector angle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the 12 angles be an arithmetic sequence with first term $a$ and common difference $d$ (both integers, with $d \ge 0$ since we want the smallest angle to be as small as possible). Their sum must equal $360°$:
$$12a + d(0+1+\cdots+11) = 360 \implies 12a + 66d = 360 \implies 2a + 11d = 60.$$

All angles must be positive, so $a \ge 1$. To minimize $a$, maximize $d$:
$$a = \frac{60 - 11d}{2}.$$

For $a$ to be a positive integer, $60 - 11d$ must be a positive even integer. Since $11d$ is odd when $d$ is odd and even when $d$ is even, we need $d$ even for $a$ to be an integer.

With $d = 4$: $a = \frac{60 - 44}{2} = 8$.
With $d = 6$: $a = \frac{60 - 66}{2} = -3 < 0$. Not valid.

So the smallest valid $d$ giving a positive $a$ with $d$ even is $d = 4$, giving $a = 8$.

Checking odd $d$: $11d$ must be odd, so $60 - 11d$ is odd, not divisible by 2. So odd $d$ gives non-integer $a$.

The smallest possible sector angle is $\mathbf{8}$ degrees.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
