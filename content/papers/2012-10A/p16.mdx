<Problem>
Three runners start running simultaneously from the same point on a $500$-meter circular track. They each run clockwise around the course maintaining constant speeds of $4.4$, $4.8$, and $5.0$ meters per second. The runners stop once they are all together again somewhere on the circular course. How many seconds do the runners run?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Two runners meet when the faster one has lapped the slower one — i.e., when the difference in distance traveled equals a multiple of 500 m.

Runner A at 4.4 m/s, Runner B at 4.8 m/s, Runner C at 5.0 m/s.

**A and B meet** when $(4.8 - 4.4)t = 500k$ for positive integer $k$, i.e., $0.4t$ is a multiple of 500, so $t$ is a multiple of 1250 seconds.

**A and C meet** when $(5.0 - 4.4)t = 500k$, i.e., $0.6t$ is a multiple of 500, so $t$ is a multiple of $\frac{500}{0.6} = \frac{2500}{3}$ seconds.

**B and C meet** when $(5.0 - 4.8)t = 500k$, i.e., $0.2t$ is a multiple of 500, so $t$ is a multiple of 2500 seconds.

All three meet when $t$ is a common multiple of 1250, $\frac{2500}{3}$, and 2500.

To find the LCM, write $1250 = \frac{2500}{2}$ and $\frac{2500}{3}$. We need $t = \frac{2500}{1} \cdot \text{LCM}(1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3})$... 

More cleanly: multiply all speeds by 10 to use integers. The rates become 44, 48, 50 (in units of 0.1 m/s) on a 500 m track.

Runner A covers $44 \cdot 0.1 \cdot t = 4.4t$ meters. All meet when:
- $0.4t \equiv 0 \pmod{500}$, so $t = 1250s$ for A,B
- $0.6t \equiv 0 \pmod{500}$, so $t = \frac{2500}{3}s$ for A,C  
- $0.2t \equiv 0 \pmod{500}$, so $t = 2500s$ for B,C

The least common multiple of 1250 and $\frac{2500}{3}$: we need $t = 1250a = \frac{2500b}{3}$ for positive integers $a,b$. So $3a = 2b$, meaning $a = 2, b = 3$ gives $t = 2500$.

Check: $0.6 \times 2500 = 1500 = 3 \times 500$ ✓, and $0.2 \times 2500 = 500$ ✓.

All three runners are together again at $t = 2500$ seconds.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
