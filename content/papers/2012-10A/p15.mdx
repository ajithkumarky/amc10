<Problem>
Three unit squares and two line segments connecting two pairs of vertices are shown. What is the area of $\triangle ABC$?

The figure shows three unit squares: two side by side in a row (occupying columns $[0,1]$ and $[1,2]$, row $[0,-1]$) and one below the left square (column $[0,1]$, row $[-1,-2]$). Point $A = (0,0)$ is the top-left corner of the arrangement and $B = (1,0)$ is the top-right corner of the left square. The two line segments are: one from $A=(0,0)$ to $(2,-1)$ (the bottom-right corner of the two-square row) and one from $B=(1,0)$ to $(0,-2)$ (the bottom-left corner of the bottom square). Point $C$ is the intersection of these two segments.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Set up coordinates as described: $A = (0,0)$ and $B = (1,0)$.

**Line through $A=(0,0)$ and $(2,-1)$:** slope $= -\tfrac{1}{2}$, so $y = -\tfrac{x}{2}$.

**Line through $B=(1,0)$ and $(0,-2)$:** slope $= \tfrac{-2-0}{0-1} = 2$, so $y = 2(x-1) = 2x-2$.

**Find $C$:** Set $-\tfrac{x}{2} = 2x - 2$, giving $-x = 4x - 4$, so $x = \tfrac{4}{5}$ and $y = -\tfrac{2}{5}$. Thus $C = \bigl(\tfrac{4}{5}, -\tfrac{2}{5}\bigr)$.

**Area of $\triangle ABC$** with $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,0)$, $C=\bigl(\tfrac{4}{5},-\tfrac{2}{5}\bigr)$:

The base $AB$ has length 1 (along the $x$-axis) and the height is the perpendicular distance from $C$ to the $x$-axis, which is $\tfrac{2}{5}$.

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1 \times \tfrac{2}{5} = \tfrac{1}{5}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
