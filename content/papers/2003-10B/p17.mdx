<Problem>
An ice cream cone consists of a sphere of vanilla ice cream and a right circular cone that has the same diameter as the sphere. If the ice cream melts, it will exactly fill the cone. Assume that the melted ice cream occupies $75\%$ of the volume of the frozen ice cream. What is the ratio of the cone's height to its radius? (Note: a cone with radius $r$ and height $h$ has volume $\pi r^2 h / 3$ and a sphere with radius $r$ has volume $4 \pi r^3 / 3$.)
</Problem>

<Solution>
The sphere and cone share the same radius $r$. The volume of the sphere is $\dfrac{4\pi r^3}{3}$.

When the ice cream melts, its volume becomes $75\% = \dfrac{3}{4}$ of the frozen volume:
$$V_{\text{melted}} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{4\pi r^3}{3} = \pi r^3.$$

This melted ice cream exactly fills the cone:
$$\frac{\pi r^2 h}{3} = \pi r^3.$$

Dividing both sides by $\pi r^2$:
$$\frac{h}{3} = r \implies h = 3r \implies \frac{h}{r} = 3.$$

The ratio of height to radius is $3:1$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
