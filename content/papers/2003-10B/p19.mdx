<Problem>
Three semicircles of radius $1$ are constructed on diameter $\overline{AB}$ of a semicircle of radius $2$. The centers of the small semicircles divide $\overline{AB}$ into four line segments of equal length, as shown. What is the area of the shaded region that lies within the large semicircle but outside the smaller semicircles?

The large semicircle has diameter $AB = 4$ (radius $2$). The three small semicircles of radius $1$ have centers at $-1$, $0$, and $1$ on $\overline{AB}$, with the leftmost and rightmost pointing upward and the middle one pointing upward as well.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place $A = (-2, 0)$ and $B = (2, 0)$. The three small semicircles each have radius $1$, centered at $(-1, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, and $(1, 0)$, all opening upward.

**Large semicircle area:** $\dfrac{1}{2}\pi(2)^2 = 2\pi$.

**Area of the three small semicircles:** Each small semicircle has area $\dfrac{1}{2}\pi(1)^2 = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so together they contribute $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$.

However, the middle small semicircle overlaps with each of the outer ones. The centers of adjacent small semicircles are $2$ apart and each has radius $1$, so the circles are tangent — the semicircles only share a single boundary point, contributing zero overlapping area.

But from the figure, part of the middle semicircle lies outside the large semicircle (it does not — the large semicircle fully contains all three small ones since they are all radius $1$ fitting inside radius $2$).

**Naively:** Shaded area $= 2\pi - \dfrac{3\pi}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. This doesn't match any choice.

Looking more carefully at the Asymptote code: the middle small semicircle is drawn with dashed segments from $60°$ to $120°$, suggesting parts of it are outside the large semicircle. Actually the middle semicircle is centered at the origin with radius $1$, which is entirely inside the radius-$2$ semicircle.

The key is the overlap between adjacent small semicircles. Adjacent centers are $2$ units apart and each has radius $1$, so they are externally tangent — zero overlap area. The three semicircles are disjoint.

**Revisiting:** The figure shows the middle semicircle with some dashed arcs. From the code, certain arc segments of the middle semicircle are dashed (arcs from $0°$–$60°$ and $120°$–$180°$), while the arc from $60°$–$120°$ is solid. This suggests the middle semicircle overlaps with the left and right ones — but wait, with centers $2$ apart and radius $1$ each, the circles are tangent.

Actually, re-reading: the middle semicircle is centered at $(0,0)$ and the outer ones at $(\pm1, 0)$. Distance from $(0,0)$ to $(1,0)$ is $1$, and $r_1 + r_2 = 2 > 1$, so they overlap! The circles overlap when the distance between centers is less than the sum of radii.

Distance between adjacent centers $= 1$, sum of radii $= 2 > 1$, so the semicircles significantly overlap.

**Overlap area between the middle semicircle and each outer one:** Two unit circles with centers $1$ apart. The intersection area of two unit circles with centers $d = 1$ apart is:
$$2\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{d}{2}\right) - \frac{d}{2}\sqrt{4 - d^2} = 2\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} = 2 \cdot \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

This is the intersection of the full circles. Since all relevant arcs are in the upper half-plane (all semicircles open upward), the intersection of two adjacent semicircles is half the full circle intersection:
$$\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}.$$

By inclusion-exclusion, the total area covered by the three small semicircles is:
$$3 \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} - 2\left(\frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right) = \frac{3\pi}{2} - \frac{2\pi}{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{9\pi - 4\pi}{6} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{5\pi}{6} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

(The left and right semicircles don't overlap each other: centers are $2$ apart, distance equals $2r$, so they are tangent.)

**Shaded area:**
$$2\pi - \left(\frac{5\pi}{6} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = 2\pi - \frac{5\pi}{6} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{12\pi - 5\pi}{6} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{7\pi}{6} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
