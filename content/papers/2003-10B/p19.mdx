<Problem>
Three semicircles of radius $1$ are constructed on diameter $\overline{AB}$ of a semicircle of radius $2$. The centers of the small semicircles divide $\overline{AB}$ into four line segments of equal length, as shown. What is the area of the shaded region that lies within the large semicircle but outside the smaller semicircles?

The large semicircle has diameter $AB = 4$ (radius $2$). The three small semicircles of radius $1$ have centers at $-1$, $0$, and $1$ on $\overline{AB}$, with the leftmost and rightmost pointing upward and the middle one pointing upward as well.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place $A = (-2, 0)$ and $B = (2, 0)$. The three small semicircles each have radius $1$, centered at $(-1, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, and $(1, 0)$, all opening upward.

**Large semicircle area:** $\dfrac{1}{2}\pi(2)^2 = 2\pi$.

**Overlaps between adjacent small semicircles:** The middle semicircle (center $O = (0,0)$, radius $1$) and each outer semicircle (center at $(\pm 1, 0)$, radius $1$) have centers distance $1$ apart. Since $1 < 1 + 1 = 2$, the full circles overlap. The intersection area of two unit circles whose centers are distance $d = 1$ apart is:
$$2\cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{d}{2}\right) - \tfrac{d}{2}\sqrt{4 - d^2} = 2\cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right) - \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} = \tfrac{2\pi}{3} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

Since all three semicircles open upward, each pairwise overlap region lies entirely in the upper half-plane, so the overlap of each adjacent pair of semicircles is exactly half the full-circle intersection:
$$\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{3} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = \tfrac{\pi}{3} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}.$$

The two outer semicircles are $2$ units apart (externally tangent), so they do not overlap.

**Total area covered by three small semicircles** (inclusion-exclusion):
$$3 \cdot \tfrac{\pi}{2} - 2\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right) = \tfrac{3\pi}{2} - \tfrac{2\pi}{3} + \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \tfrac{5\pi}{6} + \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

**Shaded area:**
$$2\pi - \left(\tfrac{5\pi}{6} + \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = \tfrac{7\pi}{6} - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
