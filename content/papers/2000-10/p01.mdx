<Problem>
In the year $2001$, the United States will host the International Mathematical Olympiad. Let $I$, $M$, and $O$ be distinct positive integers such that the product $I \cdot M \cdot O = 2001$. What is the largest possible value of the sum $I + M + O$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Factor $2001 = 3 \times 667 = 3 \times 23 \times 29$.

To maximize $I + M + O$ subject to $I \cdot M \cdot O = 2001$ with distinct positive integers, we want one factor to be as large as possible and the others as small as possible.

The factorizations using three distinct positive integers are:
- $1 \times 3 \times 667$: sum $= 1 + 3 + 667 = 671$
- $1 \times 23 \times 87$: sum $= 111$
- $1 \times 29 \times 69$: sum $= 99$
- $3 \times 23 \times 29$: sum $= 55$

The largest sum is $671$, achieved by $\{1, 3, 667\}$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
