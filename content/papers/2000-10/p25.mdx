<Problem>
In year $N$, the $300^\text{th}$ day of the year is a Tuesday. In year $N+1$, the $200^\text{th}$ day is also a Tuesday. On what day of the week did the $100^\text{th}$ day of year $N-1$ occur?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We use the fact that the days advance by $1$ per day, and we track differences modulo $7$.

**From day 300 of year $N$ to day 200 of year $N+1$:**

If year $N$ is not a leap year, it has 365 days. From day 300 of year $N$ to day 300 of year $N+1$ is 365 days, and from day 300 to day 200 of year $N+1$ is $365 - 100 = 265$ days.

If year $N$ is a leap year, it has 366 days, so this span is $366 - 100 = 266$ days.

Both days are Tuesday, so the number of days between them must be $\equiv 0 \pmod 7$.

Check: $265 = 7 \times 37 + 6 \equiv 6 \pmod 7$. Not divisible by 7.

Check: $266 = 7 \times 38 \equiv 0 \pmod 7$. Divisible by 7!

So year $N$ is a leap year (366 days).

**From day 100 of year $N-1$ to day 300 of year $N$:**

From day 100 of year $N-1$ to the end of year $N-1$: $(D_{N-1} - 100)$ days, where $D_{N-1}$ is the number of days in year $N-1$.

Then to day 300 of year $N$: another 300 days.

Total: $(D_{N-1} - 100) + 300 = D_{N-1} + 200$ days.

Day 300 of year $N$ is Tuesday. Day 100 of year $N-1$ was $D_{N-1} + 200$ days earlier, so it was $(D_{N-1} + 200) \pmod 7$ days before Tuesday.

Since year $N$ is a leap year, year $N-1$ is not (assuming no century-year complications). So $D_{N-1} = 365$.

Days between: $365 + 200 = 565$.

$565 \div 7 = 80$ remainder $5$. So $565 \equiv 5 \pmod 7$.

Day 100 of year $N-1$ was $5$ days before Tuesday.

Counting back $5$ days from Tuesday: Monday (1), Sunday (2), Saturday (3), Friday (4), Thursday (5).

Day 100 of year $N-1$ was a **Thursday**.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
