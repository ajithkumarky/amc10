<Problem>
In triangle $ABC$, medians $AD$ and $CE$ intersect at $P$, $PE = 1.5$, $PD = 2$, and $DE = 2.5$. What is the area of $AEDC$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Key fact about medians:** The centroid $P$ divides each median in ratio $2:1$ from vertex to midpoint. So $CP = 2 \cdot PE = 3$ and $AP = 2 \cdot PD = 4$.

**Check triangle $\triangle PED$:** $PE = 1.5$, $PD = 2$, $DE = 2.5$. Note $1.5^2 + 2^2 = 2.25 + 4 = 6.25 = 2.5^2$. So $\triangle PED$ is a right triangle with the right angle at $P$!

**Area of $\triangle PED$:** $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1.5 \cdot 2 = 1.5$.

**Area relationships:** Quadrilateral $AEDC$ consists of triangles $\triangle APE$, $\triangle EPD$, and... wait, let's think carefully. $E$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$.

The centroid divides the triangle into $6$ smaller triangles of equal area. Triangles $\triangle APE$ and $\triangle CPD$ each have area equal to $\triangle PED$ scaled by the ratio $AP/PD = 2$ and $CP/PE = 2$ respectively.

Since $\triangle PED$ has legs $PE = 1.5$ and $PD = 2$ with right angle at $P$:
- $\triangle APE$: shares leg $PE = 1.5$, and $AP = 4$, right angle at $P$, area $= \frac{1}{2}(1.5)(4) = 3$.
- $\triangle CPD$: shares leg $PD = 2$, and $CP = 3$, right angle at $P$, area $= \frac{1}{2}(2)(3) = 3$.
- $\triangle APC$: The remaining triangle has $AP = 4$, $CP = 3$, and right angle at $P$, area $= \frac{1}{2}(4)(3) = 6$.

Wait — the right angle at $P$ between $AD$ and $CE$ holds because $\triangle PED$ is right-angled at $P$ (and $AD$ passes through $P$ along $PD$, $CE$ passes through $P$ along $PE$).

Area of quadrilateral $AEDC$ = area of $\triangle AEC$ + area of $\triangle ECD$. Alternatively, $AEDC$ = $\triangle ABC$ minus $\triangle ABD$... Let me use the six small triangles.

The six equal-area triangles of a triangle's centroid subdivision all have area $= \frac{1}{6}[\triangle ABC]$.

Total area $[\triangle ABC] = [\triangle APE] + [\triangle EPD] + [\triangle DPB] + [\triangle BPE'] \ldots$

Actually, let's directly compute. The medians $AD$ and $CE$ create $4$ triangles inside quadrilateral $AEDC$: namely $\triangle APE$, $\triangle EPD$, $\triangle DPC$, and ... no, $AEDC$ has vertices $A$, $E$, $D$, $C$, and diagonal $AC$ and $ED$ cross at ... Let me use the direct approach.

Quadrilateral $AEDC$ is divided by segment $PD$ and $PE$ into triangles $\triangle APE$, $\triangle EPD$, $\triangle DPC$, and $\triangle APC$. But $P$ is inside $AEDC$.

Actually $AEDC$ = $\triangle AED + \triangle ADC$ or $= \triangle AEC + \triangle EDC$. Using diagonals $AD$ and $CE$ of the quadrilateral crossing at $P$:

$$[AEDC] = [\triangle APE] + [\triangle EPD] + [\triangle DPC] + [\triangle CPA]$$
$= 3 + 1.5 + 3 + 6 = 13.5$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
