<Problem>
A wire is cut into two pieces, one of length $a$ and the other of length $b$. The piece of length $a$ is bent to form an equilateral triangle, and the piece of length $b$ is bent to form a regular hexagon. The triangle and the hexagon have equal area. What is $\dfrac{a}{b}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Equilateral triangle** with perimeter $a$: side length $= \dfrac{a}{3}$, area $= \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\left(\frac{a}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}\, a^2}{36}$.

**Regular hexagon** with perimeter $b$: side length $= \dfrac{b}{6}$. A regular hexagon with side $s$ has area $\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}s^2$, so area $= \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\left(\frac{b}{6}\right)^2 = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}\, b^2}{72} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}\, b^2}{24}$.

Set areas equal:
$$\frac{\sqrt{3}\, a^2}{36} = \frac{\sqrt{3}\, b^2}{24}$$
$$\frac{a^2}{36} = \frac{b^2}{24}$$
$$\frac{a^2}{b^2} = \frac{36}{24} = \frac{3}{2}$$
$$\frac{a}{b} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
