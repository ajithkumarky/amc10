<Problem>
A positive integer $n$ is called "nice" if there is a positive integer $m$ with exactly four positive divisors (including $1$ and $m$) such that the sum of the four divisors is equal to $n$. How many numbers in the set $\{2010, 2011, 2012, \ldots, 2019\}$ are nice?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A positive integer $m$ with exactly four divisors has one of two forms:

**Form 1:** $m = p^3$ for a prime $p$. Divisors: $1, p, p^2, p^3$. Sum $= 1 + p + p^2 + p^3$.

**Form 2:** $m = p \cdot q$ for distinct primes $p < q$. Divisors: $1, p, q, pq$. Sum $= 1 + p + q + pq = (1+p)(1+q)$.

We need the sum to fall in $\{2010, 2011, \ldots, 2019\}$.

**Form 1 check:** $1 + p + p^2 + p^3 = \frac{p^4-1}{p-1}$.
- $p=11$: $1+11+121+1331 = 1464$. Too small.
- $p=13$: $1+13+169+2197 = 2380$. Too large.

No Form 1 values in range.

**Form 2 check:** $(1+p)(1+q) \in \{2010, \ldots, 2019\}$ where $p < q$ are primes.

For each $n$ in the range, factor $n$ as $(1+p)(1+q)$ where $p$ and $q$ are primes. So we need $n = AB$ where $A = 1+p$ and $B = 1+q$, with $A-1$ and $B-1$ both prime.

Check each $n$:

**$n = 2010$:** $2010 = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 67$. Factor pairs $(A,B)$ with $A \le B$, $A-1$ prime, $B-1$ prime:
- $(2, 1005)$: $p=1$ (not prime). No.
- $(3, 670)$: $p=2$, $q=669=3\times223$. Not prime. No.
- $(5, 402)$: $p=4$. Not prime. No.
- $(6, 335)$: $p=5$, $q=334=2\times167$. Not prime. No.
- $(10, 201)$: $p=9$. Not prime. No.
- $(15, 134)$: $p=14$. Not prime. No.
- $(30, 67)$: $p=29$ (prime!), $q=66=2\times3\times11$. Not prime. No.
Not nice.

**$n = 2011$:** $2011$ is prime. Factor pairs: $(1, 2011)$: $p=0$. No. $(2011, 1)$: same. Not nice.

**$n = 2012$:** $2012 = 4 \times 503 = 2^2 \times 503$.
- $(4, 503)$: $p=3$ (prime!), $q=502=2\times251$. Not prime. No.
- $(2, 1006)$: $p=1$. No.
Not nice.

**$n = 2013$:** $2013 = 3 \times 11 \times 61$.
- $(3, 671)$: $p=2$, $q=670=2\times5\times67$. Not prime. No.
- $(11, 183)$: $p=10$. Not prime. No.
- $(33, 61)$: $p=32$. Not prime. No.
Not nice.

**$n = 2014$:** $2014 = 2 \times 19 \times 53$.
- $(2, 1007)$: $p=1$. No.
- $(19, 106)$: $p=18$. Not prime. No.
- $(38, 53)$: $p=37$ (prime!), $q=52=4\times13$. Not prime. No.
Not nice.

**$n = 2015$:** $2015 = 5 \times 403 = 5 \times 13 \times 31$.
- $(5, 403)$: $p=4$. No.
- $(13, 155)$: $p=12$. No.
- $(31, 65)$: $p=30$. No.
Not nice.

**$n = 2016$:** $2016 = 2^5 \times 3^2 \times 7$.
- $(2, 1008)$: $p=1$. No.
- $(4, 504)$: $p=3$, $q=503$ (prime!). Yes! $(1+3)(1+503) = 4 \times 504 = 2016$. Check: $m = 3 \times 503 = 1509$, divisors $1, 3, 503, 1509$, sum $= 2016$. **Nice!**

**$n = 2017$:** $2017$ is prime. Not nice (same reason as $2011$).

**$n = 2018$:** $2018 = 2 \times 1009$. Is $1009$ prime? $1009 / 7 \approx 144$, $1009/11 = 91.7$, $1009/13 = 77.6$, $1009/17=59.4$, $1009/19=53.1$, $1009/23=43.9$, $1009/29=34.8$, $1009/31=32.5$, $31^2=961<1009<1024=32^2$. Check primes up to $31$: none divide $1009$. So $1009$ is prime.
- $(2, 1009)$: $p=1$. No.
- $(1009, 2)$: same. 
Not nice (only factor pair doesn't work).

**$n = 2019$:** $2019 = 3 \times 673$. Is $673$ prime? $\sqrt{673} \approx 26$. Check $2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23$: none divide $673$. Yes, prime.
- $(3, 673)$: $p=2$, $q=672=2^5\times3\times7$. Not prime. No.
Not nice.

Only $2016$ is nice. Count: $\mathbf{1}$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
