<Problem>
A majority of the $30$ students in Ms. Deameanor's class bought pencils at the school bookstore. Each of these students bought the same number of pencils, and this number was greater than $1$. The cost of a pencil in cents was greater than the number of pencils each student bought, and the total cost of all the pencils was $\$17.71$. What was the cost of a pencil in cents?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The total cost is $1771$ cents. Factor $1771$:
$$1771 = 7 \times 11 \times 23.$$

The total cost equals (number of students who bought) $\times$ (pencils per student) $\times$ (cost per pencil). Let $s$ = students, $p$ = pencils per student, $c$ = cost per pencil in cents. Then $s \cdot p \cdot c = 1771$.

Constraints:
- $s > 15$ (majority of 30), so $s \geq 16$.
- $p \geq 2$ (greater than 1).
- $c > p$ (cost per pencil exceeds number each student bought).

The factorizations of $1771 = 7 \times 11 \times 23$ with $s > 15$ require $s$ to use one of the prime factors. Since $s > 15$ and $7, 11 < 15$ don't work alone, we try $s = 23$: then $p \cdot c = 7 \times 11 = 77$. With $p \geq 2$ and $c > p$: if $p = 7$ and $c = 11$, then $c > p$ ✓ and $s = 23 > 15$ ✓.

The cost of a pencil is $11$ cents.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
