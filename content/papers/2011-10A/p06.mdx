<Problem>
Set $A$ has $20$ elements, and set $B$ has $15$ elements. What is the smallest possible number of elements in $A \cup B$, the union of $A$ and $B$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
By the inclusion-exclusion principle, $|A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B|$.

To minimize $|A \cup B|$, we maximize $|A \cap B|$. The intersection can be at most as large as the smaller set, so $|A \cap B| \leq 15$.

With $|A \cap B| = 15$ (i.e., $B \subseteq A$):
$$|A \cup B| = 20 + 15 - 15 = 20.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
