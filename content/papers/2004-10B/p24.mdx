<Problem>
In $\triangle ABC$ we have $AB = 7$, $AC = 8$, and $BC = 9$. Point $D$ is on the circumscribed circle of the triangle so that $AD$ bisects $\angle BAC$. What is the value of $\dfrac{AD}{CD}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since $AD$ bisects $\angle BAC$ and $D$ is on the circumcircle, $D$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$ not containing $A$.

**Key property:** Since $D$ is the midpoint of arc $BC$, we have $DB = DC$.

**Finding $AD$:** Use the angle bisector length with the extended law of sines / Ptolemy's theorem.

By the angle bisector, $D$ is equidistant from $B$ and $C$ on the circle: $DB = DC$.

Apply Ptolemy's theorem to cyclic quadrilateral $ABDC$ (with $D$ on arc $BC$ not containing $A$, order around circle: $A, B, D, C$):
$$AD \cdot BC = AB \cdot DC + AC \cdot BD.$$

Since $DB = DC$, let $DB = DC = x$:
$$9 \cdot AD = 7x + 8x = 15x \implies AD = \frac{15x}{9} = \frac{5x}{3}.$$

Therefore $\dfrac{AD}{CD} = \dfrac{5x/3}{x} = \dfrac{5}{3}$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
