<Problem>
On a trip from the United States to Canada, Isabella took $d$ U.S. dollars. At the border she exchanged them all, receiving $10$ Canadian dollars for every $7$ U.S. dollars. After spending $60$ Canadian dollars, she had $d$ Canadian dollars left. What is the sum of the digits of $d$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Isabella starts with $d$ U.S. dollars and exchanges at a rate of $\frac{10}{7}$, receiving $\frac{10d}{7}$ Canadian dollars.

After spending 60 Canadian dollars, she has $\frac{10d}{7} - 60$ Canadian dollars left, and this equals $d$:
$$\frac{10d}{7} - 60 = d.$$

Multiply both sides by 7:
$$10d - 420 = 7d \implies 3d = 420 \implies d = 140.$$

The sum of the digits of 140 is $1 + 4 + 0 = 5$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
