<Problem>
In the expression $c \cdot a^b - d$, the values of $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$ are $0$, $1$, $2$, and $3$, although not necessarily in that order. What is the maximum possible value of the result?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We want to maximize $c \cdot a^b - d$. To do this, make $c \cdot a^b$ large and $d$ small (so $d = 0$).

With $d = 0$, we maximize $c \cdot a^b$ using the remaining values 1, 2, 3 for $a$, $b$, $c$.

Try the most promising assignments:
- $a=3, b=2, c=1$: $1 \cdot 3^2 - 0 = 9$
- $a=2, b=3, c=1$: $1 \cdot 2^3 - 0 = 8$
- $a=3, b=1, c=2$: $2 \cdot 3^1 - 0 = 6$
- $a=2, b=1, c=3$: $3 \cdot 2^1 - 0 = 6$

The maximum is $9$, achieved with $a=3, b=2, c=1, d=0$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
