<Problem>
Let $1, 4, \ldots$ and $9, 16, \ldots$ be two arithmetic progressions. The set $S$ is the union of the first $2004$ terms of each sequence. How many distinct numbers are in $S$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The first sequence: starting at 1 with common difference 3. Terms: $1, 4, 7, 10, \ldots, 1 + 3(2003) = 6010$.

The second sequence: starting at 9 with common difference 7. Terms: $9, 16, 23, 30, \ldots, 9 + 7(2003) = 14030$.

Each sequence has 2004 terms, so together there are $4008$ terms (with possible duplicates).

A number is in both sequences iff $n \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ and $n \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$.

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem (since $\gcd(3,7)=1$), there is a unique residue class mod $21$. Solving: $n \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ and $n \equiv 2 \pmod 7$. From $n = 7k + 2$: $7k + 2 \equiv 1 \pmod 3 \Rightarrow k \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, so $k = 3j + 2$, giving $n = 7(3j+2) + 2 = 21j + 16$. So common terms satisfy $n \equiv 16 \pmod{21}$: they are $16, 37, 58, \ldots$

**Range of overlap:** The first sequence goes up to 6010; the second sequence starts at 9. The common terms must lie in both sequences, so they must be $\leq \min(6010, 14030) = 6010$ and within the first 2004 terms of each.

First 2004 terms of seq 1: $1$ to $6010$ (step 3), all $\equiv 1 \pmod 3$.
First 2004 terms of seq 2: $9$ to $14030$ (step 7), all $\equiv 2 \pmod 7$.

Common terms (in both sets): $n \equiv 16 \pmod{21}$, with $1 \leq n \leq 6010$ and $9 \leq n \leq 14030$. So $16 \leq n \leq 6010$, $n \equiv 16 \pmod{21}$.

Count: $n = 21k + 16$ for $k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ with $21k + 16 \leq 6010 \Rightarrow k \leq \frac{5994}{21} = 285.4$, so $k = 0, 1, \ldots, 285$, giving $286$ common values.

Total distinct numbers: $2004 + 2004 - 286 = 3722$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
