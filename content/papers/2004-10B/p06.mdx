<Problem>
Which of the following numbers is a perfect square?

$\textbf{(A)}\ 98! \cdot 99! \qquad \textbf{(B)}\ 98! \cdot 100! \qquad \textbf{(C)}\ 99! \cdot 100! \qquad \textbf{(D)}\ 99! \cdot 101! \qquad \textbf{(E)}\ 100! \cdot 101!$
</Problem>

<Solution>
A product $n! \cdot m!$ is a perfect square when all prime exponents in its factorization are even.

Note that $(n!)^2$ is always a perfect square. So check whether each option equals a factorial squared times a perfect square.

Write each option using $n! \cdot (n+1)! = n! \cdot (n+1) \cdot n! = (n+1) \cdot (n!)^2$:

- $\textbf{(A)}$ $98! \cdot 99! = 99 \cdot (98!)^2$ — is $99$ a perfect square? No ($99 = 9 \cdot 11$).
- $\textbf{(B)}$ $98! \cdot 100! = 98! \cdot 100 \cdot 99! = 100 \cdot (99!)(98!)$ — rewrite: $= 100 \cdot 99 \cdot (98!)^2$. Is $9900$ a perfect square? $9900 = 4 \cdot 9 \cdot 275 = 4 \cdot 9 \cdot 25 \cdot 11$, so $9900 = (2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5)^2 \cdot 11$. Not a perfect square.
- $\textbf{(C)}$ $99! \cdot 100! = 100 \cdot (99!)^2$. Is $100$ a perfect square? Yes, $100 = 10^2$.
- Therefore $99! \cdot 100! = (10 \cdot 99!)^2$, which is a perfect square.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
