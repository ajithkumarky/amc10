<Problem>
A triangle with sides of length $5$, $12$, and $13$ has both an inscribed and a circumscribed circle. What is the distance between the centers of those circles?
</Problem>

<Solution>
This is a right triangle (since $5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13^2$).

**Circumscribed circle (circumcircle):** For a right triangle, the circumcenter is the midpoint of the hypotenuse. With the right angle at the origin, let the triangle have vertices $C = (0,0)$, $A = (12,0)$, $B = (0,5)$. The circumcenter is the midpoint of $AB$: $O_{\text{circ}} = (6, 5/2)$, with circumradius $R = 13/2$.

**Inscribed circle (incircle):** The inradius is $r = \dfrac{\text{area}}{\text{semi-perimeter}} = \dfrac{\frac{1}{2}(5)(12)}{\frac{5+12+13}{2}} = \dfrac{30}{15} = 2$.

The incenter is at $(r, r) = (2, 2)$ (for a right triangle with legs along the axes, the incenter is at distance $r$ from each leg).

**Distance between centers:**
$$d = \sqrt{(6-2)^2 + (5/2 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{16 + (1/2)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 1/4} = \sqrt{65/4} = \frac{\sqrt{65}}{2}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
