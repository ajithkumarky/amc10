<Problem>
Three circles of radius $1$ are externally tangent to each other and internally tangent to a larger circle. What is the radius of the large circle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The three unit circles are mutually externally tangent, so the distance between any two of their centers is $1 + 1 = 2$. Their centers form an equilateral triangle with side length 2.

The circumradius of this equilateral triangle (distance from centroid to each vertex) is:
$$r_{\text{triangle}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$

The large circle is internally tangent to each unit circle, so the distance from the large circle's center to each unit circle's center equals $R - 1$, where $R$ is the large radius. By symmetry, the large circle's center coincides with the centroid of the equilateral triangle.

Therefore:
$$R - 1 = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} \implies R = 1 + \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{3 + 2\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
