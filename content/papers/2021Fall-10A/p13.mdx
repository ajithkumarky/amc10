<Problem>
Each of $6$ balls is randomly and independently painted either black or white with equal probability. What is the probability that every ball is different in color from more than half of the other $5$ balls?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Each ball has $5$ other balls. "Different in color from more than half" means different from at least $3$ of the other $5$.

If a ball is black and there are $k$ black balls total (including itself), it shares color with $k-1$ others and differs from $6-k$ others. The condition requires $6-k \ge 3$, i.e., $k \le 3$.

Similarly, if a ball is white and there are $6-k$ white balls total, it differs from $k$ others. The condition requires $k \ge 3$.

So every ball satisfies the condition if and only if $k = 3$: exactly $3$ black and $3$ white balls.

The total number of equally likely outcomes is $2^6 = 64$.

The number of outcomes with exactly $3$ black balls is $\dbinom{6}{3} = 20$.

$$P = \frac{20}{64} = \frac{5}{16}.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
