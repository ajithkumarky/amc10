<Problem>
What is the maximum number of balls of clay of radius $2$ that can completely fit inside a cube of side length $6$, assuming the balls can be reshaped but not compressed before they are packed in the cube?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The key insight is that the balls can be **reshaped** (but not compressed), so what matters is total volume, not geometric packing.

Volume of the cube: $6^3 = 216$ cubic units.

Volume of one ball: $\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi (2)^3 = \dfrac{32\pi}{3}$ cubic units.

Maximum number of balls: $\left\lfloor \dfrac{216}{32\pi/3} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \dfrac{648}{32\pi} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \dfrac{648}{100.53\ldots} \right\rfloor = \lfloor 6.44\ldots \rfloor = 6$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
