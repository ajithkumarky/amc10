<Problem>
When a certain unfair die is rolled, an even number is $3$ times as likely to appear as an odd number. The die is rolled twice. What is the probability that the sum of the numbers rolled is even?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $P(\text{odd}) = p$. Since an even number is $3$ times as likely, $P(\text{even}) = 3p$. These must sum to $1$:

$$p + 3p = 1 \implies p = \frac{1}{4}, \quad P(\text{even}) = \frac{3}{4}.$$

The sum of two rolls is even exactly when both rolls are even, or both rolls are odd:

$$P(\text{sum even}) = P(\text{even})^2 + P(\text{odd})^2 = \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{16} + \frac{1}{16} = \frac{10}{16} = \frac{5}{8}.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
