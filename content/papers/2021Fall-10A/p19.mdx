For $y \in [1, s-1]$, distance $= 1 - x$, which is in $[0, 1)$, always $\le 1$ ✓. For $y \in [0,1)$: nearest boundary point is $(1, 1)$ at distance $\sqrt{(1-x)^2 + (1-y)^2}$. This is $\le 1$ iff $(1-x)^2 + (1-y)^2 \le 1$.

So the swept region outside $[1,s-1]^2$ but inside $[0,s]^2$ is:
- Strips: $x \in [0, 1], y \in [1, s-1]$ (left strip): area $1 \times (s-2) = s-2$.
- Symmetrically for right, top, bottom: 4 strips of area $s - 2$ each. But wait, some of this extends into the corner regions outside $[1,s-1]^2$.
- Corner regions (e.g., $x \in [0,1], y \in [0,1]$): include points where $(1-x)^2 + (1-y)^2 \le 1$ (quarter-disk of radius 1 centered at $(1,1)$). Area $= \frac{\pi}{4}$ per corner.

$$= 4(s-2) + \pi + 4s - 12$$
