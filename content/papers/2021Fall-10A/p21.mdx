<Problem>
Each of the $20$ balls is tossed independently and at random into one of the $5$ bins. Let $p$ be the probability that some bin ends up with $3$ balls, another with $5$ balls, and the other three with $4$ balls each. Let $q$ be the probability that every bin ends up with $4$ balls. What is $\dfrac{p}{q}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Both $p$ and $q$ involve distributing 20 distinguishable balls into 5 distinguishable bins, each with total $5^{20}$.

**Computing $q$:** Each bin gets exactly 4 balls.

$$q = \frac{\dbinom{20}{4}\dbinom{16}{4}\dbinom{12}{4}\dbinom{8}{4}\dbinom{4}{4}}{5^{20}} = \frac{\dfrac{20!}{(4!)^5}}{5^{20}}.$$

**Computing $p$:** One bin gets 3, one bin gets 5, three bins get 4 each.

- Choose which bin gets 3: 5 ways.
- Choose which bin gets 5: 4 ways.
- The remaining 3 bins each get 4.

Number of ways to distribute:
$$\binom{20}{3}\binom{17}{5}\binom{12}{4}\binom{8}{4}\binom{4}{4} \times 5 \times 4.$$

So:
$$p = \frac{20 \cdot \dbinom{20}{3}\dbinom{17}{5}\dbinom{12}{4}\dbinom{8}{4}\dbinom{4}{4}}{5^{20}}.$$

**Computing the ratio $\dfrac{p}{q}$:**

$$\frac{p}{q} = \frac{20 \cdot \dbinom{20}{3}\dbinom{17}{5}\dbinom{12}{4}\dbinom{8}{4}\dbinom{4}{4}}{\dbinom{20}{4}\dbinom{16}{4}\dbinom{12}{4}\dbinom{8}{4}\dbinom{4}{4}}.$$

Cancel $\dbinom{12}{4}\dbinom{8}{4}\dbinom{4}{4}$:

$$\frac{p}{q} = 20 \cdot \frac{\dbinom{20}{3}\dbinom{17}{5}}{\dbinom{20}{4}\dbinom{16}{4}}.$$

Compute each:
$$\binom{20}{3} = \frac{20 \cdot 19 \cdot 18}{6} = 1140, \quad \binom{17}{5} = \frac{17 \cdot 16 \cdot 15 \cdot 14 \cdot 13}{120} = 6188.$$
$$\binom{20}{4} = \frac{20 \cdot 19 \cdot 18 \cdot 17}{24} = 4845, \quad \binom{16}{4} = \frac{16 \cdot 15 \cdot 14 \cdot 13}{24} = 1820.$$

$$\frac{p}{q} = 20 \cdot \frac{1140 \cdot 6188}{4845 \cdot 1820}.$$

Let's simplify step by step. First: $\dfrac{\binom{20}{3}}{\binom{20}{4}} = \dfrac{1140}{4845} = \dfrac{4!}{(20-3)! / (20-4)!} $... 

Using the identity $\dfrac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{n}{k+1}} = \dfrac{k+1}{n-k}$:

$$\frac{\binom{20}{3}}{\binom{20}{4}} = \frac{4}{20-3} = \frac{4}{17}.$$

And $\dfrac{\binom{17}{5}}{\binom{16}{4}}$:

$$\binom{17}{5} = \frac{17}{5} \cdot \binom{16}{4} \implies \frac{\binom{17}{5}}{\binom{16}{4}} = \frac{17}{5}.$$

So:
$$\frac{p}{q} = 20 \cdot \frac{4}{17} \cdot \frac{17}{5} = 20 \cdot \frac{4}{5} = 16.$$

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
