<Problem>
Mr. Lopez has a choice of two routes to get to work. Route A is $6$ miles long, and his average speed along this route is $30$ miles per hour. Route B is $5$ miles long, and his average speed along this route is $40$ miles per hour, except for a $\tfrac{1}{2}$-mile stretch in a school zone where his average speed is $20$ miles per hour. By how many minutes is Route B quicker than Route A?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Route A:** $\dfrac{6}{30} = \dfrac{1}{5}$ hour $= 12$ minutes.

**Route B:** The non-school portion is $5 - \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{9}{2}$ miles at $40$ mph, and the school zone is $\tfrac{1}{2}$ mile at $20$ mph.

$$\text{Time B} = \frac{9/2}{40} + \frac{1/2}{20} = \frac{9}{80} + \frac{1}{40} = \frac{9}{80} + \frac{2}{80} = \frac{11}{80} \text{ hours}.$$

Converting: $\dfrac{11}{80} \times 60 = \dfrac{33}{4} = 8.25$ minutes.

Route B is quicker by $12 - 8.25 = 3.75 = 3\tfrac{3}{4}$ minutes.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
