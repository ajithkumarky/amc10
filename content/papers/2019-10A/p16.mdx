<Problem>
The figure below shows $13$ circles of radius $1$ within a larger circle. All the intersections occur at points of tangency. The $13$ unit circles are arranged so that a central circle is surrounded by a ring of $6$ circles (forming a hexagonal pattern), and that ring is surrounded by another ring of $6$ circles at the outer layer. What is the area of the region inside the larger circle but outside all the circles of radius $1$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The 13 unit circles are arranged with centers at $(0,0)$, six inner-ring centers at distance $2$ from the origin (two tangent unit circles have centers $2$ apart), and six outer-ring centers at distance $2\sqrt{3}$ from the origin. The large enclosing circle has its center at the origin and must reach the farthest unit-circle centers plus their radius $1$, giving:
$$R = 2\sqrt{3} + 1.$$

Area of the large circle minus area of the 13 unit circles:
$$\pi R^2 - 13\pi = \pi(2\sqrt{3}+1)^2 - 13\pi = \pi(12 + 4\sqrt{3} + 1) - 13\pi = \pi(13 + 4\sqrt{3}) - 13\pi = 4\pi\sqrt{3}.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
