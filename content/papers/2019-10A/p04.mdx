<Problem>
A box contains $28$ red balls, $20$ green balls, $19$ yellow balls, $13$ blue balls, $11$ white balls, and $9$ black balls. What is the minimum number of balls that must be drawn from the box without replacement to guarantee that at least $15$ balls of a single color will be drawn?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We use the Pigeonhole Principle in its "worst-case" form: we want to find the minimum draw that *forces* at least 15 of one color, no matter how unlucky we are.

In the worst case, we draw as many balls as possible while keeping every color below 15. Let's count the maximum we can draw without reaching 15 of any single color:

- Red: at most 14 (there are 28 available, but we stop at 14)
- Green: at most 14 (there are 20 available)
- Yellow: at most 14 (there are 19 available)
- Blue: at most 13 (only 13 available — we take all of them)
- White: at most 11 (only 11 available — we take all of them)
- Black: at most 9 (only 9 available — we take all of them)

So the worst-case total without achieving 15 of any color is:
$$14 + 14 + 14 + 13 + 11 + 9 = 75.$$

After drawing 75 balls, we could still have no color at 15. But the very next ball (the 76th) must be red, green, or yellow (those are the only colors not yet exhausted), and each of those already has 14 balls drawn — so drawing one more gives us 15 of that color.

Therefore the minimum number of draws needed to guarantee 15 of one color is $75 + 1 = \boxed{76}$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
