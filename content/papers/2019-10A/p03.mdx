<Problem>
Ana and Bonita were born on the same date in different years, $n$ years apart. Last year Ana was $5$ times as old as Bonita. This year Ana's age is the square of Bonita's age. What is $n$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $A$ and $B$ be Ana's and Bonita's ages **this year**.

From "this year Ana's age is the square of Bonita's age": $A = B^2$.

From "last year Ana was $5$ times as old as Bonita": $A - 1 = 5(B - 1)$.

Substitute $A = B^2$:

$$B^2 - 1 = 5B - 5 \implies B^2 - 5B + 4 = 0 \implies (B - 1)(B - 4) = 0.$$

So $B = 1$ or $B = 4$. If $B = 1$, then $A = 1$ and they'd be the same age, contradicting "different years." So $B = 4$ and $A = 16$.

Thus $n = A - B = 12$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>

<AlternateMethod title="Test small ages">
The relationship "$A = B^2$" gives small candidates: $(B, A) = (2, 4), (3, 9), (4, 16), (5, 25), \ldots$ For each, check whether "$A - 1 = 5(B - 1)$" holds. Only $(4, 16)$ works: $15 = 5 \cdot 3$. So $n = 12$.
</AlternateMethod>
