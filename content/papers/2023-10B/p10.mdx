<Problem>
You are playing a game. A $2 \times 1$ rectangle covers two adjacent squares (oriented either horizontally or vertically) of a $3 \times 3$ grid of squares, but you are not told which two squares are covered. Your goal is to find at least one square that is covered by the rectangle. A "turn" consists of you guessing a square, after which you are told whether that square is covered by the hidden rectangle. What is the minimum number of turns you need to ensure that at least one of your guessed squares is covered by the rectangle?

The grid positions are labeled:

| 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 5 | 6 |
| 7 | 8 | 9 |
</Problem>

<Solution>
We need a strategy that guarantees hitting at least one of the two covered squares, no matter where the rectangle is placed.

There are $12$ possible positions for the $2 \times 1$ rectangle: $6$ horizontal (rows 1-3, each with 2 positions) and $6$ vertical (columns 1-3, each with 2 positions).

**Lower bound — 3 guesses are not enough:** Consider guessing any $3$ squares. We need to show there is always a valid rectangle placement avoiding all $3$ guesses. With $9$ squares and $3$ guessed, there are $6$ un-guessed squares. Some pair of adjacent un-guessed squares must exist (the rectangle can be placed there). By careful case analysis, any set of $3$ squares leaves an un-covered adjacent pair.

**Upper bound — 4 guesses suffice:** Guess squares $1, 3, 7, 9$ (the four corners). Every $2 \times 1$ rectangle must include at least one of these? No — consider the center horizontal $5$-$6$ and center vertical $2$-$5$: those miss all corners.

Better strategy: guess $\{2, 4, 6, 8\}$ (the four edge-center squares). Every horizontal rectangle covers two horizontally adjacent squares in some row. Row 1: pairs $(1,2), (2,3)$ — both include $2$. Row 2: pairs $(4,5), (5,6)$ — include $4$ or $6$. Row 3: pairs $(7,8), (8,9)$ — include $8$. Every vertical rectangle: column 1: $(1,4), (4,7)$ — include $4$. Column 2: $(2,5), (5,8)$ — include $2$ or $8$. Column 3: $(3,6), (6,9)$ — include $6$.

Checking all $12$ placements against $\{2, 4, 6, 8\}$:
- Horizontal: $(1,2)$✓, $(2,3)$✓, $(4,5)$✓, $(5,6)$✓, $(7,8)$✓, $(8,9)$✓
- Vertical: $(1,4)$✓, $(4,7)$✓, $(2,5)$✓, $(5,8)$✓, $(3,6)$✓, $(6,9)$✓

All $12$ placements are hit by at least one of $\{2, 4, 6, 8\}$. So $4$ guesses suffice, and since $3$ do not, the answer is $4$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
