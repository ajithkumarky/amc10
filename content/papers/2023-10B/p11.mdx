<Problem>
Suzanne went to the bank and withdrew $\$800$. The teller gave her this amount using $\$20$ bills, $\$50$ bills, and $\$100$ bills, with at least one of each denomination. How many different collections of bills could Suzanne have received?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $a$, $b$, $c$ be the numbers of $\$20$, $\$50$, and $\$100$ bills, each at least $1$.

The equation is $20a + 50b + 100c = 800$. Dividing by $10$: $2a + 5b + 10c = 80$.

Since $2a + 10c$ is always even, we need $5b$ to be even, so $b$ must be even. Let $b = 2k$ with $k \ge 1$.

Substituting: $2a + 10k + 10c = 80$, so $a + 5k + 5c = 40$, giving $a = 40 - 5k - 5c = 5(8 - k - c)$.

For $a \ge 1$: we need $5(8 - k - c) \ge 1$, so $8 - k - c \ge 1$, i.e., $k + c \le 7$.

With $k \ge 1$ and $c \ge 1$, let $k' = k - 1 \ge 0$ and $c' = c - 1 \ge 0$:
$$k' + c' \le 5, \quad k', c' \ge 0.$$

The number of non-negative integer solutions to $k' + c' \le 5$ equals the number of solutions to $k' + c' + s = 5$ (introducing slack $s \ge 0$), which is $\binom{5+2}{2} = \binom{7}{2} = 21$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
