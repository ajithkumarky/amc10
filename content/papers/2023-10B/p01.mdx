<Problem>
Mrs. Jones is pouring orange juice into four identical glasses for her four sons. She fills the first three glasses completely but runs out of juice when the fourth glass is only $\frac{1}{3}$ full. What fraction of a glass must Mrs. Jones pour from each of the first three glasses into the fourth glass so that all four glasses will have the same amount of juice?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The total juice on hand equals $3 + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$ glasses. Dividing equally among four glasses gives each son $\frac{10}{3} \div 4 = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}$ of a glass.

The fourth glass currently has $\frac{1}{3}$ and needs $\frac{5}{6}$, so it needs $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{6} - \frac{2}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$ more glass of juice. This extra juice comes equally from each of the three full glasses, so each contributes $\frac{1}{2} \div 3 = \frac{1}{6}$ of a glass.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
