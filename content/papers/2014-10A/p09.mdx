<Problem>
The two legs of a right triangle, which are altitudes, have lengths $2\sqrt{3}$ and $6$. How long is the third altitude of the triangle?
</Problem>

<Solution>
In a right triangle, the two legs are themselves altitudes (each leg is perpendicular to the other). So the two legs have lengths $a = 2\sqrt{3}$ and $b = 6$.

The area of the triangle is:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot a \cdot b = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\sqrt{3} \cdot 6 = 6\sqrt{3}.$$

The hypotenuse has length:
$$c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{12 + 36} = \sqrt{48} = 4\sqrt{3}.$$

The third altitude $h$ (to the hypotenuse) satisfies:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot c \cdot h \implies 6\sqrt{3} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4\sqrt{3} \cdot h \implies h = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{3}} = 3.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
