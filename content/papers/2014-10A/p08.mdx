<Problem>
Which of the following numbers is a perfect square?

$$\frac{14!\cdot 15!}{2},\quad \frac{15!\cdot 16!}{2},\quad \frac{16!\cdot 17!}{2},\quad \frac{17!\cdot 18!}{2},\quad \frac{18!\cdot 19!}{2}$$
</Problem>

<Solution>
Notice that $\dfrac{n! \cdot (n+1)!}{2} = \dfrac{n! \cdot (n+1) \cdot n!}{2} = \dfrac{(n+1)}{2} \cdot (n!)^2.$

This is a perfect square exactly when $\dfrac{n+1}{2}$ is a perfect square (since $(n!)^2$ is always a perfect square).

For each choice:
- $n=14$: $\dfrac{15}{2}$ — not an integer.
- $n=15$: $\dfrac{16}{2} = 8$ — not a perfect square.
- $n=16$: $\dfrac{17}{2}$ — not an integer.
- $n=17$: $\dfrac{18}{2} = 9 = 3^2$ — **perfect square!**
- $n=18$: $\dfrac{19}{2}$ — not an integer.

So $\dfrac{17! \cdot 18!}{2} = 9 \cdot (17!)^2 = (3 \cdot 17!)^2$ is a perfect square.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
