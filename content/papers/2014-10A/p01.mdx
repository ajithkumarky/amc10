<Problem>
What is $10 \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{1}{10}\right)^{-1}$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
First simplify the sum inside the parentheses. Find a common denominator of 10:

$$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{5} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{5}{10} + \frac{2}{10} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{8}{10} = \frac{4}{5}.$$

Taking the reciprocal: $\left(\dfrac{4}{5}\right)^{-1} = \dfrac{5}{4}$.

Now multiply: $10 \cdot \dfrac{5}{4} = \dfrac{50}{4} = \dfrac{25}{2}$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
