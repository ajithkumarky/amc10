<Problem>
The diagram shows the circular face of a clock with radius $20$ cm and a circular disk with radius $10$ cm externally tangent to the clock face at $12$ o'clock. The disk has an arrow painted on it, initially pointing in the upward vertical direction. Let the disk roll clockwise around the clock face. At what point on the clock face will the disk be tangent when the arrow is next pointing in the upward vertical direction?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The small disk (radius $r = 10$) rolls clockwise around the outside of the clock face (radius $R = 20$) without slipping. We need to find the total rotation of the disk in the fixed (ground) frame.

When the disk's center has swept through angle $\theta$ around the clock center, the contact arc on the clock face has length $R\theta = 20\theta$. Rolling without slipping means the disk rotates $\dfrac{20\theta}{10} = 2\theta$ radians relative to the line joining the two centers. In addition, that line itself has rotated by $\theta$ (clockwise) in the fixed frame. Both contributions are in the same direction, so the disk's total rotation in the fixed frame is:

$$\phi = 2\theta + \theta = 3\theta.$$

For the arrow to point upward again, the disk must complete a whole number of full turns: $3\theta = 2\pi k$ for a positive integer $k$. The smallest solution is $\theta = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, i.e., $120°$ clockwise from 12 o'clock.

Starting at 12 and moving $120°$ clockwise lands at the **4 o'clock** position.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
