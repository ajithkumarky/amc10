<Problem>
Two right circular cylinders have the same volume. The radius of the second cylinder is $10\%$ more than the radius of the first. What is the relationship between the heights of the two cylinders?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the first cylinder have radius $r$ and height $h_1$, and the second have radius $1.1r$ and height $h_2$.

Equal volumes:
$$\pi r^2 h_1 = \pi (1.1r)^2 h_2$$
$$r^2 h_1 = 1.21 r^2 h_2$$
$$h_1 = 1.21 h_2.$$

So the first height is $1.21$ times the second height, meaning the first height is $21\%$ more than the second.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
