<Problem>
Let $S$ be a square of side length $1$. Two points are chosen at random on the sides of $S$. The probability that the straight-line distance between the points is at least $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is $\tfrac{a-b\pi}{c}$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are positive integers with $\gcd(a,b,c)=1$. What is $a+b+c$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Parametrize the perimeter by arc length $t \in [0,4)$ from a fixed corner. The two points are chosen uniformly and independently, so the sample space is $[0,4)^2$ with area 16.

We compute the area of the region where the Euclidean distance between the two points is **less than** $\tfrac{1}{2}$, then subtract from 1.

**Same side.** For each of the 4 sides, both points lie on that side when both parameters fall in the corresponding unit interval. The Euclidean distance equals the difference of the arc-length parameters. The area within one side's $1\times1$ block where that difference is less than $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is $1 - 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot(\tfrac{1}{2})^2 / 1$. More carefully: the area of $\{(u,v)\in[0,1]^2 : |u-v|<\tfrac{1}{2}\}$ equals $1 - (\tfrac{1}{2})^2 = \tfrac{3}{4}$. Four sides contribute $4 \cdot \tfrac{3}{4} = 3$.

**Adjacent sides (near a shared corner).** At each corner the two adjacent side-intervals each contribute one axis in the local $1\times 1$ block of parameter space. A point at arc-distance $x$ from the corner on one side and a point at arc-distance $y$ from the corner on the other side are Euclidean distance $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ apart. The area in $[0,1]^2$ where $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}<\tfrac{1}{2}$ is the quarter-disk $\tfrac{\pi}{4}\cdot(\tfrac{1}{2})^2 = \tfrac{\pi}{16}$.

Each corner contributes **two** ordered blocks (point 1 on side $i$, point 2 on side $i+1$; and vice versa), so the total contribution from adjacent-side pairs is $4 \times 2 \times \tfrac{\pi}{16} = \tfrac{\pi}{2}$.

**Non-adjacent sides.** Points on opposite sides are always at Euclidean distance $\geq 1 > \tfrac{1}{2}$, so they contribute nothing to the "close" region.

**Total area where distance $<\tfrac{1}{2}$:** $3 + \tfrac{\pi}{2}$.

$$P\!\left(\text{dist} < \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{3 + \frac{\pi}{2}}{16} = \frac{6+\pi}{32}.$$

$$P\!\left(\text{dist} \ge \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = 1 - \frac{6+\pi}{32} = \frac{26-\pi}{32}.$$

With $a=26$, $b=1$, $c=32$: since $\gcd(26,1,32)=1$, we have $a+b+c = 26+1+32 = 59$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
