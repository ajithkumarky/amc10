<Problem>
An even number of circles are nested, starting with a radius of $1$ and increasing by $1$ each time, all sharing a common point. The region between every other circle is shaded, starting with the region inside the circle of radius $2$ but outside the circle of radius $1$. An example showing $8$ circles is displayed below. What is the least number of circles needed to make the total shaded area at least $2023\pi$?

$\textbf{(A) } 46 \qquad \textbf{(B) } 48 \qquad \textbf{(C) } 56 \qquad \textbf{(D) } 60 \qquad \textbf{(E) } 64$
</Problem>

<Solution>
Suppose we use $2n$ circles (radii $1, 2, \ldots, 2n$). The shaded regions are:

$$\text{inside circle of radius }2k \text{ but outside circle of radius }2k-1, \quad k = 1, 2, \ldots, n.$$

**Computing the shaded area for each annular ring:**

$$\pi(2k)^2 - \pi(2k-1)^2 = \pi\bigl[(4k^2) - (4k^2 - 4k + 1)\bigr] = \pi(4k - 1).$$

**Total shaded area with $2n$ circles:**

$$A(2n) = \pi \sum_{k=1}^{n}(4k-1) = \pi\!\left(4 \cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2} - n\right) = \pi\,n(2n+1).$$

**Finding the minimum $n$ with $n(2n+1) \ge 2023$:**

$$n=31: \quad 31 \times 63 = 1953 < 2023.$$
$$n=32: \quad 32 \times 65 = 2080 \ge 2023. \checkmark$$

The minimum number of circles is $2 \times 32 = \boxed{64}$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
