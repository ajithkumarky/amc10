<Problem>
A digital display shows the current date as an $8$-digit integer consisting of a $4$-digit year, followed by a $2$-digit month, followed by a $2$-digit date within the month. For example, Arbor Day this year is displayed as $20230428.$ For how many dates in $2023$ does each digit appear an even number of times in the $8$-digit display for that date?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Every date in 2023 starts with $2023$, so the 8-digit display is $2023MMDD$ where $MM$ is the two-digit month (01–12) and $DD$ is the two-digit day.

The digits from $2023$ are: $2, 0, 2, 3$ — that is, one $0$, two $2$s, and one $3$. For every digit in the full 8-digit string to appear an even number of times, the digits from $MMDD$ must contribute:

- An odd number of $0$s (to make the total even, since $2023$ already has one $0$)
- An odd number of $3$s (since $2023$ has one $3$)
- An even number of $2$s (since $2023$ already has two $2$s — already even)
- An even number of all other digits

So $MMDD$ must contain an odd count of $0$s, an odd count of $3$s, and even counts of everything else.

Since $MMDD$ has exactly 4 digits, and we need odd-count digits for exactly $0$ and $3$, we need exactly one $0$ and one $3$ in $MMDD$ (since having three of each would exceed the 4-digit limit in most cases, and two of each would make counts even).

So $MMDD$ is a 4-digit string containing exactly one $0$, exactly one $3$, and two other digits — each of the remaining two digits must appear an even number of times among themselves. Since those two remaining spots hold 2 digits, they must be equal (each appears once... wait, once is odd). Actually, both remaining digits must each appear an even number (0 or 2) of times among those 2 spots. Since there are 2 spots with digits that are neither $0$ nor $3$, those 2 digits must be equal (both the same digit $d \neq 0, 3$, appearing twice — that's even). Or they could both be absent, meaning the remaining 2 spots are filled by $0$s and $3$s — but we've already placed one each, and having two more would make counts even for $0$ and $3$, contradicting our requirement.

So: $MMDD$ has exactly one $0$, one $3$, and the two remaining digits are equal (say $d, d$ for $d \notin \{0,3\}$).

Now let's enumerate valid $(MM, DD)$ pairs.

**Valid months ($MM$) in 2023:** $01$ through $12$.

**Valid days ($DD$):** depends on the month.

The 4 characters of $MMDD$ must be a permutation of $\{0, 3, d, d\}$ for some digit $d \notin \{0,3\}$.

Let me enumerate by considering which arrangement of the month and day digits works.

**Case: $d = 0$ — wait, $d \notin \{0,3\}$.**

Let me list possible month-day combinations directly. The four digits of $MMDD$ are one $0$, one $3$, and two of the same digit $d \in \{1,2,4,5,6,7,8,9\}$.

For each choice of $d$ and arrangement into valid $(MM, DD)$:

$d = 1$: digits are $0,1,1,3$. Possible arrangements for $MMDD$: the four digits in positions $M_1M_2D_1D_2$.
- Months containing these digits: $MM$ is two of $\{0,1,1,3\}$, and $DD$ is the other two.
  - $MM = 01, DD = 13$: Month 01 (January), Day 13. Valid (Jan has 31 days).
  - $MM = 01, DD = 31$: January 31. Valid.
  - $MM = 10, DD = 13$: October 13. Valid.
  - $MM = 10, DD = 31$: October 31. Valid.
  - $MM = 11, DD = 03$: November 3. Valid.
  - $MM = 11, DD = 30$: November 30. Valid (Nov has 30 days).
  - $MM = 13, DD$: 13 is not a valid month.
  - $MM = 31, DD$: not valid.
  - $MM = 03, DD = 11$: March 11. Valid.
  - $MM = 30, DD$: not valid month.
  So for $d=1$: dates are Jan 13, Jan 31, Mar 11, Oct 13, Oct 31, Nov 3, Nov 30 — that's 7 dates.

Wait, I need to be more careful. Let me recount systematically.

$d = 1$: multiset of digits $\{0,1,1,3\}$.
All two-digit combinations for $MM$ (using digits from the multiset):
- $01, 10, 11, 13, 31, 03, 30, 00$ (but $00$ not valid month), also $11$ uses two $1$s.
  Valid months from these: $01, 03, 10, 11$.
  - $MM=01$ uses $0,1$; remaining $DD$ from $\{1,3\}$: $DD=13$ or $DD=31$. Jan 13 ✓, Jan 31 ✓.
  - $MM=03$ uses $0,3$; remaining $DD$ from $\{1,1\}$: $DD=11$. Mar 11 ✓.
  - $MM=10$ uses $1,0$; remaining $DD$ from $\{1,3\}$: $DD=13$ or $DD=31$. Oct 13 ✓, Oct 31 ✓.
  - $MM=11$ uses $1,1$; remaining $DD$ from $\{0,3\}$: $DD=03$ or $DD=30$. Nov 3 ✓, Nov 30 ✓.
  Total for $d=1$: **7 dates**.

$d = 2$: multiset $\{0,2,2,3\}$.
Valid months from this set: $02, 20, 22, 23, 32, 03, 30, 00$.
  Valid months: $02, 03$.
  - $MM=02$ uses $0,2$; remaining $\{2,3\}$: $DD=23$ or $DD=32$. Feb 23 ✓, Feb 32 ✗.
  - $MM=03$ uses $0,3$; remaining $\{2,2\}$: $DD=22$. Mar 22 ✓.
  Total for $d=2$: **2 dates**.

$d = 4$: multiset $\{0,3,4,4\}$.
Valid months: $03, 04, 30, 40, 34, 43, 44, 00$.
  Valid: $03, 04$.
  - $MM=03$ uses $0,3$; remaining $\{4,4\}$: $DD=44$ ✗ (no such day).
  - $MM=04$ uses $0,4$; remaining $\{3,4\}$: $DD=34$ ✗, $DD=43$ ✗.
  Total for $d=4$: **0 dates**.

$d = 5$: multiset $\{0,3,5,5\}$.
Valid months: $03, 05$.
  - $MM=03$ uses $0,3$; remaining $\{5,5\}$: $DD=55$ ✗.
  - $MM=05$ uses $0,5$; remaining $\{3,5\}$: $DD=35$ ✗, $DD=53$ ✗.
  Total for $d=5$: **0 dates**.

$d = 6, 7, 8, 9$: similar — remaining DD values would be too large (like 66, 34-type combos with $d\ge 4$). None valid.

Wait, I should also check if $d$ can form valid months beyond those I listed. For larger $d$, the month must be at most 12. With $d \ge 4$, any month using $d$ would be at most $0d$ or $d0$ etc. — $04, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09$ are valid months, $10, 11, 12$ are valid.

$d=6$: $\{0,3,6,6\}$. Months: $03, 06$. $DD$ options: $66$ ✗ for $MM=03$; $36$ or $63$ ✗ for $MM=06$.

$d=7,8,9$: same pattern — no valid DD possible.

So total dates: $7 + 2 = 9$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
