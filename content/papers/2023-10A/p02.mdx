<Problem>
The weight of $\frac{1}{3}$ of a large pizza together with $3\frac{1}{2}$ cups of orange slices is the same as the weight of $\frac{3}{4}$ of a large pizza together with $\frac{1}{2}$ cup of orange slices. A cup of orange slices weighs $\frac{1}{4}$ of a pound. What is the weight, in pounds, of a large pizza?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $p$ be the weight of a large pizza in pounds. A cup of orange slices weighs $\frac{1}{4}$ lb, so the condition says:

$$\frac{p}{3} + \frac{7}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3p}{4} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4}$$

$$\frac{p}{3} + \frac{7}{8} = \frac{3p}{4} + \frac{1}{8}$$

Subtract $\frac{p}{3}$ from both sides and subtract $\frac{1}{8}$ from both sides:

$$\frac{7}{8} - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{3p}{4} - \frac{p}{3}$$

$$\frac{6}{8} = \frac{9p - 4p}{12} = \frac{5p}{12}$$

$$\frac{3}{4} = \frac{5p}{12} \implies p = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{12}{5} = \frac{9}{5} = 1\frac{4}{5}$$

This matches choice (A).

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
