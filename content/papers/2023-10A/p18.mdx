<Problem>
A rhombic dodecahedron is a solid with $12$ congruent rhombus faces. At every vertex, $3$ or $4$ edges meet, depending on the vertex. How many vertices have exactly $3$ edges meet?

$\textbf{(A) }5\qquad\textbf{(B) }6\qquad\textbf{(C) }7\qquad\textbf{(D) }8\qquad\textbf{(E) }9$
</Problem>

<Solution>
We use Euler's formula and a degree-count argument.

**Step 1: Count faces and edges.**

The rhombic dodecahedron has $F = 12$ faces, each a rhombus (4 sides). Each edge is shared by exactly 2 faces, so:
$$E = \frac{12 \times 4}{2} = 24.$$

**Step 2: Count vertices via Euler's formula.**

$$V - E + F = 2 \implies V = 2 + E - F = 2 + 24 - 12 = 14.$$

**Step 3: Use the handshake lemma.**

Let $v_3$ = number of degree-3 vertices and $v_4$ = number of degree-4 vertices.

$$v_3 + v_4 = 14,$$
$$3v_3 + 4v_4 = 2E = 48.$$

Substituting $v_4 = 14 - v_3$ into the second equation:
$$3v_3 + 4(14 - v_3) = 48 \implies 3v_3 + 56 - 4v_3 = 48 \implies v_3 = 8.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
