<Problem>
Barb the baker has developed a new temperature scale for her bakery called the Breadus scale, which is a linear function of the Fahrenheit scale. Bread rises at $110$ degrees Fahrenheit, which is $0$ degrees on the Breadus scale. Bread is baked at $350$ degrees Fahrenheit, which is $100$ degrees on the Breadus scale. Bread is done when its internal temperature is $200$ degrees Fahrenheit. What is this, in degrees, on the Breadus scale?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The Breadus scale is a linear function of Fahrenheit, so we have the two-point correspondence:

$$110^\circ\text{F} \leftrightarrow 0^\circ\text{Br}, \qquad 350^\circ\text{F} \leftrightarrow 100^\circ\text{Br}.$$

The conversion rate is $\dfrac{100 - 0}{350 - 110} = \dfrac{100}{240} = \dfrac{5}{12}$ Breadus degrees per Fahrenheit degree.

For $200^\circ\text{F}$, the number of Fahrenheit degrees above the $110^\circ$ baseline is $200 - 110 = 90$.

Converting: $90 \times \dfrac{5}{12} = \dfrac{450}{12} = 37.5$ degrees on the Breadus scale.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
