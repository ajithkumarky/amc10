<Problem>
Circle $A$ has radius $100$. Circle $B$ has an integer radius $r < 100$ and remains internally tangent to circle $A$ as it rolls once around the circumference of circle $A$. The two circles have the same points of tangency at the beginning and end of circle $B$'s trip. How many possible values can $r$ have?
</Problem>

<Solution>
When circle $B$ (radius $r$) rolls inside circle $A$ (radius $100$), the center of $B$ traces a circle of radius $100 - r$. As $B$ completes one full trip around the interior, it rotates about its own center.

For the same point of tangency to appear at start and end, circle $B$ must complete a whole number of rotations. The number of rotations $B$ makes about its own center as it travels once around is $\dfrac{100}{r}$ (this comes from the arc length $2\pi(100-r)$ traveled by $B$'s center, giving $\dfrac{2\pi(100-r)}{2\pi r} = \dfrac{100-r}{r}$ rotations relative to a fixed direction, plus 1 for the revolution itself, totaling $\dfrac{100}{r}$).

For the same tangency point: $\dfrac{100}{r}$ must be a positive integer, meaning $r$ must divide $100$.

The divisors of $100 = 2^2 \times 5^2$ are: $1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100$. Since $r < 100$, we exclude $100$.

That gives $8$ possible values.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
