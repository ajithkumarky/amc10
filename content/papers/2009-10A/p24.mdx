<Problem>
Three distinct vertices of a cube are chosen at random. What is the probability that the plane determined by these three vertices contains points inside the cube?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A cube has $8$ vertices, so there are $\binom{8}{3} = 56$ equally likely triples.

A plane through 3 vertices contains interior points of the cube unless it lies entirely on a face. The only planes containing 3 or more cube vertices that avoid the interior are the 6 face planes (any other plane through 3 cube vertices cuts through the interior). Each face has $\binom{4}{3} = 4$ vertex triples, giving $6 \times 4 = 24$ triples that lie on a face.

The remaining $56 - 24 = 32$ triples determine planes that pass through the interior, so the probability is

$$\frac{32}{56} = \frac{4}{7}.$$

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
