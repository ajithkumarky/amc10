<Problem>
Three distinct vertices of a cube are chosen at random. What is the probability that the plane determined by these three vertices contains points inside the cube?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A cube has $8$ vertices. The number of ways to choose $3$ is $\binom{8}{3} = 56$.

We need to count triples that lie on a face or a "diagonal plane" that does NOT pass through the interior.

A plane through 3 vertices does NOT contain interior points exactly when the three points are coplanar with a face of the cube (or a plane that is tangent to the cube, touching only a face or edge). Actually, for a cube, the only planes containing 3 or more vertices that avoid the interior are the 6 face planes and the 6 diagonal planes (each containing 4 vertices, cutting through edges but not the interior... wait, diagonal planes do cut through the interior).

Let me reconsider. A plane "contains points inside the cube" means the plane intersects the interior (open set). The planes that do NOT intersect the interior are exactly the 6 face planes. (Any other plane through 3 cube vertices will intersect the interior.)

Each face has $\binom{4}{3} = 4$ triples of vertices. With 6 faces: $6 \times 4 = 24$ triples lie on a face.

Triples NOT on any face: $56 - 24 = 32$.

Probability the plane contains interior points $= \dfrac{32}{56} = \dfrac{4}{7}$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
