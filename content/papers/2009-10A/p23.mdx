<Problem>
Convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ has $AB = 9$ and $CD = 12$. Diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at $E$, $AC = 14$, and $\triangle AED$ and $\triangle BEC$ have equal areas. What is $AE$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let $AE = x$, so $CE = 14 - x$.

Since $\triangle AED$ and $\triangle BEC$ share the angle at $E$ (vertical angles), their areas depend on the sides from $E$:

$$[\triangle AED] = \frac{1}{2} \cdot AE \cdot DE \cdot \sin\theta, \quad [\triangle BEC] = \frac{1}{2} \cdot CE \cdot BE \cdot \sin\theta,$$

where $\theta$ is the angle at $E$. Setting these equal:

$$AE \cdot DE = CE \cdot BE \implies \frac{AE}{CE} = \frac{BE}{DE}.$$

Also note $[\triangle ABE]$ and $[\triangle CDE]$ share the same angle at $E$:

$$[\triangle ABE] = \frac{1}{2} \cdot AE \cdot BE \cdot \sin\theta, \quad [\triangle CDE] = \frac{1}{2} \cdot CE \cdot DE \cdot \sin\theta.$$

From $AE \cdot DE = CE \cdot BE$, triangles $AED$ and $BEC$ are similar (by SAS similarity with the equal angle at $E$):

$$\frac{AE}{BE} = \frac{DE}{CE} = \frac{AD}{BC}.$$

Wait, let me use the equal-area condition more directly. Equal areas of $\triangle AED$ and $\triangle BEC$ means:

$$[\triangle ABD] = [\triangle AED] + [\triangle ABE] = [\triangle BEC] + [\triangle ABE] = [\triangle ABC].$$

Triangles $ABD$ and $ABC$ share base $AB$, so they have equal areas, meaning $D$ and $C$ are equidistant from line $AB$. This means $DC \parallel AB$.

With $DC \parallel AB$, triangles $AEB$ and $DEC$ are similar (AA: vertical angles at $E$, and alternate interior angles). So:

$$\frac{AE}{CE} = \frac{BE}{DE} = \frac{AB}{DC} = \frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}.$$

Therefore $AE = \frac{3}{7} \cdot AC = \frac{3}{7} \cdot 14 = 6$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
