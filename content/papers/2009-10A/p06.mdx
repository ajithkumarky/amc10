<Problem>
A circle of radius $2$ is inscribed in a semicircle, as shown. The area inside the semicircle but outside the circle is shaded. What fraction of the semicircle's area is shaded?

The semicircle has radius $4$ (the inscribed circle of radius $2$ sits tangent to the diameter and to the curved arc, so the semicircle's radius equals the diameter of the inscribed circle, which is $4$).
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since the inscribed circle has radius $2$ and is tangent to the flat diameter and to the curved part, the center of the small circle is at height $2$ above the diameter. For the small circle to be tangent internally to the semicircle, the distance from the semicircle's center to the small circle's center plus the small circle's radius equals the semicircle's radius. The small circle's center lies on the vertical axis of symmetry at distance $2$ from the flat edge, so its distance from the semicircle's center is $2$. Thus $2 + 2 = 4$, confirming the semicircle has radius $4$.

- Area of semicircle: $\dfrac{1}{2}\pi(4)^2 = 8\pi$.
- Area of small circle: $\pi(2)^2 = 4\pi$.
- Shaded area: $8\pi - 4\pi = 4\pi$.
- Fraction shaded: $\dfrac{4\pi}{8\pi} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
