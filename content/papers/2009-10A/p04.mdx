<Problem>
Eric plans to compete in a triathlon. He can average $2$ miles per hour in the $\tfrac{1}{4}$-mile swim and $6$ miles per hour in the $3$-mile run. His goal is to finish the triathlon in $2$ hours. To accomplish his goal, what must his average speed in miles per hour be for the $15$-mile bicycle ride?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Compute the time spent on the swim and run, then find what's left for the bike.

- Swim: $\dfrac{1/4}{2} = \dfrac{1}{8}$ hour.
- Run: $\dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ hour.

Time remaining for the bike: $2 - \dfrac{1}{8} - \dfrac{1}{2} = 2 - \dfrac{1}{8} - \dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{11}{8}$ hours.

Required bike speed: $\dfrac{15}{11/8} = \dfrac{15 \times 8}{11} = \dfrac{120}{11}$ mph.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
