<Problem>
Four congruent rectangles are placed as shown. The area of the outer square is $4$ times that of the inner square. What is the ratio of the length of the longer side of each rectangle to the length of its shorter side?

The four rectangles are arranged around a central square, each aligned with one side of the outer square, forming a pinwheel pattern.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the shorter side of each rectangle be $a$ and the longer side be $b$.

From the Asymptote code, the rectangles span from $(1,1)$ to $(-2,1)$ to $(-2,2)$ to $(1,2)$ in one orientation, so the shorter side is $1$ and longer side is $3$, giving ratio $3$. Let's verify with the area condition.

The outer square has side length $a + b$ (the rectangle's longer side plus the inner square's side, where the inner square's side is $b - a$):

Actually, set up variables: the inner square has side $s$. Each rectangle has shorter side $a$ and longer side wrapping to form the outer square. The outer square's side $= a + s + a$? No — the four rectangles each occupy one "arm."

From the symmetric arrangement, the outer square's side $= b$ and the inner square's side $= b - 2a$. Then:

$$\text{outer area} = b^2, \quad \text{inner area} = (b-2a)^2.$$

Condition: $b^2 = 4(b-2a)^2$, so $b = 2(b-2a)$ (taking the positive root since $b > 2a$):

$$b = 2b - 4a \implies b = 4a \implies \frac{b}{a} = 4.$$

Hmm, but the Asymptote coordinates give ratio 3. Let me re-examine. From the code, one rectangle goes from $(1,1)$ to $(-2,2)$: width $= 3$, height $= 1$. The outer square formed by all four (rotated 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) has vertices at $(\pm 2, \pm 2)$... actually the outer boundary forms a square of side $3$ (from $-2$ to $1$ is $3$ units), and the inner square has side $1$ (from $-1$ to $1$? Let's check: the inner square corner is at $(1,1)$, so inner square has side $= 2$).

Actually from the Asymptote path, the inner square corners are at $(\pm 1, \pm 1)$, side $= 2$; outer square side $= 3$ (from $-2$ to $1$... that's $3$, but then the other direction goes $-1$ to $2$ giving $3$). So outer side $= 3$, inner side $= 2$, outer area $= 9$, inner area $= 4$. But $9 \neq 4 \times 4$. So the areas: outer/inner $= 9/4$, not $4$.

Using the area ratio condition properly with longer side $b$ and shorter side $a$: the outer square has side $= a + b$ and inner square has side $= b - a$. Area condition: $(a+b)^2 = 4(b-a)^2$. Taking positive square root: $a+b = 2(b-a) = 2b - 2a$, so $3a = b$, giving ratio $b/a = 3$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
