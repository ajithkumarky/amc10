<Problem>
A solid cube of side length $1$ is removed from each corner of a solid cube of side length $3$. How many edges does the remaining solid have?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Place the large cube with corners at $(0,0,0)$ to $(3,3,3)$. Remove a unit cube from each of the 8 corners; focus on the corner at $(0,0,0)$, removing $[0,1]^3$.

**Original edges (trimmed).** Each of the 12 edges of the large cube loses its two end unit-segments (one per removed corner), leaving a length-1 middle segment. This gives $12$ edges.

**Cut-surface edges.** Removing the unit cube at $(0,0,0)$ exposes three new $1\times 1$ square faces (on the planes $x=1$, $y=1$, $z=1$ within the removed region). Each new square face has 2 edges that lie on a face of the large cube and 2 interior edges. Listing the edges on cube faces for the corner at $(0,0,0)$:
- On $z=0$: $(1,0,0)\text{–}(1,1,0)$ and $(0,1,0)\text{–}(1,1,0)$
- On $y=0$: $(1,0,0)\text{–}(1,0,1)$ and $(0,0,1)\text{–}(1,0,1)$
- On $x=0$: $(0,1,0)\text{–}(0,1,1)$ and $(0,0,1)\text{–}(0,1,1)$

That is 6 new cut-surface edges per corner, none shared between corners. Total: $8 \times 6 = 48$ edges.

**Interior edges.** The three new square faces at each corner meet pairwise along interior edges (inside the notch). There are 3 such pairs per corner, giving $8 \times 3 = 24$ interior edges.

**Total:**
$$12 + 48 + 24 = 84.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
