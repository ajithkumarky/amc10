<Problem>
Let points $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (1, 2)$, $C = (3, 3)$, and $D = (4, 0)$. Quadrilateral $ABCD$ is cut into equal area pieces by a line passing through $A$. This line intersects $\overline{CD}$ at point $\left(\dfrac{p}{q}, \dfrac{r}{s}\right)$, where these fractions are in lowest terms. What is $p + q + r + s$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Find the area of quadrilateral $ABCD$** using the shoelace formula with $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,2)$, $C=(3,3)$, $D=(4,0)$:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}|x_A(y_B-y_D)+x_B(y_C-y_A)+x_C(y_D-y_B)+x_D(y_A-y_C)|$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}|0(2-0)+1(3-0)+3(0-2)+4(0-3)|$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}|0+3-6-12| = \frac{1}{2}\cdot 15 = \frac{15}{2}.$$

The line through $A$ must divide this into two equal areas of $\frac{15}{4}$ each.

**Find point $E$ on $\overline{CD}$:** Let $E = (4-t, 3-3t/1)$... Let me parametrize $\overline{CD}$. $C=(3,3)$, $D=(4,0)$. Parametrically: $E = (3+t, 3-3t)$ for $t \in [0,1]$.

The line through $A=(0,0)$ and $E=(3+t, 3-3t)$ divides the quadrilateral into:
- Triangle/polygon $A$-$B$-$C$-$E$ (above the line)
- Triangle $A$-$E$-$D$ (below the line)

**Area of triangle $AED$:** with $A=(0,0)$, $E=(3+t,3-3t)$, $D=(4,0)$:
$$= \frac{1}{2}|x_A(y_E-y_D)+x_E(y_D-y_A)+x_D(y_A-y_E)|$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}|0(3-3t-0)+(3+t)(0-0)+4(0-(3-3t))|$$
$$= \frac{1}{2}|0+0+4(-3+3t)| = \frac{1}{2}|12t-12| = 6|t-1|.$$

For $t \in [0,1]$: Area $= 6(1-t)$.

Set this equal to $\frac{15}{4}$:
$$6(1-t) = \frac{15}{4} \implies 1-t = \frac{15}{24} = \frac{5}{8} \implies t = \frac{3}{8}.$$

So $E = \left(3+\frac{3}{8}, 3-\frac{9}{8}\right) = \left(\frac{27}{8}, \frac{15}{8}\right)$.

Check: both fractions in lowest terms? $\gcd(27,8)=1$ ✓, $\gcd(15,8)=1$ ✓.

So $p=27$, $q=8$, $r=15$, $s=8$.

$p+q+r+s = 27+8+15+8 = 58$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
