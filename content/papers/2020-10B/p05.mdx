<Problem>
How many distinguishable arrangements are there of $1$ brown tile, $1$ purple tile, $2$ green tiles, and $3$ yellow tiles in a row from left to right? (Tiles of the same color are indistinguishable.)
</Problem>

<Solution>
We have $1 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 7$ tiles total. If all tiles were distinct, there would be $7!$ arrangements. We divide by the number of ways to permute identical tiles among themselves:

$$\frac{7!}{1! \cdot 1! \cdot 2! \cdot 3!} = \frac{5040}{1 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 6} = \frac{5040}{12} = 420.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
