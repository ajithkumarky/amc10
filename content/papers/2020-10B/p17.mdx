<Problem>
There are $10$ people standing equally spaced around a circle. Each person knows exactly $3$ of the other $9$ people: the $2$ people standing next to him or her, as well as the person directly across the circle. How many ways are there for the $10$ people to split up into $5$ pairs so that the members of each pair know each other?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Label the people $0, 1, 2, \ldots, 9$ around the circle. Each person knows their two neighbors and the person directly opposite, giving 10 adjacent edges and 5 opposite edges. We count 5-edge perfect matchings using only these edges.

Branch on the partner of person $0$ (who can pair with $1$, $5$, or $9$):

**Case A: $0$ pairs with $5$ (the opposite pair $\{0,5\}$).**

Branch on the partner of $9$ (can pair with $8$ or $4$):
- $9$–$8$: vertex $4$ is forced to pair with $3$; then $\{1,2\},\{6,7\}$ or $\{1,6\},\{2,7\}$ — giving **2** matchings.
- $9$–$4$: vertex $8$ can pair with $7$ or $3$:
  - $8$–$7$: forces $\{2,3\},\{1,6\}$ — **1** matching.
  - $8$–$3$: vertex $7$ pairs with $6$ or $2$, each valid — **2** matchings.

Case A subtotal: $2 + 1 + 2 = \mathbf{5}$ matchings.

**Case B: $0$ pairs with $1$.**

Branch on the partner of $9$ (can pair with $8$ or $4$):
- $9$–$8$: vertex $5$ pairs with $4$ or $6$; each choice forces the rest uniquely — **2** matchings.
- $9$–$4$: vertex $5$ is forced to pair with $6$; then vertex $8$ pairs with $7$ or $3$, each valid — **2** matchings.

Case B subtotal: $2 + 2 = \mathbf{4}$ matchings.

**Case C: $0$ pairs with $9$.**

By the reflection symmetry swapping $1 \leftrightarrow 9$, $2 \leftrightarrow 8$, $3 \leftrightarrow 7$, $4 \leftrightarrow 6$, Case C is identical in structure to Case B — **4** matchings.

**Total:** $5 + 4 + 4 = \mathbf{13}$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
