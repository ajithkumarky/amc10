<Problem>
In a certain card game, a player is dealt a hand of $10$ cards from a deck of $52$ distinct cards. The number of distinct (unordered) hands that can be dealt to the player can be written as $158A00A4AA0$. What is the digit $A$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We need to find $\binom{52}{10}$ and identify the repeated digit $A$.

**Compute $\binom{52}{10}$:**

$$\binom{52}{10} = \frac{52!}{10!\cdot 42!} = \frac{52\cdot51\cdot50\cdot49\cdot48\cdot47\cdot46\cdot45\cdot44\cdot43}{10!}$$

The denominator is $10! = 3628800$.

Compute the numerator step by step:
- $52 \cdot 51 = 2652$
- $2652 \cdot 50 = 132600$
- $132600 \cdot 49 = 6497400$
- $6497400 \cdot 48 = 311875200$
- $311875200 \cdot 47 = 14658134400$
- $14658134400 \cdot 46 = 674274182400$
- $674274182400 \cdot 45 = 30342338208000$
- $30342338208000 \cdot 44 = 1335062841152000$
- $1335062841152000 \cdot 43 = 57407702169536000$

Now divide by $3628800$:

$$\binom{52}{10} = \frac{57407702169536000}{3628800}$$

Let's simplify incrementally. Divide numerator and denominator step by step, or use the standard fact:

$$\binom{52}{10} = 15820024220$$

Let's verify the digit pattern. The problem states the answer is $158A00A4AA0$, an 11-digit number.

$15820024220$ has 11 digits. Matching: $1\,5\,8\,2\,0\,0\,2\,4\,2\,2\,0$.

Comparing to $1\,5\,8\,A\,0\,0\,A\,4\,A\,A\,0$: all four $A$'s equal $2$.

Therefore $A = 2$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
