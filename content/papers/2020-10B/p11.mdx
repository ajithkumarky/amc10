<Problem>
Ms. Carr asks her students to read any $5$ of the $10$ books on a reading list. Harold randomly selects $5$ books from this list, and Betty does the same. What is the probability that there are exactly $2$ books that they both select?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The total number of ways Harold can choose his 5 books is $\binom{10}{5} = 252$. Betty independently does the same, so the total number of pairs of choices is $252^2$.

For the favorable outcomes, we need exactly $2$ books in common:

1. Choose the $2$ books both select: $\binom{10}{2} = 45$ ways.
2. Harold picks $3$ more from the remaining $8$ books: $\binom{8}{3} = 56$ ways.
3. Betty picks $3$ more from the $8 - 3 = 5$ books Harold did not pick (to ensure no additional overlap): $\binom{5}{3} = 10$ ways.

Favorable outcomes: $45 \times 56 \times 10 = 25{,}200$.

Probability:
$$\frac{25{,}200}{252^2} = \frac{25{,}200}{63{,}504} = \frac{25{,}200}{63{,}504}.$$

Simplify: divide numerator and denominator by $25{,}200$:
$$\frac{1}{63{,}504/25{,}200} = \frac{25200}{63504}.$$

Let's simplify by finding GCD. Divide both by $504$: $25200/504 = 50$ and $63504/504 = 126$. So we get $\dfrac{50}{126} = \dfrac{25}{63}$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
