<Problem>
A square of side length $1$ and a circle of radius $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ share the same center. What is the area inside the circle, but outside the square?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The circle has radius $r = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, so area $= \pi r^2 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

The square has side $1$, so half-side $= \dfrac{1}{2}$. The distance from the center to each side is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $r = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.577 > 0.5$, the circle extends outside the square through all four sides.

**Area inside circle but outside square** $=$ (circle area) $-$ (area inside both).

By symmetry, consider one side. The circle intersects a side at distance $\dfrac{1}{2}$ from center. The half-chord length is $\sqrt{r^2 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{12}} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}$.

The full chord length is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. The area of the circular segment outside the square on one side is the area of the circular sector minus the triangle.

The half-angle $\theta$ satisfies $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1/2}{1/\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $\theta = 30°= \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The full sector angle is $2\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

Area of one sector $= \dfrac{1}{2}r^2 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{18}$.

Area of the triangle within the sector $= \dfrac{1}{2}r^2 \sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{12}$.

Area of one segment (outside square) $= \dfrac{\pi}{18} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{12}$.

There are 4 such segments (one per side), so total area outside square:
$$4\left(\frac{\pi}{18} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{12}\right) = \frac{4\pi}{18} - \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{12} = \frac{2\pi}{9} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
