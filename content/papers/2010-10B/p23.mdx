<Problem>
The entries in a $3 \times 3$ array include all the digits from $1$ through $9$, arranged so that the entries in every row and column are in increasing order. How many such arrays are there?
</Problem>

<Solution>
We need to place digits $1$–$9$ in a $3 \times 3$ grid so that every row and every column is increasing (read left to right and top to bottom). These are called **standard Young tableaux** of shape $3 \times 3$.

The digit $1$ must go in position $(1,1)$ and $9$ must go in position $(3,3)$.

We choose which 3 of the remaining digits $\{2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}$ go in the first row (along with 1) and which 3 go in the second row; the last 3 go in the third row. The positions within each row and column are then forced.

However, not every choice of rows is valid — we also need columns to be increasing. The count of such tableaux for a $3 \times 3$ shape is given by the hook length formula:

$$f = \frac{9!}{\prod_{\text{cells}} h(c)}$$

where $h(c)$ is the hook length at cell $c$.

The hook lengths for a $3 \times 3$ grid are:
- Row 1: $5, 4, 3$
- Row 2: $4, 3, 2$
- Row 3: $3, 2, 1$

Product of hook lengths $= 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 5 \cdot 4^2 \cdot 3^3 \cdot 2^2 \cdot 1 = 5 \cdot 16 \cdot 27 \cdot 4 = 8640$.

$$f = \frac{9!}{8640} = \frac{362880}{8640} = 42.$$

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
