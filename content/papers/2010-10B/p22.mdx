<Problem>
Seven distinct pieces of candy are to be distributed among three bags. The red bag and the blue bag must each receive at least one piece of candy; the white bag may remain empty. How many arrangements are possible?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Each piece of candy can go into any of the 3 bags, giving $3^7 = 2187$ total distributions without restriction.

Use inclusion-exclusion to subtract distributions where the red bag is empty or the blue bag is empty.

Let $R$ = "red bag empty," $B$ = "blue bag empty."

$|R|$: all 7 candies in blue or white $= 2^7 = 128$.
$|B|$: all 7 candies in red or white $= 2^7 = 128$.
$|R \cap B|$: all 7 candies in white only $= 1^7 = 1$.

By inclusion-exclusion:
$$|R \cup B| = 128 + 128 - 1 = 255.$$

Valid arrangements $= 2187 - 255 = 1932$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
