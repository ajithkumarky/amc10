<Problem>
A bee is moving in three-dimensional space. A fair six-sided die with faces labeled $A^+$, $A^-$, $B^+$, $B^-$, $C^+$, and $C^-$ is rolled. Suppose the bee occupies the point $(a, b, c)$. If the die shows $A^+$, then the bee moves to the point $(a+1, b, c)$ and if the die shows $A^-$, then the bee moves to the point $(a-1, b, c)$. Analogous moves are made with the other four outcomes. Suppose the bee starts at the point $(0, 0, 0)$ and the die is rolled four times. What is the probability that the bee traverses four distinct edges of some unit cube?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The total number of equally likely 4-move sequences is $6^4 = 1296$.

We count sequences in which the four moves trace four **distinct** (undirected) edges, all belonging to a single unit cube. "Distinct" means the path never retraces an edge in either direction.

Since the bee starts at the origin, the relevant unit cube is determined by which directions the bee moves: for each axis, the cube extends either in the $+$ or $-$ direction. There are $2^3 = 8$ such cubes (one per octant). By symmetry it suffices to count valid sequences for one octant and multiply.

**Case 1: The path uses all three axes** (one axis used twice, the other two once each).

Consider the positive octant cube with corners in $\{0,1\}^3$, with axis $X$ repeated. The moves must be one $X^+$, one $X^-$, one $Y^+$, one $Z^+$ (directions forced by the starting point and the cube). The path stays in the cube as long as $X^+$ precedes $X^-$, giving $\frac{4!}{2} = 12$ orderings. However, $X^+$ and $X^-$ traverse the **same** undirected edge whenever the $Y$- and $Z$-coordinates are identical at both $X$-steps — i.e., whenever neither $Y^+$ nor $Z^+$ occurs between them.

Enumerating all 12 orderings shows that exactly 6 have $X^+$ and $X^-$ on the same undirected edge (the two $X$-steps are adjacent or both $Y^+, Z^+$ appear entirely before or entirely after both $X$-steps). The remaining $12 - 6 = 6$ orderings give four distinct edges.

With 8 octants and 3 choices of repeated axis: $8 \times 3 \times 6 = 144$ valid sequences.

**Case 2: The path forms a square** (two axes each used twice, returning to the start).

For axis pair $\{X, Y\}$ and the positive-$x$, positive-$y$ face, the two valid cyclic orderings are $(X^+, Y^+, X^-, Y^-)$ and $(Y^+, X^+, Y^-, X^-)$, tracing the four edges of a square face. With $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ axis pairs, $2^2 = 4$ face orientations each, and 2 orderings each: $3 \times 4 \times 2 = 24$ valid sequences.

**Total favorable outcomes:** $144 + 24 = 168$.

$$P = \frac{168}{1296} = \frac{7}{54}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
