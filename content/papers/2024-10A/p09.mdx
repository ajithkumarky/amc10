<Problem>
In how many ways can $6$ juniors and $6$ seniors form $3$ disjoint teams of $4$ people so that each team has $2$ juniors and $2$ seniors?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Partition the 6 juniors into 3 unordered pairs: $\dfrac{\binom{6}{2}\binom{4}{2}\binom{2}{2}}{3!} = \dfrac{90}{6} = 15$ ways. Do the same for the 6 seniors: $15$ ways. Then match the 3 junior-pairs with the 3 senior-pairs in $3! = 6$ ways to form the teams.

$$15 \times 15 \times 6 = 1350.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
