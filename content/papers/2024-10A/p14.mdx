<Problem>
One side of an equilateral triangle of height $24$ lies on line $\ell$. A circle of radius $12$ is tangent to line $\ell$ and is externally tangent to the triangle. The area of the region exterior to the triangle and the circle and bounded by the triangle, the circle, and line $\ell$ can be written as $a\sqrt{b} - c\pi$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are positive integers and $b$ is not divisible by the square of any prime. What is $a + b + c$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Set up the geometry.**

An equilateral triangle of height $h = 24$ has side length $s = \dfrac{24}{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}} = 16\sqrt{3}$.

Place the triangle with its base on line $\ell$. The base vertices are at $(\pm 8\sqrt{3}, 0)$ and the apex is at $(0, 24)$.

The circle of radius $12$ is tangent to $\ell$ (from above), so its center is at $(x_0, 12)$ for some $x_0$. It is externally tangent to the triangle, meaning it touches the nearest slanted side.

**Find the circle center.**

By symmetry, place the circle to one side. The left slanted side runs from $(-8\sqrt{3}, 0)$ to $(0, 24)$. Its equation:

Direction vector: $(8\sqrt{3}, 24)$, so slope $= \dfrac{24}{8\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}$.

Line: $y - 0 = \sqrt{3}(x + 8\sqrt{3})$, i.e., $\sqrt{3}x - y + 24 = 0$.

Distance from center $(x_0, 12)$ to this line equals $12$ (external tangency):
$$\frac{|\sqrt{3}\,x_0 - 12 + 24|}{2} = 12 \implies |\sqrt{3}\,x_0 + 12| = 24.$$

Since the circle is outside (to the left of) the triangle: $\sqrt{3}\,x_0 + 12 = -24$, giving $x_0 = -\dfrac{36}{\sqrt{3}} = -12\sqrt{3}$.

**Identify the bounded region.**

The region is bounded above by the triangle's two slanted sides and the circle arc, and below by line $\ell$. It lies between the circle and the triangle (to the left), inside the "wedge" formed by $\ell$ and the triangle's left side, but exterior to both the triangle and the circle.

More precisely: the region is the area inside the "corner" formed by line $\ell$ and the left side of the triangle (a $30°$-$60°$-$90°$ wedge region meeting at the left base vertex), minus the portion of the circle inside that corner.

**Corner angle at the base vertex.**

At vertex $(-8\sqrt{3}, 0)$: the interior angle of the equilateral triangle is $60°$, so the exterior corner angle between line $\ell$ and the left slant side (on the outside) is $180° - 60° = 120°$. Wait — the region outside the triangle below the slant side and above $\ell$ forms an angle of $180° - 60° = 120°$. But the circle sits in this $120°$ wedge.

Actually, let me reconsider. The angle between $\ell$ (horizontal) and the slant side at the left vertex: the slant side rises at $60°$ from horizontal (since the triangle is equilateral). The region between $\ell$ and the outside of the slant forms a $180° - 60° = 120°$ angle at the left base vertex.

**Area of the region.**

The bounded region is: the right triangle formed by the tangent point, the vertex, and the foot — minus the circular sector.

Let $V = (-8\sqrt{3}, 0)$ be the left base vertex. The circle is tangent to $\ell$ at point $P = (-12\sqrt{3}, 0)$ and tangent to the slant side at point $Q$.

The region is bounded by: segment $VP$ on $\ell$, the arc $PQ$ on the circle, and segment $QV$ on the slant side.

$VP = |-12\sqrt{3} - (-8\sqrt{3})| = 4\sqrt{3}$.

The distance from the circle center $C = (-12\sqrt{3}, 12)$ to $V = (-8\sqrt{3}, 0)$:
$$CV = \sqrt{(-4\sqrt{3})^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{48 + 144} = \sqrt{192} = 8\sqrt{3}.$$

The angle $\angle CVP$: $\tan(\angle CVP) = \frac{12}{4\sqrt{3}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}$, so $\angle CVP = 60°$.

But the corner angle at $V$ between $\ell$ and the slant is $120°$, and $\angle CVP = 60°$ (the center lies on the angle bisector of the $120°$ corner, since it's equidistant from both sides). So the half-angle is $60°$. ✓

**Area of the wedge triangle $VPQ$** (the triangle formed by the two tangent lines and the chord $PQ$, i.e., the curvilinear region):

The area of triangle $VCT$ where $T$ is the tangent point: use the right triangles from $C$ to each tangent line.

$VP = CQ_\perp$-type: from the tangency, $VC \cdot \sin(60°) = 12$, so $VC = \frac{12}{\sin 60°} = \frac{12}{\sqrt{3}/2} = 8\sqrt{3}$ ✓.

Area of triangle $VPC$: $\frac{1}{2} \cdot VP \cdot CP = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4\sqrt{3} \cdot 12 = 24\sqrt{3}$.

By symmetry (the two tangent lines from $V$ are equal), the triangle $VPQ$ (with $P$ and $Q$ the two tangent points) has area $= 2 \times 24\sqrt{3} = 48\sqrt{3}$.

The angle at $C$ subtended by arc $PQ$: the angle $\angle PCQ = 180° - 60° = 120°$ (since $\angle PVC = 60°$ and the quadrilateral $VPCQ$... actually $\angle CVP = 60°$, and $\angle CPV = 90°$, so $\angle PCQ = 180° - 2\cdot 60° = 60°$... let me redo).
