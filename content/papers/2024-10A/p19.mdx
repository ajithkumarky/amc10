<Problem>
The first three terms of a geometric sequence are the integers $a$, $720$, and $b$, where $a < 720 < b$. What is the sum of the digits of the least possible value of $b$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
For a geometric sequence $a, 720, b$, the middle-term property gives $720^2 = ab$, so $b = \dfrac{518400}{a}$.

To minimize $b$ we maximize $a$, subject to $a < 720$ and $a \mid 518400$. Since $720 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$, we have $518400 = 2^8 \cdot 3^4 \cdot 5^2$.

We search for the largest divisor of $2^8 \cdot 3^4 \cdot 5^2$ strictly below $720$. Checking candidates just below $720$:

- $2^3 \cdot 3^4 = 648$: gives $b = 800$.
- $3^3 \cdot 5^2 = 675$: gives $b = 768$. Since $675 > 648$, this is better.

No divisor of $518400$ lies in the interval $(675, 720)$: every such candidate either introduces a prime factor outside $\{2,3,5\}$ (e.g., $700 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2 \cdot 7$) or evaluates to exactly $720$ (e.g., $2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 720$). A systematic check of all products $2^\alpha 3^\beta 5^\gamma$ with $\alpha \le 8$, $\beta \le 4$, $\gamma \le 2$ confirms the next divisor above $675$ is $720$ itself.

Therefore $a = 675$ is optimal, giving $b = 768$.

**Sum of digits of $768$:** $7 + 6 + 8 = 21$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
