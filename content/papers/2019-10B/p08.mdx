<Problem>
A square contains four equilateral triangles, with each triangle having a side lying on a side of the square. Each triangle has side length $2$, and the third (apex) vertices of all four triangles meet at the center of the square. The region inside the square but outside the four triangles is shaded. What is the area of the shaded region?

Because each equilateral triangle has a base of length $2$ on a side of the square and its apex reaches the center, the square's side equals twice the triangle height: side $= 2\sqrt{3}$.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Each equilateral triangle has side $2$, so its height is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot 2 = \sqrt{3}$. The apex of each triangle reaches the center of the square, and the apex sits at distance (height) $\sqrt{3}$ from the side. Since the center is half the square's side away from each side, the side length is $2\sqrt{3}$, and the square's area is

$$(2\sqrt{3})^2 = 12.$$

Each equilateral triangle of side $2$ has area $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\cdot 2^2 = \sqrt{3}$. The four triangles meet only at the center point, so they do not overlap in area. Their combined area is $4\sqrt{3}$.

The shaded region is what remains:

$$12 - 4\sqrt{3}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
