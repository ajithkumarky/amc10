<Problem>
Consider the statement, "If $n$ is not prime, then $n-2$ is prime." Which of the following values of $n$ is a counterexample to this statement?
</Problem>

<Solution>
A counterexample needs $n$ to be **not prime** (so the hypothesis holds) while $n-2$ is **also not prime** (so the conclusion fails). We just check each choice.

- $n=11$: prime, so the hypothesis fails — not a counterexample.
- $n=15$: not prime, and $15-2=13$ is prime — statement holds here.
- $n=19$: prime — hypothesis fails.
- $n=21$: not prime, and $21-2=19$ is prime — statement holds.
- $n=27$: not prime, and $27-2=25=5^2$ is not prime — hypothesis holds but conclusion fails.

So $n=27$ is the counterexample.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
