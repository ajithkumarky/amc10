<Problem>
There is a real $n$ such that $(n+1)! + (n+2)! = n! \cdot 440$. What is the sum of the digits of $n$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Factor $n!$ out of the left side. Since $(n+1)! = (n+1)\cdot n!$ and $(n+2)! = (n+2)(n+1)\cdot n!$,

$$(n+1)! + (n+2)! = n!\big[(n+1) + (n+2)(n+1)\big] = n!\,(n+1)\big[1 + (n+2)\big] = n!\,(n+1)(n+3).$$

Setting this equal to $n! \cdot 440$ and dividing by $n!$,

$$(n+1)(n+3) = 440.$$

We need two factors of $440$ differing by $2$. Since $440 = 20 \cdot 22$, take $n+1 = 20$ and $n+3 = 22$, giving $n = 19$.

The sum of the digits of $19$ is $1 + 9 = 10$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
