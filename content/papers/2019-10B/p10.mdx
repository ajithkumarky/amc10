<Problem>
In a given plane, points $A$ and $B$ are $10$ units apart. How many points $C$ are there in the plane such that the perimeter of $\triangle ABC$ is $50$ units and the area of $\triangle ABC$ is $100$ square units?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Take base $AB = 10$. The area condition fixes the height from $C$ to line $AB$:

$$\frac{1}{2}\cdot 10 \cdot h = 100 \implies h = 20.$$

So $C$ must lie on one of the two lines parallel to $AB$ at distance $20$.

Now use the perimeter condition: $AC + BC = 50 - 10 = 40$. This says $C$ lies on an **ellipse** with foci $A$ and $B$, where the sum of distances is $40$. The semi-major axis is $a = 20$, and with $c = 5$ (half of $AB$), the semi-minor axis is

$$b = \sqrt{a^2 - c^2} = \sqrt{400 - 25} = \sqrt{375} \approx 19.36.$$

The maximum distance from line $AB$ to a point on this ellipse is exactly $b \approx 19.36$, which is **less than** the required height $20$. So no point on the ellipse is far enough from $AB$ to give area $100$.

There are no such points $C$.

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
