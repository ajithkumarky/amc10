<Problem>
Three equally spaced parallel lines intersect a circle, creating three chords of lengths $38, 38,$ and $34$. What is the distance between two adjacent parallel lines?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the three equally-spaced parallel lines be at $y = 0$, $y = d$, and $y = 2d$, and let the circle have center at height $h$ and radius $r$. The chord at perpendicular distance $t$ from the center has length $2\sqrt{r^2 - t^2}$.

The two equal chords of length 38 cannot be the symmetric outer pair (lines $y=0$ and $y=2d$): that would force $h = d$ (center on the middle line), making the middle chord the longest, but $34 < 38$, a contradiction.

So the two 38-chords come from two adjacent lines, say $y = 0$ and $y = d$, with the 34-chord from $y = 2d$. Equal chord lengths at $y=0$ and $y=d$ force the center to be equidistant from those two lines, giving $h = d/2$. The three distances from the center to the lines are then $d/2$, $d/2$, and $3d/2$.

Setting up the two chord equations:

$$2\sqrt{r^2 - (d/2)^2} = 38 \implies r^2 - \tfrac{d^2}{4} = 361,$$
$$2\sqrt{r^2 - (3d/2)^2} = 34 \implies r^2 - \tfrac{9d^2}{4} = 289.$$

Subtracting the second from the first:

$$\frac{9d^2}{4} - \frac{d^2}{4} = 361 - 289 \implies 2d^2 = 72 \implies d = 6.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
