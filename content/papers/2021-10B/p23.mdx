<Problem>
A square with side length $8$ is colored white except for $4$ black isosceles right triangular regions with legs of length $2$ in each corner of the square and a black diamond with side length $2\sqrt{2}$ in the center of the square. A circular coin with diameter $1$ is dropped onto the square and lands in a random location where the coin is completely contained within the square. The probability that the coin will cover part of the black region of the square can be written as $\dfrac{1}{196}\left(a + b\sqrt{2} + \pi\right)$, where $a$ and $b$ are positive integers. What is $a + b$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
The coin (radius $1/2$) lands uniformly on the square, with the coin's center constrained to be at least $1/2$ from every edge. So the center ranges over a $7 \times 7$ square (from $1/2$ to $7.5$ in each coordinate, given the $8 \times 8$ square from $0$ to $8$). The valid area for the center is $49$.

The coin covers part of the black region if and only if the coin's center is within $1/2$ of some black point, i.e., the center lies in the "expanded" black region (Minkowski sum of black region with disk of radius $1/2$), intersected with the $7\times 7$ valid square.

**Set up coordinates** with the square from $(0,0)$ to $(8,8)$. Center valid region: $[1/2, 15/2]^2$.

**Black corner triangles.** Each corner has an isosceles right triangle with legs $2$. For the bottom-left corner: vertices $(0,0)$, $(2,0)$, $(0,2)$ — the region where $x + y \leq 2$, $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$.

The expanded region (points within $1/2$ of this triangle) includes:
- The triangle itself expanded outward by $1/2$.
- Intersected with the valid center region $[1/2, 15/2]^2$.

The coin center is within $1/2$ of the corner triangle if and only if $x + y \leq 2 + 1/2 \cdot \sqrt{2}$... actually the boundary of the triangle consists of two legs and a hypotenuse. The offset of the hypotenuse $x + y = 2$ (inward normal direction $(-1,-1)/\sqrt{2}$, outward $(1,1)/\sqrt{2}$) by $1/2$ inward gives $x + y = 2 - \sqrt{2}/2$; outward gives $x + y = 2 + \sqrt{2}/2$.

The center is within $1/2$ of the black triangle iff it's within distance $1/2$ of the closed triangle region (points inside or on the triangle). This means the center is inside the Minkowski sum: the triangle expanded by $1/2$ in all directions.

For the bottom-left triangle ($x \geq 0, y \geq 0, x+y \leq 2$), the expanded region is:
$$x + y \leq 2 + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \quad x \geq -\frac{1}{2}, \quad y \geq -\frac{1}{2}.$$

Intersected with valid region $x \geq 1/2$, $y \geq 1/2$: the constraints $x \geq -1/2$ and $y \geq -1/2$ are superseded.

So within the valid region, center is near the BL corner triangle iff:
$$x \geq \frac{1}{2},\; y \geq \frac{1}{2},\; x + y \leq 2 + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.$$

Area of this region: it's a right triangle (or trapezoid). With $x, y \geq 1/2$ and $x+y \leq 2 + \sqrt{2}/2$:

Let $u = x - 1/2 \geq 0$, $v = y - 1/2 \geq 0$, $u + v \leq (2 + \sqrt{2}/2 - 1) = 1 + \sqrt{2}/2$.

This is a right isosceles triangle with leg $1 + \sqrt{2}/2$, area $= \dfrac{1}{2}(1 + \sqrt{2}/2)^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(1 + \sqrt{2} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3 + 2\sqrt{2}}{2} = \dfrac{3 + 2\sqrt{2}}{4}$.

By symmetry, each of the 4 corners contributes the same area. Total from corners: $4 \cdot \dfrac{3 + 2\sqrt{2}}{4} = 3 + 2\sqrt{2}$.

We must check that corner regions don't overlap with each other or with the center diamond region — they don't for an $8 \times 8$ square.

**Black center diamond.** The diamond has vertices at $(4,2)$, $(6,4)$, $(4,6)$, $(2,4)$ (center $(4,4)$, side $2\sqrt{2}$, so the diamond has diagonals of length $4$ along the axes). It's the region $|x-4| + |y-4| \leq 2$.

The coin center is within $1/2$ of this diamond iff the center is within distance $1/2$ of the region $|x-4|+|y-4| \leq 2$. This means the center lies in the expanded region $|x-4|+|y-4| \leq 2 + \sqrt{2}/2$ ... 

Wait: the $L^1$ ball (diamond) expanded by a Euclidean distance $r$ gives a region that is a rounded diamond (the diamond plus quarter-circles of radius $r$ at corners, plus rectangles along the sides). For the $\ell^1$-ball $|x-4|+|y-4| \leq 2$ with $r = 1/2$:

The expanded region is the set of points within Euclidean distance $1/2$ of the closed diamond. The diamond's boundary has 4 sides; each side contributes a rectangle of width $1/2$ offset outward, and each of 4 corners contributes a quarter-circle of radius $1/2$.

Area of expanded diamond:
- Original diamond: $\frac{1}{2}d_1 d_2 = \frac{1}{2}(4)(4) = 8$.
- 4 rectangles (each side has length $2\sqrt{2}$, width $1/2$): $4 \cdot 2\sqrt{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 4\sqrt{2}$.
- 4 quarter-circles: together one full circle, $\pi (1/2)^2 = \pi/4$.

Total expanded area: $8 + 4\sqrt{2} + \pi/4$.

This is fully within the valid region (since the diamond center is at $(4,4)$ and the diamond extends to at most $|x-4|+|y-4|=2+1/2$, well inside $[1/2, 15/2]^2$).

**Probability:**

$$P = \frac{\text{total favorable area}}{49} = \frac{(3 + 2\sqrt{2}) + (8 + 4\sqrt{2} + \pi/4)}{49} = \frac{11 + 6\sqrt{2} + \pi/4}{49}.$$

$$= \frac{1}{49} \cdot \frac{44 + 24\sqrt{2} + \pi}{4} = \frac{44 + 24\sqrt{2} + \pi}{196}.$$

So $a = 44$, $b = 24$, and $a + b = 68$.

Answer: **C**.
</Solution>
