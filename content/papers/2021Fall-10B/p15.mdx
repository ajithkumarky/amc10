<Problem>
In square $ABCD$, points $P$ and $Q$ lie on $\overline{AD}$ and $\overline{AB}$, respectively. Segments $\overline{BP}$ and $\overline{CQ}$ intersect at right angles at $R$, with $BR = 6$ and $PR = 7$. What is the area of the square?

(In the figure: $A$ is at bottom-left, $B$ at bottom-right, $C$ at top-right, $D$ at top-left. $P$ is on the left side $AD$ and $Q$ is on the bottom side $AB$. $BP$ and $CQ$ cross at right angles at $R$, with $BR = 6$ and $PR = 7$ labeled along $BP$.)
</Problem>

<Solution>
Let the side length of the square be $s$. Place $A = (0,0)$, $B = (s,0)$, $C = (s,s)$, $D = (0,s)$.

Since $P$ lies on $\overline{AD}$, write $P = (0, p)$ for some $0 \le p \le s$. Since $Q$ lies on $\overline{AB}$, write $Q = (q, 0)$ for some $0 \le q \le s$.

**Key similarity observation.** In square $ABCD$, triangles $ABP$ and $BCQ$ are congruent (right triangles sharing the same hypotenuse structure), so $\angle ABP + \angle CBP = 90°$. Because $\overline{BP} \perp \overline{CQ}$, and $\angle CBQ$ is complementary to $\angle ABP$... let's use the right-angle condition directly.

Direction of $\overrightarrow{BP}$: from $B = (s,0)$ to $P = (0,p)$ is $(-s, p)$.
Direction of $\overrightarrow{CQ}$: from $C = (s,s)$ to $Q = (q,0)$ is $(q-s, -s)$.

Perpendicularity:
$$(-s)(q-s) + (p)(-s) = 0 \implies -s(q-s) - ps = 0 \implies s-q = p \implies p+q = s.$$

So $p + q = s$.

**Using the given lengths $BR = 6$ and $PR = 7$.**

$R$ divides $\overline{BP}$ in ratio $BR : RP = 6 : 7$. Since $|BP| = \sqrt{s^2 + p^2}$ and $BR = 6$, $PR = 7$:
$$BP = BR + RP = 13, \quad \text{so } \sqrt{s^2 + p^2} = 13.$$

**Finding $CQ$ using triangle $BRC$.**

Since $\overline{BP} \perp \overline{CQ}$ at $R$, triangles $BRC$ and $PRsomething$ share angle relationships. Use coordinate intersection.

$R$ lies on $BP$ at parameter $t = \dfrac{BR}{BP} = \dfrac{6}{13}$ from $B$:
$$R = B + \frac{6}{13}(P - B) = \left(s - \frac{6s}{13},\, \frac{6p}{13}\right) = \left(\frac{7s}{13},\, \frac{6p}{13}\right).$$

$R$ also lies on $CQ$. Direction from $C = (s,s)$ to $Q = (q,0) = (s-p, 0)$ is $(s-p-s, -s) = (-p, -s)$.

Parameterize $CQ$: $(s,s) + u(-p,-s) = (s - up,\, s - us)$.

Set equal to $R$:
$$s - up = \frac{7s}{13} \implies up = s - \frac{7s}{13} = \frac{6s}{13} \implies u = \frac{6s}{13p}.$$
$$s - us = \frac{6p}{13} \implies us = s - \frac{6p}{13} = \frac{13s - 6p}{13} \implies u = \frac{13s - 6p}{13s}.$$

Setting both expressions for $u$ equal:
$$\frac{6s}{13p} = \frac{13s-6p}{13s} \implies \frac{6s}{p} = \frac{13s-6p}{s} \implies 6s^2 = p(13s - 6p).$$

We also have $s^2 + p^2 = 169$ (from $BP = 13$) and $q = s - p$.

From $6s^2 = 13ps - 6p^2$:
$$6(s^2 + p^2) = 13ps \implies 6 \cdot 169 = 13ps \implies ps = \frac{6 \cdot 169}{13} = 78.$$

So $ps = 78$ and $s^2 + p^2 = 169$.

$$s^2 + p^2 = (s+p)^2 - 2sp = 169 \implies (s+p)^2 = 169 + 2 \cdot 78 = 169 + 156 = 325.$$

We need $s^2$. From $ps = 78$, we get $p = 78/s$, and:
$$s^2 + \frac{78^2}{s^2} = 169 \implies s^4 - 169s^2 + 78^2 = 0.$$

$$s^2 = \frac{169 \pm \sqrt{169^2 - 4 \cdot 6084}}{2} = \frac{169 \pm \sqrt{28561 - 24336}}{2} = \frac{169 \pm \sqrt{4225}}{2} = \frac{169 \pm 65}{2}.$$

So $s^2 = \dfrac{234}{2} = 117$ or $s^2 = \dfrac{104}{2} = 52$.

Check $s^2 = 52$: then $p = 78/s$ and $p^2 = 78^2/52 = 117$, so $p > s$ — impossible since $P$ is on $AD$ with $p \le s$.

Check $s^2 = 117$: then $p^2 = 169 - 117 = 52$, $p = \sqrt{52} < s = \sqrt{117}$ ✓.

The area of the square is $s^2 = \boxed{117}$.

Answer: **D**.
</Solution>
