<Problem>
A cube is constructed from $4$ white unit cubes and $4$ blue unit cubes. How many different ways are there to construct the $2 \times 2 \times 2$ cube using these smaller cubes? (Two constructions are considered the same if one can be rotated to match the other.)
</Problem>

<Solution>
We apply Burnside's lemma with the rotation group of the cube ($|G|=24$) acting on the 8 corner positions. We count, for each rotation, the number of colorings (4 white, 4 blue) left fixed by that rotation, then divide by 24.

The 24 rotations fall into 5 conjugacy classes:

**Identity (1 rotation).** All $\binom{8}{4}=70$ colorings are fixed. Contribution: $70$.

**Face rotations by $90°$/$270°$ (6 rotations).** Each cycles the 4 top positions and 4 bottom positions in two separate 4-cycles. A fixed coloring requires each 4-cycle to be monochromatic; the only 4W+4B assignments have one cycle all-white and the other all-blue: 2 colorings fixed each. Contribution: $6\times 2=12$.

**Face rotations by $180°$ (3 rotations).** Each pairs the 8 positions into 4 transpositions. A fixed coloring assigns the same color to each paired positions; choosing which 2 of the 4 pairs are white gives $\binom{4}{2}=6$ fixed colorings each. Contribution: $3\times 6=18$.

**Vertex rotations by $120°$/$240°$ (8 rotations).** A $120°$ rotation around a main diagonal fixes the 2 corner positions on that diagonal and cycles the remaining 6 positions as two 3-cycles. For a fixed coloring each 3-cycle must be monochromatic; with 4W+4B, the two fixed positions and two 3-cycles (of size 1,1,3,3) must total 4 white. The only distributions are: (fixed$_1$=W, fixed$_2$=B, 3-cycle A=W, 3-cycle B=B) and (W$\leftrightarrow$B), giving $2\times 2=4$ colorings fixed each. Contribution: $8\times 4=32$.

**Edge rotations by $180°$ (6 rotations).** Each pairs the 8 positions into 4 transpositions. Same as face $180°$: $\binom{4}{2}=6$ fixed colorings each. Contribution: $6\times 6=36$.

**Burnside count:**
$$\frac{70+12+18+32+36}{24}=\frac{168}{24}=7.$$

Answer: **A**.
</Solution>
