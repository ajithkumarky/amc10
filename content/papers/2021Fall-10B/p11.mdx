<Problem>
A regular hexagon of side length $1$ is inscribed in a circle. Each minor arc of the circle determined by a side of the hexagon is reflected over that side. What is the area of the region bounded by these $6$ reflected arcs?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Since the hexagon has side length $1$ and is inscribed in a circle, the circle has radius $r = 1$.

**Setting up the area calculation.**
The regular hexagon has area $\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Each side of the hexagon is a chord of the circle. The corresponding minor arc (which lies outside the hexagon) subtends a central angle of $60°$ at the center. When we reflect this arc over its chord, the arc flips to the inside of the hexagon, creating an inward-curving arc.

The region bounded by the 6 reflected arcs is the hexagon minus the 6 circular segments cut off by those reflected arcs.

**Area of one circular segment.**
Each circular segment corresponds to a $60°$ sector minus an equilateral triangle (the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord, which is equilateral since $r = 1 =$ chord length).

$$\text{Sector area} = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta = \frac{1}{2}(1)^2\cdot\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}{6}.$$

$$\text{Triangle area} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(1)^2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}.$$

$$\text{Segment area} = \frac{\pi}{6} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}.$$

**Total region area.**
$$\text{Region} = \text{Hexagon} - 6 \times \text{Segment} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} - 6\!\left(\frac{\pi}{6}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right) = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} - \pi + \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} = 3\sqrt{3} - \pi.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
