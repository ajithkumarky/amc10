<Problem>
For each integer $n \geq 2$, let $S_n$ be the sum of all products $jk$, where $j$ and $k$ are integers and $1 \leq j < k \leq n$. What is the sum of the 10 least values of $n$ such that $S_n$ is divisible by $3$?
</Problem>

<Solution>
**Finding a formula for $S_n$.**

$$S_n = \sum_{1 \le j < k \le n} jk.$$

Use the identity:
$$\left(\sum_{i=1}^n i\right)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n i^2 + 2\sum_{j < k} jk,$$

so
$$S_n = \frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2 - \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\right].$$

$$S_n = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{6}\left[\frac{3n(n+1)}{2} - (2n+1)\right] = \frac{n(n+1)}{12}\left[\frac{3n^2+3n-4n-2}{2}\right]$$

Wait, let's simplify cleanly:

$$S_n = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{n^2(n+1)^2}{4} - \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\right] = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}\left[\frac{n(n+1)}{4} - \frac{2n+1}{6}\right]$$

$$= \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \cdot \frac{3n(n+1) - 2(2n+1)}{12} = \frac{n(n+1)(3n^2+3n-4n-2)}{24} = \frac{n(n+1)(3n^2-n-2)}{24}.$$

Factor $3n^2 - n - 2 = (3n+2)(n-1)$:

$$S_n = \frac{n(n+1)(n-1)(3n+2)}{24} = \frac{(n-1)n(n+1)(3n+2)}{24}.$$

**Condition $3 \mid S_n$.**

We need $3 \mid \dfrac{(n-1)n(n+1)(3n+2)}{24}$.

Since $3 \mid (3n+2) \iff 3 \mid 2$, which is false. So $\gcd(3, 3n+2) = 1$.

The factor $24 = 8 \times 3$. So $3 \mid S_n \iff 3^2 \mid (n-1)n(n+1)$ or $3 \mid (n-1)n(n+1)$... let me be precise.

$S_n = \dfrac{(n-1)n(n+1)(3n+2)}{24}$. For $S_n$ to be an integer and divisible by 3:

$3n+2 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, so $\gcd(3, 3n+2) = 1$.

$24 = 2^3 \cdot 3$.

The product $(n-1)n(n+1)$ is the product of 3 consecutive integers, so $3 \mid (n-1)n(n+1)$. Write $v_3(k)$ for the 3-adic valuation of $k$.

$v_3(S_n) = v_3((n-1)n(n+1)) + v_3(3n+2) - v_3(24) = v_3((n-1)n(n+1)) + 0 - 1$.

For $3 \mid S_n$, we need $v_3(S_n) \ge 1$, i.e., $v_3((n-1)n(n+1)) \ge 2$.

$(n-1)n(n+1)$ always has $v_3 \ge 1$ (three consecutive integers). We need $v_3 \ge 2$.

$v_3((n-1)n(n+1)) \ge 2$ iff at least one of $n-1, n, n+1$ is divisible by $9$, OR at least two of them are divisible by 3.

But since they are consecutive, at most one is divisible by 3. Wait — among three consecutive integers, exactly one is divisible by 3. So $v_3((n-1)n(n+1)) = v_3(\text{that one element})$.

We need $v_3(\text{that one element}) \ge 2$, i.e., the multiple of 3 among $\{n-1, n, n+1\}$ must be divisible by $9$.

So the condition is: $9 \mid (n-1)$, $9 \mid n$, or $9 \mid (n+1)$.

Equivalently: $n \equiv 1 \pmod{9}$, $n \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$, or $n \equiv 8 \pmod{9}$.

Combined: $n \equiv 0, 1, 8 \pmod{9}$.

**Finding the 10 least values of $n \ge 2$ satisfying this.**

$n \equiv 0 \pmod 9$: $9, 18, 27, \ldots$
$n \equiv 1 \pmod 9$: $10, 19, 28, \ldots$ (since $1 < 2$, start from $10$)
$n \equiv 8 \pmod 9$: $8, 17, 26, \ldots$

Wait: $n \equiv 1 \pmod 9$ gives $n = 1, 10, 19, \ldots$ but $n \ge 2$, so start from $10$.

Sorted: $8, 9, 10, 17, 18, 19, 26, 27, 28, 35$.

Sum $= (8+9+10) + (17+18+19) + (26+27+28) + 35$
$= 27 + 54 + 81 + 35 = 197$.

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
