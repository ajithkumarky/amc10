<Problem>
Regular polygons with $5$, $6$, $7$, and $8$ sides are inscribed in the same circle. No two of the polygons share a vertex, and no three of their sides intersect at a common point. At how many points inside the circle do two of their sides intersect?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Two chords intersect inside the circle if and only if their four endpoints alternate around the circle. For a regular $m$-gon and a regular $n$-gon in generic position (no shared vertices, no three sides concurrent), we count crossing pairs by fixing a side $s$ of the $m$-gon: it subtends an arc of $360°/m$, and a side of the $n$-gon crosses $s$ if and only if exactly one of its two endpoints lies in that arc. Since the $n$-gon's vertices are evenly spaced, those falling inside the arc form one consecutive block; if the block is nonempty and not all $n$ vertices, exactly 2 sides of the $n$-gon straddle the boundary of the arc and cross $s$.

**Checking when every arc of the $m$-gon contains at least one $n$-gon vertex.** The $m$ arcs of the $m$-gon partition the circle, so they contain a total of $n$ vertices of the $n$-gon. If every arc contains at least one vertex (i.e., $n \geq m$), each side of the $m$-gon is crossed by exactly 2 sides of the $n$-gon, giving $2m$ intersections. If some arc contains 0 vertices, those sides of the $m$-gon contribute 0; by a symmetric count from the $n$-gon's perspective, the total is still $2\min(m,n)$.

For each pair of polygons from $\{5,6,7,8\}$ with $m \le n$: in each $m$-gon arc of $360°/m$ degrees, the number of $n$-gon vertices is $n/m$ on average; since $n \geq m$ for all pairs below, every arc receives at least $\lfloor n/m \rfloor \geq 1$ vertex (verified case by case: $5|6$: avg $1.2$; $5|7$: avg $1.4$; $5|8$: avg $1.6$; $6|7$: avg $1.17$; $6|8$: avg $1.33$; $7|8$: avg $1.14$ — all $\geq 1$). Thus every side of the smaller polygon is crossed exactly twice.

**Counting intersections per pair:**

| Pair $(m,n)$ | Intersections $= 2m$ |
|---|---|
| $(5,6)$ | $10$ |
| $(5,7)$ | $10$ |
| $(5,8)$ | $10$ |
| $(6,7)$ | $12$ |
| $(6,8)$ | $12$ |
| $(7,8)$ | $14$ |

**Total:** $10 + 10 + 10 + 12 + 12 + 14 = \boxed{68}$.

Answer: **E**.
</Solution>
