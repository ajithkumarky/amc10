<Problem>
Regular polygons with $5$, $6$, $7$, and $8$ sides are inscribed in the same circle. No two of the polygons share a vertex, and no three of their sides intersect at a common point. At how many points inside the circle do two of their sides intersect?
</Problem>

<Solution>
Two sides (one from polygon $A$ and one from polygon $B$) intersect inside the circle if and only if their four endpoints alternate around the circle — one pair of endpoints from each polygon interlaces with the other pair.

**Counting intersections between two specific polygons.**

For a regular $m$-gon and a regular $n$-gon inscribed in the same circle (no shared vertices, no three sides concurrent), the number of interior intersection points between their sides equals the number of pairs of sides, one from each polygon, whose endpoints alternate around the circle.

A side of the $m$-gon subtends an arc of $\dfrac{360°}{m}$; similarly for the $n$-gon. Two chords (one from each polygon) intersect inside the circle if and only if their endpoints alternate. 

For each side $s_i$ of the $m$-gon, the number of sides of the $n$-gon that cross it equals the number of sides of the $n$-gon that have one endpoint in each arc that $s_i$ divides the circle into. This count depends on how the vertices of the $n$-gon are distributed.

**General formula.** For two regular polygons with $m$ and $n$ sides (no shared vertices, generic position), the total number of interior intersections is $2mn$ divided by $m+n$... actually the exact count is $mn$ when all chords cross, but in general it's less.

The correct count uses the following: two chords $A_1A_2$ (from the $m$-gon) and $B_1B_2$ (from the $n$-gon) intersect inside the circle iff the four points alternate on the circle. Given the polygons are in "general position" (no shared vertices, no three sides concurrent), the number of crossings between the $m$-gon and $n$-gon equals

$$2mn \cdot \frac{\text{(probability a random side of each cross)}}{1}.$$

More concretely: fix a side $s$ of the $m$-gon. It divides the circle into two arcs; call them of lengths $\ell$ and $m\cdot\text{arc} - \ell$ in terms of $n$-gon vertices. The number of sides of the $n$-gon crossing $s$ equals the number of $n$-gon sides with one endpoint in each arc.

By the general formula for two regular polygons in generic position on a circle:

**Number of intersections between the $m$-gon and $n$-gon $= 2mn \cdot P(\text{cross})$.**

**Direct approach.** A side of the $m$-gon is a chord spanning $1/m$ of the circle. A side of the $n$-gon spans $1/n$ of the circle. They intersect inside the circle iff their arcs "interleave."
