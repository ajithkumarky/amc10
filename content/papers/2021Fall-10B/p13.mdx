<Problem>
A square with side length $3$ is inscribed in an isosceles triangle with one side of the square along the base of the triangle. A square with side length $2$ has two vertices on the other square and the other two on sides of the triangle, as shown. What is the area of the triangle?

The figure shows an isosceles triangle with a $3 \times 3$ square resting on its base and a $2 \times 2$ square centered on top of the first square, with its top two corners touching the legs of the triangle.
</Problem>

<Solution>
Set up coordinates with the base on the $x$-axis and the apex directly above the midpoint of the base. By symmetry, place the apex at $(0, h)$ and the base from $(-b, 0)$ to $(b, 0)$.

**Condition from the big square (side 3).**
The square of side 3 is centered on the base. Its top edge lies at height $y = 3$, stretching from $x = -\tfrac{3}{2}$ to $x = \tfrac{3}{2}$. The corners at $(±\tfrac{3}{2}, 3)$ lie on the legs of the triangle.

The width of the triangle at height $y$ is $2b\!\left(1 - \dfrac{y}{h}\right)$, so the half-width at $y = 3$ is $\dfrac{b(h-3)}{h}$. Setting this equal to $\tfrac{3}{2}$:

$$\frac{b(h-3)}{h} = \frac{3}{2}. \tag{1}$$

**Condition from the small square (side 2).**
The square of side 2 is centered on top of the big square, so its top edge lies at $y = 5$, stretching from $x = -1$ to $x = 1$. The corners at $(±1, 5)$ lie on the legs. The half-width at $y = 5$ is $\dfrac{b(h-5)}{h}$:

$$\frac{b(h-5)}{h} = 1. \tag{2}$$

**Solving the system.**
Divide equation (1) by equation (2):

$$\frac{h-3}{h-5} = \frac{3}{2}.$$

$$2(h-3) = 3(h-5) \implies 2h - 6 = 3h - 15 \implies h = 9.$$

Substituting back into (2): $\dfrac{b(9-5)}{9} = 1 \Rightarrow b = \dfrac{9}{4}$.

**Area of the triangle.**
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 2b \times h = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{9}{2} \times 9 = \frac{81}{4} = 20\frac{1}{4}.$$

Answer: **B**.
</Solution>
